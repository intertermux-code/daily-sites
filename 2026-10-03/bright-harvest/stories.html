§ 00 — THE VOICES
Six stories. More on the way.

◆ Voices of the line

Everyone in
the line has a
whole story.
We asked.

We don’t publish statistics without the humans attached. Here are six of them — neighbors who’ve used Bright Harvest, volunteers who never left, and the farmers who keep the trucks full.

§ 01

The stories
themselves.

Story 01 · Elena, 38

“The pantry kept my girls in school.”

Elena’s husband was laid off in November 2025. They had one car, two daughters in middle school, and a rent check due on the first. “I was going to pull them out of the school they loved and move into my mother’s spare room,” she says. “A friend told me about Bright Harvest. I went on a Tuesday. I came out with a week of groceries, a jar of peanut butter my youngest actually likes, and my pride somehow intact.”

Elena has shopped at the River Ward pantry every week since. In April, she started volunteering on the welcome desk. “I wanted to be the person who smiled at the next woman the way that first volunteer smiled at me.”

Filed under: Pantry · River Ward · 2025

Story 02 · Marcus, 68

“I came for an hour. I stayed six years.”

Marcus taught American history at East Hollow High for thirty-one years. When he retired, he says, “I had a lot of silence in my day and not much to do with it.” A flyer at the library asked for garden volunteers. He signed up for one hour a week.

Six years later, Marcus has his own forty-by-four-foot plot in the teaching garden, a friend he meets every Saturday morning at six, and a reputation for growing the best garlic in the valley. “I tell my old students,” he says, “that I teach now, too. Just tomatoes.”

Filed under: Garden · Apprentice · Long-term

Story 03 · Priya, 24

“I learned to grow dinner in a borrowed plot.”

Priya came to Bright Harvest the winter after her father died. “I didn’t know what to do with my hands,” she says. She enrolled in the apprentice program on a friend’s recommendation.

Eighteen months later, Priya runs a quarter-acre market garden of her own two towns over. She sells to local restaurants and brings her three-year-old daughter to the teaching garden every Wednesday. “She pulls carrots with me,” Priya says. “She thinks they are magic.”

Filed under: Garden · Apprentice · 2024 cohort

Story 04 · Tom, 54

“The ugly apples still taste like apples.”

Tom runs Orchard Hill, a fourth-generation farm ten miles from East Hollow. Every September, his crew sorts out the apples too small, too scarred, or too oddly shaped for the supermarket. “Used to go to the pigs, or to the compost,” he says.

For the last nine years, those apples have gone to Bright Harvest. Last fall, 22,000 pounds of them. “A kid at the River Ward pantry told me my apples were her favorite thing all week,” Tom says. “I’ve got a drawing of it on my fridge.”

Filed under: Partner · Orchard Hill Farms

Story 05 · Amara, 42

“The kitchen taught me to cook for 340.”

Amara started as a volunteer on the kitchen line in 2019. She was working nights at a diner and wanted to learn something beyond fry cook. “The chef at the time, Rosa, let me shadow her,” Amara says. “She taught me how to scale a recipe from four to four hundred.”

Amara is now the paid head chef of the Community Kitchen. She serves 340 plates a night, five nights a week. “I get to cook my grandmother’s recipes,” she says. “For people who haven’t had a home-cooked meal in a long time.”

Filed under: Kitchen · Staff · From volunteer

Story 06 · Ravi, 61

“I’ve driven this truck 48,000 miles this year.”

Ravi retired from a trucking company in 2023. He signed up to drive a Bright Harvest mobile market truck the following spring. “I like the roads,” he says. “And I like the people at the end of them.”

Ravi drives the Friday route — eleven towns, twenty-two stops, every weekend. At a stop in Millbury, an elderly couple brings him coffee every Saturday. “They call me their grandson,” he says. “I don’t correct them.”

Filed under: Mobile Market · Driver · Volunteer
