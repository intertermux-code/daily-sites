Masthead · The publication

About Signal.

Signal is a reader-funded tech publication. We publish longform reporting on the machines, systems, and people shaping the world — the kind of work that takes weeks, costs real money, and doesn't fit inside a sponsored post. We have no ads, no VC, and no sponsored content. We have never published a piece that a paying advertiser asked us to.

Our mission and principles

Principle · 01

The work comes first.

Every story we publish is reported. Every quote is attributed. Every claim is sourced. We do not publish hot takes, aggregated listicles, or press releases rewritten in the active voice. If a piece is not worth a week of reporting, it is not worth publishing.

Principle · 02

The reader is the client.

We are funded by the people who read us. 41,208 of them, as of this writing. They pay between $6 and $60 a month. None of them has ever asked us to kill a story. None of them has ever asked us to write one.

Principle · 03

We cover the infrastructure, not the keynote.

We do not write about product launches. We write about the supply chains the product sits inside, the maintainers the product depends on, the workers the product displaces, and the legal frameworks the product is quietly rewriting.

Principle · 04

We are slow, on purpose.

We publish roughly three pieces a week. Each one is fact-checked, copy-edited, and reviewed by at least two editors. We do not chase the news cycle. We try to produce something you will still want to read in five years.

Standards · Editorial code

What we will and will not do.

We will

  • Publish corrections prominently and permanently.
  • Pay our freelancers on acceptance, not on publication.
  • Disclose every financial interest, including our own.
  • Protect sources, including from ourselves — no editor can compel a reporter to reveal one.
  • Respond to reader complaints within three business days.

We will not

  • Accept advertising, sponsored posts, or paid placements.
  • Allow a funder to see a piece before publication.
  • Publish a piece because it would be good for our metrics.
  • Pursue a story because a competitor has.
  • Write a headline that the story does not support.

The full code of ethics is available here and is reviewed annually by an outside counsel. The most recent revision was in January 2026.
