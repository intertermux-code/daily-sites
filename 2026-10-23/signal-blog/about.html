A Newsroom Run Like a Ledger

Nine reporters, six desks, one accountant, and a published budget. Here is who we are, what we owe our independence to, and exactly where the money comes from.

Studiestræde 14 · Copenhagen · third floor, no lift

Why Signal Exists

Technology reporting had drifted into product reporting. Launches were covered, contracts were not. Keynotes got column inches; the water licence that decided whether the keynote happened got none. We started in 2017 with a stubborn proposition: the interesting thing about a system is who is obliged to whom.

So we report like a registry. Every story is filed under a desk with a named editor. Every claim carries a document, a dataset, or a person willing to be quoted. Every correction gets its own line in the ledger and stays there. We do not run advertising, we do not take sponsored slots, and we do not accept travel from anyone we cover — a rule that has cost us roughly 40 reporting trips and one very good hotel buffet in Barcelona.

Signal is owned by the cooperative that runs it. Our readers are our only funders, and they can see the accounts. That is not a boast; it is the only structure in which “independent” means anything you could check.

Standing Orders

Five rules, unchanged since issue one, applied to ourselves first.

  1. SO 1

    Every claim has an artefact

    A document, a dataset, a specification, or a named person on the record. If we cannot show you the artefact, we do not print the claim — and when a source needs protection, we publish the method instead of the name.

  2. SO 2

    Corrections are permanent and public

    We keep the wrong version of a sentence on the page with a dated note. 88 corrections and four retractions, all listed in the archive. Nobody at Signal gets to quietly improve the past.

  3. SO 3

    No vendor money, no vendor travel

    No advertising, no sponsored slots, no paid attendance at launch events, no review units we keep. We bought the four hardware wallets in Duarte’s investigation at retail, and we published the receipts.

  4. SO 4

    Explain the numbers, then the story

    Methodology boxes run above the fold on data stories, and our scripts go into the open repository the same day. If you cannot reproduce our chart from our data, we have not finished reporting.

  5. SO 5

    Sources are told what we are writing

    Before publication, subjects of an investigation get a factual summary and a deadline. They do not get to approve text. Twenty-two of the twenty-seven maintainers in the current long read checked their own quoted figures.

Ten Years, in the Order It Happened

Including the two years we nearly stopped.

2017

Issue one, 400 subscribers

Brandt and Demir self-published the first Signal issue from a borrowed desk in Copenhagen: 18 pages on the DNS certificate mess, printed, and mailed to anyone who sent a stamp. Eleven of those 400 are still subscribed.

2019

The year we almost closed

Reader cancellations after a pandemic-adjacent revenue dip took us to four months of runway. We sold the printing contract, went digital-only, and took our first — and only — institutional grant, from a foundation with no view on our coverage. Full disclosure in the 2019 accounts.

2021

Chicago desk opens

Rebecca Mendes started filing from county procurement meetings, which turned out to be the story a lot of broadband coverage was missing. Our US readership passed Denmark’s for the first time.

2023

The cooperative buys itself

Readers converted Signal Media ApS into a worker-and-reader cooperative. Two venture offers declined, both on the record in the accounts, because the terms would have required a seat that could stop a story.

2026

612 stories, nine reporters

128,400 weekly readers, 61% newsletter open rate, and a published operating budget that shows a €38,000 surplus. We are, unglamorously, solvent — which is the whole point of the structure.
