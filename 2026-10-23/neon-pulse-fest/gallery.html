Seven editions

The same spine, every year

From one borrowed mixer in a print shop to a freight terminal with five tuned rooms. Follow the line.

2019 · Edition 01

Print Shop, 90 Capacity

Nine hours, four DJs, a sound system rented on a card that maxed out mid-set. Nobody left before 04:00 because the door lock jammed — the origin of our “no early close” habit.

  • 90 people
  • 1 room
  • 4 artists
’19

2021 · Edition 03

The Empty Year

Streamed from a locked warehouse with no audience at all. Fourteen thousand households watched the 06:00 sunrise set, and the rooftop became the festival’s signature because of it.

  • 0 in room
  • 14k online
  • Sunrise invented
’21

2022 · Edition 04

Dock Street, First Night

The terminal opened with one stage and a leaking roof. Two thousand people, one generator failure at 01:20, and a crowd that kept singing until the backup came up.

  • 2,000 people
  • 1 stage
  • 22 min blackout
’22

2024 · Edition 06

Five Rooms, One Clock

The Basement and Dome opened together and the whole weekend moved onto a single shared clock, so stage changes stopped overlapping in the worst way.

  • 5 stages
  • 7,400 people
  • 44 artists
’24

2025 · Edition 07

Loudest Year, Quietest Room

Peak occupancy of 9,140, and the first year the quiet room got used more than the bar queue. Both are in these frames, and we are proud of the second one.

Photographers

Every frame on this page was shot by Ana Reyes (warehouse and basement), Joris Peeters (main and dome) or Mina Okafor (rooftop and the morning after). Prints are available at the Dock Street desk each night, $35, and the sale covers next year’s film budget.

Using these images

Press may download the archive pack with credit. Commercial use is not licensed — if a brand wants a frame from a crowd that paid $289 to be there, the answer is no, and we will say so publicly.
