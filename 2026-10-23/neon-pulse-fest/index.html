Transmission 01

A festival wired like a terminal

Neon Pulse started in 2019 as a warehouse night with a borrowed mixer and a spreadsheet for a ticketing system. Seven editions later it still runs on that spreadsheet’s logic: everyone gets the same sound pressure, every set gets announced the second it is confirmed, and nothing is sold as a secret.

This December the freight terminal on Dock Street becomes a single machine. Five stages share one clock, one lighting bus and one sound system tuned by the same three engineers who mixed it last year — so a low end that hits you in the CRT Warehouse is the same low end you feel on the Rooftop at 06:00.

You will not find a QR-code maze or a sponsor activation masquerading as an artist. You will find 62 acts, four kitchens, twelve free water fountains and a curfew we negotiate with the neighbours every September.

The three nights

Follow the spine from gates to sunrise

Each entry is a fixed point on the weekend clock. Times are local, sets are locked, and the last train back downtown leaves at 02:40 — the shuttle runs until 07:15.

Gates & Cold Open

Doors throw at 18:00 sharp. The freight hall runs ambient and dub until midnight so the room fills slowly and nobody misses the open-to-close arc.

Five rooms

Pick a room, stay in it

Every stage has its own tuning, its own crowd contract, and its own water point. Capacity caps are enforced at the door, not at the bar.
