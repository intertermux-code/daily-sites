01 — Ledger

Transactions sorted before you look at them

Mintwise has learned from 1.9 billion anonymised transactions, so a charge from “TJ’s #418” is groceries and “DMV-Vehicle Reg” is a government fee — with no rule written by you. When it guesses wrong, correcting it once creates a permanent rule scoped to the merchant, not the amount.

Splits handle the messy middle: a warehouse run that was half groceries and half garden, or a hotel bill that covers business and pleasure. Tag the split once and next quarter’s trip inherits it.

  • 46 built-in categories, 12 custom slots
  • Merchant rules with wildcards and priority order
  • Recurring detection names subscriptions and renewal dates
  • Per-account defaults for shared household cards
  • Refund matching so reimbursements do not look like income
  • Bulk re-categorise by merchant or date range

02 — Foresight

A 60-day forecast that admits uncertainty

Most balance projections are one confident line. Ours is a band: the date every connected bill is due, the payday cadence you set, your rolling ten-day spend average, and the irregular costs already scheduled. You see where you are likely to land and how wide the margin is.

Anomaly alerts watch for the four things that quietly wreck a plan: a subscription that raised its price, a duplicate charge, a card used in an unfamiliar place, and a category running ahead of pace for the third day in a row.

  • Low / expected / high band on every future date
  • Overdraft risk flagged 5 days ahead, never in a huff
  • Price-rise detection comparing last month to this month
  • One digest notification per day, configurable by channel

03 — Smoothing

Annual bills, turned into twelve small ones

Car insurance, the tax bill, a wedding in Lisbon, school uniforms. Mintwise finds recurring irregular charges in your history, asks you to confirm the ones it is unsure about, then sets aside the exact monthly slice into a named sinking fund. The money leaves on payday, so the due date is a non-event.

If the real charge lands higher than the fund, the shortfall is spread across the remaining months of the year rather than dumped on your next payday.

  • Automatic detection of annual and semi-annual charges
  • Sinking funds with their own balance and due date
  • Audit trail showing every dollar set aside and when it was used
  • Shared visibility with a partner’s accounts

04 — Automation

Payday autoflow moves money while the balance is small

Set the number you want kept and the buffer you need to live on. The morning your payroll clears, Mintwise divides what is left across savings, debt and investing in the order you choose. Nothing is spent first, which is the entire trick.

Round-ups sweep the cents from each card authorization into the goal you pick. Miss a target in a heavy month and the shortfall is made up gently over the next three cycles, so one bad month never breaks the habit.

  • Payday, weekly or custom cadence
  • Priority order: ring-fenced bills, then savings, then investing
  • Automatic catch-up across up to six months
  • Instant pause with a single hardware-backed confirmation
  • Speed bumps: any increase over 20% waits 24 hours

05 — Goals

Portfolios with a glide path, not a jar

Each goal gets its own allocation and its own date. A deposit in eighteen months sits in cash ladders and short treasuries; a retirement thirty years out sits in equities. Mintwise rebalances the mix automatically as the deadline approaches, and tells you why each change happened.

The warning that matters most is the six-month one: if the projection says the goal will miss its date, you hear about it the month the drift starts, not the month before you need the money.

  • Unlimited goals with shared contributors
  • Cash ladder suggestions in 1, 3 and 6-month buckets
  • Rate comparison across 94 tracked high-yield accounts
  • Automatic de-risking as the target date nears

07 — Underneath

A security model built to be refused

Read-only is a real constraint, not a marketing line: the tokens Mintwise holds cannot initiate an outbound transfer to an account we control. Money movement happens only through flows you created, to destinations you verified once with a micro-deposit.

Your bank credentials never reach our servers — Plaid holds them and hands us a scoped token. Data is encrypted with AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit, keys rotate every 90 days, and access is logged with a two-person rule for anything that touches raw ledgers.

  • SOC 2 Type II, audited annually by Prescott & Vane
  • PCI DSS Level 1 for card metadata
  • GDPR and CCPA compliant, EU data residency on request
  • Hardware-key and passkey second factors supported
  • Bug bounty with 633 researchers, 41 disclosed fixes published
  • Complete deletion within 30 days, verified by certificate
