Record 001 · Squat, bench, deadlift

Powerlifting

A four-week undulating wave repeated four times, with the load re-cut by your coach every time an attempt is missed. Volume lands in weeks 1–3 of each wave and peaks out in week 4, when you take a top single the coach has already predicted within 5 kg.

Every competitor and every curious beginner trains the same competition lifts to the same commands: squat to parallel on a sit-down whistle, bench with a controlled touch and a still pause, deadlift to a head-up lockout. Judging is rehearsed weekly so meet day is not the first time you have heard “down”.

Record 002 · Intervals, sleds, ergometers

Engine / HIIT

Twenty-eight minutes of work measured against your own baseline, never against the person on the next machine. Your split targets come from a 200 m row test and a maximal heart-rate assessment taken in week one and re-tested in week eight.

Intervals run on a fixed grid: forty seconds on, twenty off for the neural blocks; four-minute efforts at threshold for the aerobic-power blocks. Sled pushes and carries feature heavily because they load the legs without the impact that ends a conditioning block early.

Record 003 · Loaded range of motion

Mobility

Range you cannot load is range you will lose under a bar. This is not a stretch club: work is performed with kettlebells, bands and your own bodyweight taken slowly into the end position, so the new range arrives with strength attached to it.

Every block opens with a five-point screen — ankle dorsiflexion, hip flexion, thoracic rotation, overhead depth, shoulder external rotation — measured in centimetres and degrees, written into your file, and re-run at week eight so you can see exactly what changed.

Record 004 · New to the barbell

On-Ramp

Six coached sessions across three weeks for anyone who has never loaded a barbell, or who has trained somewhere else for years without ever being taught to brace. Intakes open every Monday at 18:00 and are capped at eight lifters.

You learn five patterns in a fixed order — hinge, brace, squat, press, carry — each one rehearsed with a dowel, then an empty bar, then a load that stays comfortably inside your technique. Week three ends with a written starting weight for all five lifts.

Record 005 · Peaking for the platform

Comp Prep

Twelve weeks that end at a federation meet, with a second coach calling attempts from the rail. Openers are chosen to guarantee nine white lights; second and third attempts are decided in advance, on paper, before you leave for the hall.

The block includes two rehearsed mock meets at the club — command cadence, rack height, singlet, weigh-in procedure, and the attempt clock set to 60 seconds — so the real thing is a repetition rather than a first.

Method

Inside a
Forge block.

The block is the unit of training here — not the workout. You are never handed a vague four-week plan and left to interpret it.

Week 0 is measurement. Four numbers, taken fresh, written in your file with the date and the coach's initials.

Weeks 1–3 accumulate. Sets are prescribed in percentages of your tested number, so two lifters on the same program train at visibly different loads.

Week 4 is expression. A top single, a re-test, or a mock meet — then the coach signs the wave off or re-cuts it. Nobody advances on a handshake.

[ Meet who signs it off ]

Rule 01

Two missed sessions, block repeats.

Not a punishment. A wave built on stale numbers predicts the wrong load, and the wrong load is how a niggle becomes an injury.

Rule 02

Bracing before load.

No bar touches your back in On-Ramp until the brace pattern holds for three breaths against a coach's hand on your ribs.

Rule 03

Video is part of the session.

Every platform has a fixed camera. Your coach replays the set before you load the next one — and sends the clip to your file.
