Dana Kovac

Head coach, powerlifting · co-founder

Dana built the four-wave structure the whole club trains on after eight years of coaching under a system she did not trust. She has taken eleven lifters from a first meet to a national platform, and she still calls attempts from the rail herself.

Expect a coach who measures before she prescribes and who will tell you, in plain terms, when your technique has degraded enough that the load should come off. Her sessions are quiet, dense and run to the clock.

Marcus Ellery

Head coach, conditioning

Six seasons as a collegiate middle-distance runner before he discovered that intervals written for other people were more interesting than intervals written for himself. Marcus built the 28-minute format that now runs six times a week at Forge.

His sessions are the loudest in the building — he reads splits aloud, writes your target on the board before you start, and holds the room to it. Everyone who has trained with him can quote the same line: “Pace is a decision, not a feeling.”

Priya Raman

Lead mobility coach · physiotherapist

Priya runs the five-point screen every member gets on day one, and she keeps the numbers that prove whether the club's 9 % injury rate is real. Twelve years of outpatient physio first, then a decade of loaded range work with lifters who had been told to stop squatting.

She measures in centimetres and degrees, writes the change in your file, and refuses to accept “it feels better” as evidence. If you have a bar path you cannot explain, she is the coach who finds it.

Theo Brand

Technical coach, squat & bench

Theo spent seven years as a club lifter before he started writing programs, and he still teaches the squat the same way he was taught it: bottom position first, barbell last. He runs the Saturday competition-command block, where every rep is judged to the whistle.

He is the coach members ask for when a lift has stalled for months. His fix is usually one cue and a change of rack height rather than a new program, and he keeps a wall of before-and-after clips to prove it.

Josue Fenn

Head coach, On-Ramp & intake

Joined Forge as a member who had never touched a barbell, qualified as a coach three years later, and now teaches the intake. He remembers precisely what it feels like to be nervous at an empty bar, which is why On-Ramp starts with a dowel and a brace rather than a load.

He runs every Monday intake, caps it at eight, and writes each lifter's five starting numbers by hand. Ask him about the transition from On-Ramp into a coached block — he will tell you honestly whether you are ready.

Standard 01

Nobody watches from the office.

If a coached session is running, at least one coach is standing on the platform with a clipboard. The other is on the rail with the camera.

Standard 02

Every coach trains their own block.

Coaching staff lift on the same program they prescribe, in their own time, and their numbers are on the board by the door.

Standard 03

Switch coaches whenever you want.

Requests go to the desk and are honoured at the end of your current block — no negotiation, no retention conversation.

Matching

Which coach should
read your sets.

You do not choose on day one. The intake screen decides which record fits, and the desk pairs you with the coach whose discipline matches — then you can switch at the end of week one.
