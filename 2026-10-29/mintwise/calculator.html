§ 03 — Reading the answer

Three questions this calculator should settle

  1. 01 — Is the ring honest?

    Your savings rate is what is left after everything real

    Most people calculate savings rate on payslip gross and forget tax, pension and the annual insurance bill. Here, income means cash in hand and expenses include the money that is already committed. If the number surprises you, it is probably the honest one.

  2. 02 — Which ring moves first?

    Essentials are a negotiation, lifestyle is a decision

    When the inner ink ring passes 55% of income, savings maths stops being fun: the levers are rent, insurance, debt and transport, and they take months to shift. Below 45%, the fastest gains are almost always in the lifestyle lines — which is also where the weekly figure lives.

  3. 03 — What is the runway for?

    It prices your freedom, in months

    Runway is saved cash divided by monthly spend: how long you could keep your life going with no income at all. Three months makes a bad week survivable, six makes a job you hate optional, twelve makes a career change a plan rather than a fantasy.

§ 05 — Worked examples

Three months, typed out

  • The 8% to 18% fix

    “Dana, 31, retail manager. Take-home $3,180, spend $2,925, rent fixed.”

    Nothing dramatic: two forgotten streaming bundles and a car insurance renewal that was silently rolling monthly. Cutting $310 a month moved her from 8.0% to 17.8%, and the annual insurance payment stopped being a crisis because it became a sinking fund.

    $310 a month · $3,720 a year

  • The spiky-income problem

    “Iftekhar, 38, freelance developer. Six months over $6k, six months under $2.4k.”

    His savings rate looked like 11% because good months were spent as though they were ordinary. Setting the target at 25% of the twelve-month average, then paying the flat weekly figure regardless of income, made the variance stop mattering.

    Runway 2.1 → 5.8 months

  • The household with two opinions

    “Nora & Sam, 34 and 36. One shared ledger, two private categories.”

    They agreed on essentials and disagreed on everything else. Splitting the shared costs from personal spend, then measuring their savings rate on combined take-home, turned the argument into arithmetic they could both check.

    42% of income kept, together
