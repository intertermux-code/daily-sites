We weigh everything in pounds and meals because grants ask for it. What we cannot count is the Tuesday a kid eats before practice, or the morning a retiree decides to come back for a second shift. These are the parts that keep the dock running.

“I Came for Groceries. I Stayed Because Nobody Made Me Feel Small.”

Dana Ferrell spent March rationing pasta. Two shifts at the packaging plant had been cut to one, and the numbers on the fridge magnet — rent, heat, car note — did not move when the hours did. She drove past the Millrace lot four times before she pulled in.

“I had this picture in my head of a folding table and a clipboard and somebody asking me to prove I was poor enough,” she said. “Instead a man named Wendell handed me a basket and said, take your time, we are not going anywhere. I picked the tomatoes I wanted. That sounds so small until you have not chosen anything in a month.”

Her youngest was signed up for the backpack program by a counselor at Harlow Middle, quietly, the same week he handed back a library book. By June Dana was shopping the Mobile Market in her own courtyard. In August she started greeting at that same truck on Thursday afternoons, because she knew which families came for the last of the bread.

Bell Falls, Zip 04016

Marcus Odell, 74, has not driven since his strokes. His box arrives on his porch the third Tuesday of every month, and the driver who brings it stays for ten minutes to talk about the Red Sox. “The bread is the thing I look forward to. The talking is the thing I need.”

The Grower Who Would Not Quit a Block

Ezra Kalb of Riverton Growers called us about 2,400 pounds of misshapen carrots and then, embarrassed, asked whether we also wanted his winter squash. We took both. He now brings us everything the market declines — eight loads a week, in a truck he washes himself.

Tell Us Yours — On Your Terms

Some people want their name on it. Some want only the town. A few want us to write it down and never publish it, which we do too; those stories shape the programs more than the printed ones do.

  • You choose whether we use your real name, a changed name, or no name at all.
  • We never photograph a face without a signed release, and never a child without a guardian present.
  • Sharing a story changes nothing about the food you receive, in either direction.
  • Interpreter available in Spanish, Somali and ASL — just ask at the intake table.
