Five Other Ways to Fill the Warehouse

  • Match it at work. 71 Cedar Valley employers double charitable gifts; tell us the name and our team sends the paperwork.
  • Donor-advised fund. Direct a grant to Bright Harvest CFB, EIN 47-1938204, and skip the income recognition.
  • Qualified charitable distribution. If you are 70½ or older, an IRA distribution to us counts toward your required minimum.
  • Grow a garden for us. Eighteen home gardeners deliver to the Saturday drop table between July and October.
  • Give food directly. Our monthly need list — currently pasta sauce, tuna, 1% milk and infant oats — is posted on the programs page.

Gift & Tax Answers

Yes. Bright Harvest Community Food Bank is a registered 501(c)(3) — EIN 47-1938204 — and your gift is deductible to the extent allowed by law. The emailed receipt states the amount and confirms that no goods or services were provided in return, which is all most filers need. Year-end summaries go out in January.

We divide annual food and food-transport spending by meals distributed, and we include the truck, the fuel and the dock labor that gets a carrot from a farm to a hand. What we leave out is the building, the van fleet and staff salaries — if you add those, a fully loaded meal is closer to $2.10.

Any time, no notice period and no hard feelings. Reply to your receipt, email give@brightharvest.org, or call the help line at (207) 555-0134. Changes take effect on the next billing date, and pausing for three months is the single most common request we get in December.

We honor designations for the Senior Grocery Box, Kids’ Backpack Program and Mobile Market, and we mark the gift in the memo line of our buying sheet. Every other program runs on unrestricted dollars, which is what lets us move money to whatever is thinnest that week. Gifts in memory or honor of someone are welcome too.

We accept them, but we would rather you fundraise. A grocery drive costs us sorting hours, storage racks and, when items arrive past date, disposal fees — roughly $0.42 of value for every $1.00 of food it collects. If your group wants to run a drive anyway, we will send the current need list so nothing arrives we cannot use.

Then supply, not cash, is the bottleneck — and we spend on capacity: a third refrigerated van, more cold storage days, and pre-season contracts with growers who would otherwise plow a block under. Nine months of reserves sit behind this year’s buying plan for exactly that reason.
