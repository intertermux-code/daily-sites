What we run

Seven Programs, Built Around the Person Who Shows Up

We asked 480 neighbors what would make food help easier. The answers were rarely about food: shorter lines, no paperwork, someone who knows your name, produce you actually know how to cook. Here is what we changed because of it.

30 sites Pantries, schools, fridges and trucks
41 towns Riverton, Marsh and Bell counties
Zero forms No income test, no ID required to eat

The lineup

Pick the Door That Fits Your Week

Every program below is open to any neighbor in the valley. You do not qualify for one and then get routed to another — you just choose the easiest way to get fed this week.

Every week

Neighborhood Grocery Network

Fifteen partner pantries where you shop a shelf-choice layout instead of receiving a pre-packed bag. Choose your own vegetables, protein and staples; the milk case is at the back so you walk past everything first.

Mobile Market

Two refrigerated trucks that turn a church lot or a courtyard into a grocery store for ninety minutes. Items cost $0.25 to $1.50, coupons spend like cash, and the stop is announced on the block the week before.

First visit

Three Steps, About Twenty Minutes

No appointment, no case manager, no proof of anything. Bring a bag if you have one and a cooler if you are grabbing dairy on a hot day.

  1. Say hello at the table

    One volunteer, seated at the entrance with a clipboard you are not asked to fill out. Tell them how many people you feed and what night you cook. That is the whole intake.

  2. Shop with a basket

    Pick produce, protein, dairy, pantry staples and something you want purely because it tastes good. Household of four takes roughly 25 pounds; there is no ceiling and no audit.

  3. Take the extras

    Bread that day, cat or dog food, hygiene items, baby formula, and a recipe card for whatever is unfamiliar in your bag. Come back next week — the cycle resets every seven days.

Agencies & groups

Run a Pantry, Shelter or Club? Let’s Split the Work

Forty-eight community partners pull from our warehouse on a standing route, which keeps their shelves full and their budget free for staff. If you serve Cedar Valley neighbors and are not on the list yet, the process takes one site visit and a signed food-safety acknowledgment.

  • Wholesale-price purchasing on meat, dairy and shelf-stable staples.
  • Refrigerated delivery on Tuesday and Friday routes, or pick up at the dock any weekday.
  • Sharing of our current need list, so drives collect what we can actually use.
  • Training on cold chain, allergen labeling and dignity-first intake, free for staff and volunteers.
