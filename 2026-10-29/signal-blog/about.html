02 Why This Exists

A Newsroom Built Around Reproduction

“Technology reporting collapsed into two modes: press-release transcription and vibes. Neither can be checked, so neither can be argued with. We chose a third mode — bring the instrument.” Ines Vogel, Publisher, 2019 founding memo

Signal started in 2019 as a newsletter written by two engineers who were tired of reading benchmark charts without axes. Seven years later the desk runs to nine reporters across three cities, and the rule has not changed: a claim needs a measurement, and a measurement needs a method you can read in ninety seconds.

That constraint is expensive. It is why we file fewer stories than our competitors and why we sometimes decline a story entirely because nobody on the desk can reproduce the figure. It is also why two of our datasets have been cited in a parliamentary committee and one was used as evidence in a procurement challenge.

We do not accept advertising, sponsored placement, affiliate links, vendor review units, or paid travel. The newsroom is owned by a cooperative of its writers and by a reader membership that holds veto seats on editorial appointments. If you want to argue with a dispatch, the appendix is in the same document as the conclusion.

04 Standards & Corrections

Six Rules Every Dispatch Passes

See them applied →
01

Publish the Method With the Finding

Every measurement piece carries its instrument, sample window, and failure count in the same document. If a reader cannot reconstruct the number, the number does not run.

02

No Vendor Review Units, No Paid Travel

We buy our own hardware at retail and pay our own way to every facility we visit. Companies that give us equipment get a disclosure line, not a favourable sentence.

03

Corrections Are Front-Page Work

Factual errors are fixed within four hours and logged at the foot of the piece with the original wording. Nineteen corrections ran last year; each is listed in the log.

04

Tail Statistics or Nothing

We report p95 and p99, never a median alone. A single central figure is how an industry learns to stop looking at the people waiting longest.

05

Right of Reply, Counted in Days

Anyone named in a Signal dispatch gets the specific claims and at least 72 hours to answer. Three of this year’s pieces changed their headline as a result.

06

Sources Choose Their Exposure

Anonymous sourcing is approved by two editors, is used only for first-hand knowledge, and never for opinion. Datasets are pseudonymised at the row level before publication.

05 Seven Years, Short Version

How the Desk Got Its Instruments

  1. 2019

    Two Engineers and a Newsletter

    Marta Ellison and Ines Vogel start Signal with 340 subscribers and a rule against quoting a chart without axes. The first issue runs to eleven paragraphs.

  2. 2021

    The First Probe

    A €2,400 reader grant buys the hardware for the delivery-network measurement that becomes the Latency Ledger. The dataset is published under an open licence.

  3. 2023

    Reorganised as a Cooperative

    Writers take collective ownership; a reader membership gains veto seats on editorial appointments. Advertising revenue, which was 41% of income, drops to zero by design.

  4. 2024

    Lagos Bureau Opens

    Dev Okonkwo and Samuel Whitaker start covering platforms and models from West Africa, and the desk gains a second time zone and a third legal jurisdiction.

  5. 2025

    Cited in a Procurement Challenge

    The tender-document series is entered as evidence in a public cloud procurement challenge in the Netherlands. Nothing about the reporting changes; the filing confirms the reading.

  6. 2026

    Twenty-Seven Datasets, Nine Reporters

    Signal now maintains 27 published datasets and reaches 27,411 readers weekly — still with no advertising and still correcting itself in public.

07 Ethics Policy & Openings

What We Omit, and Who We’re Hiring

Conflict declarations. Every reporter files a standing declaration of holdings, advisory roles, and side contracts, reviewed twice a year by the standards editor. Two of the nine currently hold small equity in public technology companies; those holdings are printed on the relevant dispatches.

No embargoes as exclusivity. We accept embargo windows for coordination and safety, never for marketing advantage, and we say so in the piece when we do.

Harm test for exploit reporting. Vulnerability stories run only after a fix path exists or a 90-day disclosure window closes. We have held three pieces back on that rule this year.

Reader correction route. If you can reproduce a different number from our own method, the desk will run your result next to ours. Four pieces this year include a reader measurement.
