Behind the Frames

What the photographs don’t tell you.

Four things every guest asks after seeing these pictures. Open one and it unfolds.

Still curious about the space? Hours, seating and access details are on the Visit page.

Why is it so dark in there?

Because the fire is the only light source worth having in the room. We run two practical downlights over the pass so the kitchen can see, and one reading lamp by the host stand.

If you need more, ask for the tables along the Miller Street windows — they take the last of the daylight until about nine, and they are the only ones with a dimmer under the ledge.

Does the food taste like smoke?

No — and that is the point of cooking over coals rather than logs. Once the flame has collapsed into embers, the smoke is gone; what reaches the food is radiant heat and a Maillard crust.

Only three items on the list are actually smoked: the trout rillettes, the cellared cheese, and the butter. Tell us if you want the rest pushed closer to the flame.

Can I watch the kitchen?

The Fire Bar holds eight guests directly across the hearth, close enough to feel the heat change when the grill cook opens the vent. It is the best seat for the first seating and the worst for a quiet conversation.

Request it in the notes when you book. Bar seating is first come, first served for walk-ins from 5 PM.

May we photograph dinner?

Please do — phones only, no flash, and nothing that blocks the pass. We photograph the plates ourselves each Monday for the reprint sheet and will happily send you the frame of your own table.

For engagements, anniversaries and anything that needs a tripod, email us first and we will hold the room until 4:30 for you.

Come Stand Near It

Twenty-eight seats, and the fire takes up six of them.

Evenings book eight to twelve days out. Tuesday and Wednesday are the easiest, and the cheapest seat in the house is the bar.
