Before you order

The kitchen handles wheat, dairy, nuts, and shellfish in one small space, so we cannot promise a fully allergen-free plate — tell us what you need avoided and we will build around it where we honestly can.

Vegetan and vegan menus run in parallel on every service; ask, and one arrives within a minute. Parties of nine or more are served the seven-course Fire Menu at the Grain Room table, $145 per guest, with 48 hours’ notice.

Pairing Notes

Ines Vargas walks the room course by course.

Open a course to read what she pours against it — and why. Nothing here is a rule; it is a starting argument.

Full cellar list, including fifty magnums and the back-vintage shelf, is available at the table. Ask for it by name.

Starters

Start with the Chablis or a cool-climate pét-nat — something with enough acid to reset the palate after the ash-char reds. Grilled oysters want the Etna Rosso; the beets want the pét-nat and nothing else.

If the table order splits, pour the Poulsard. It is chilled, it is light, and it will not start a fight with either plate.

Mains

Dry-aged ribeye is the only dish on the list we will let pick its own wine: a northern-Rhône syrah, twenty years old if the shelf has it. The lamb wants the Morgon. The chicken wants the Chablis, drunk like beer.

Charred cabbage is the surprise — a skin-contact Georgian qvevri, tannic enough to stand against the lacquer-dark leaves.

Desserts

Burnt honey tart with a pour of vin jaune — six years of oxidative flor under the cork, tasting of walnut and curry spice, is the single best pairing in this building.

Otherwise, stop drinking wine and take the warmed junmai sake with the chocolate mousse. That is the one I would fight you on.

By the glass

Twenty-six pours change nightly: whatever a bottle was opened for, and whatever is at its best that week. There are no house-pour shortcuts and nothing under a $40 retail spine.

Tuesdays carry no corkage, so bring the bottle you have been holding on. Tell us at booking and we will get the cellar to temperature for it.
