§ 03.2 Reading

Which band you landed in, and what it buys

Benchmarks from 38,400 Mintwise households, grouped by take-home band. Compare like with like.

Savings rate bands, with the practical consequence of each.
BandRateMedian householdWhat it buys first
Negative< 0%7% of membersA floor plan: cut the three largest recurring charges

Strong15–30%26% of membersDebt payoff dates, or a deposit on something
Exceptional30%+7% of membersChoices: retire early, work less, fund someone else

Rate under 5%?

Do not cut lifestyle yet. Find the recurring charges first — Bill Radar usually turns up $30–$60 a month that nobody chose deliberately.

Rate 5–15%?

Move the surplus on the day pay lands. Money that sits in checking gets spent; money in a jar does not. Sweep is a feature for a reason.

Rate over 15%?

Your constraint is now decisions, not dollars. Set a date for each jar; un-deployed surplus quietly loses to inflation.

Free days is the number that lands with people: your surplus expressed as days of life you already paid for.

§ 03.4 Method

Four definitions, stated before the maths

Every calculator has hidden choices. Here are ours, in writing.

Take-home, not gross

The ring divides by what actually reaches your account. Employer pension match, HSA contributions, and pre-tax commuter deductions sit outside this worksheet — Mintwise reads them from your payslip and shows a second, inclusive rate in the app.

Debt payments are spending

Minimum payments reduce the ring. Extra principal above the minimum counts as retention, because it buys your freedom date. Most apps treat it as spending and make the picture look worse than it is.

Annual costs are smoothed

Car registration, holiday gifts, property tax: divide by twelve and enter the monthly figure. The calculator assumes a normal month, and a normal month is the one you plan against.

One month, then three

A single worksheet is a snapshot with all the bias that implies. Run it for three consecutive months in the app and the trend line replaces the guess.

If a number makes you feel worse without telling you what to do next, it is a vanity metric. We deleted two of those in 2025.
