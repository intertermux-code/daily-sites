§ 01 Envelopes

Give every dollar a ceiling before it leaves

Envelope budgeting is old, dull, and the only method we have found that survives contact with a real month.

You set a monthly figure per category. Mintwise reserves it inside your checking balance, so the money is spoken for the moment it lands — and the number you see when you open the app is genuinely spendable.

Three policies per envelope, chosen once and editable forever:

  • Rollover — unspent money waits for next month. Good for quarterly bills.
  • Sweep — leftovers drop into a goal jar or your surplus line on the last day.
  • Borrow — an overage pulls from a named donor envelope and leaves a note in the ledger.

Members run a median of 11 envelopes. The oldest still in daily use is “Christmas, begun in October.”

The borrow note matters more than the ceiling. Seeing “Groceries took $40 from Dining Out” is the whole lesson.

§ 02 Sorting

It reads the merchant description so you don’t have to

A 2,100-recognised-merchant classifier plus your own corrections, retrained on your household within the first week.

How a transaction gets filed

Raw descriptor → merchant normalisation → category guess → your rule history overrides it. Corrections are permanent unless you delete them, and they apply backwards through your whole archive.

  • aDEBIT 4471 GREENLINE MKT 11/04Normalised → Greenline Market · Groceriesconf. 0.97
  • bACH NORTHWIND STUDIO PAYROLLMatched rule → Income · Salaryconf. 1.00
  • cCARD 8820 SQ *FEEDDOG 1142Ambiguous → asked you once, filed under Petsconf. 0.61

Confidence, shown

Anything below 0.70 is queued for one tap instead of guessed silently.

Sub-categories that split two ways

Groceries at a big-box store gets divided by receipt line items, not guessed by store name.

Refunds and reversals

Returned money cancels against the original entry, so an envelope never looks overfunded.

§ 03 Rules

If this, then that — written in plain English, stored as text

Rules are readable strings you can edit by hand. No black box, no drag-and-drop maze.

Write a rule once and the ledger keeps applying it: splits by percentage, recurring charges, tenant payments, employer reimbursements, the annual insurance bill that must be smoothed across twelve months.

Three live examples from a member’s file

  • R-01when merchant = “Harbor Point” → Housing, $1,640, day 1then sweep remainder to Envelope “Rent Buffer”on
  • R-02when merchant contains “Costco” → split 62% Household / 38% Sisterthen flag if either share exceeds envelopeon
  • R-03when amount > $400 and category = Insurance → amortise over 12then post to Jar “Premiums” monthlyon

Every rule records when it fired, what it changed, and how to undo it in one click.

§ 04 Radar

Bill Radar finds the increases before they become a year

Subscription drift is the quietest way a household loses $600 annually. Mintwise compares each recurring charge against its own history.

What it watches

  • Price changes — same merchant, new amount, flagged the first cycle.
  • Trials ending — a free period converting to a charge, warned three days out.
  • Duplicates — the same service billed twice or on two cards.
  • Forgotten annuals — the domain renewal, the cloud plan, the app you deleted in March.

What members did about it

$31.40median recurring spend per member flagged for review
31average subscriptions found on first connect
68%of warnings lead to a cancel or downgrade within a week

Mintwise never contacts a merchant or cancels for you. It shows the charge, the first date it appeared, and the amount change.

The report reads like a proof: charge, prior charge, delta, first seen. You can argue with a number that is written down.

§ 06 Household

Two adults, one ledger, privacy kept on both sides

Shared finances fail when one person does the bookkeeping. The Household plan gives both people the same numbers and separate private envelopes.

Two logins, one file

Each adult signs in on their own device with their own passkey. Nobody shares a password, and both histories stay reconcilable.

Splits by rule

50/50 on rent, 70/30 by income on groceries, 100% personal on the things that are nobody’s business but yours.

Private envelopes

Mark any envelope private. Totals still roll into the household surplus; the line items do not appear for the other person.

The monthly “who owes who” figure is the one number that ends the argument. We print it at the top of the summary.

§ 08 Security

Read-only by design, audited on the calendar

The shortest honest description of how your data is handled, with the dates to check us on.

SOC 2 Type II

Last audit closed 14 August 2026. Report on request within one business day.

Encryption

AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit, per-account key envelopes, 90-day rotation.

Read-only tokens

Plaid and Tink connections cannot move funds. There is no transfer API in our stack.

Passkeys & 2FA

WebAuthn passkeys by default; hardware keys supported; SMS as a last resort.

What we will never do

Sell transaction data Run ad networks Train third-party models on your ledger Move money on your behalf Require a card to start

Revenue comes from subscriptions, full stop. That is the entire business model, and it is why the reports read plainly.
