01 / Implants

Replacing one tooth, or a whole arch

A titanium root that behaves like a tooth.

An implant is a medical-grade titanium screw placed into the bone where the root used to be. It fuses over eight to twelve weeks, then carries a ceramic crown that matches the tooth beside it. Unlike a bridge, nothing is filed down on either side.

We place them with a CBCT scan and a printed surgical guide, so the screw goes where it was planned to the tenth of a millimetre. Most single implants are done in one visit with a temporary crown fitted the same afternoon — you leave with a tooth, not a gap.

  1. Assess — 45 min

    CBCT scan, gum health check, bone volume measured. If bone is thin we say so and quote the graft at the same time rather than after you start.

  2. Place — 60 to 90 min

    Local anaesthetic, gas and air available. Guided surgery, stitched, same-day temporary crown for front teeth.

  3. Heal — 8 to 12 weeks

    Two review visits, both included. Soft diet guidance and a night guard if you clench.

  4. Restore — 2 visits

    Digital scan of the healed implant, laboratory crown fitted, bite adjusted, ten-year guarantee issued in writing.

What can go wrong

Roughly 2 in 100 single implants fail to fuse, usually in the first three months and usually in smokers. A failed fixture is replaced at no fee if you attend the reviews. Peri-implantitis after year three is a cleaning problem, not an implant problem — which is why your hygiene slots are part of the guarantee.

Fixture, abutment, laboratory crown, surgical guide, imaging, sedation if requested and every review to the twelve-month re-check. Bone grafts, when needed, are quoted separately and always before consent.

02 / Orthodontics

Adult alignment is two thirds of our work

Straight teeth on a schedule you can see.

Clear aligners move teeth in a printed sequence of trays, changed every ten days. Fixed ceramic brackets do the jobs aligners cannot — rotations of premolars, closing a bite that has collapsed, any case where roots must be moved a long way.

Most clinics review every six to eight weeks, which means a plan that drifts is discovered months late and re-treatment is billed to you. We review twice a month from a tablet at your own pace, and mid-course refinements are inside the quoted fee.

04 / Hygiene & prevention

The visit that stops the other visits

Fifty minutes, not twenty.

Our hygienists work to a periodontal chart recorded at every six-monthly visit, so bleeding pockets are caught as a trend rather than an emergency. Air-polishing with glycine powder removes staining and biofilm without scaling healthy enamel — it is quieter, and it does not scrape.

You are taught the two or three surfaces that actually matter for your anatomy, and shown them on your own plaque disclosure. Fluoride varnish and interdental brushes are included; nobody sells you a water flosser at the desk.

  • Gingivitis reversal: 74% at 6 months
  • Halitosis clinic on request
  • Smoker & diabetic protocols
  • Mouth-guard moulding £65

Hygienist-led, dentist-backed

If your chart shows progression, the hygienist books you with Dr Okonjo that same week rather than leaving a note in your file.

06 / Questions

Asked at almost every consultation

Before you book

Q1Do I need a referral from another dentist?

No. Walk in as a new patient. If you have recent X-rays, bring them — we will use them rather than repeat the dose, and we will say so in writing.

Q2What if the plan changes halfway through?

Anything already agreed stays at the agreed price. If we discover extra work, you get a revised written plan with the new line itemised before it happens, and you keep the option to stop where you are.

Q3I gag badly in the chair. Can you still treat me?

Yes. Nitrous oxide is free and available at every appointment, X-rays are taken with a slim digital sensor, and impressions are replaced by scans. For severe reflex we refer for IV sedation through our anaesthetist, quoted at £280 per session.

Q4Is an implant always better than a bridge?

Not always. If the bone behind a front tooth has already collapsed, a bridge may give a better gum contour with one fewer surgery. We show you both options costed and let the anatomy, not the fee, drive the recommendation.

Q5Do you take NHS patients?

We hold an NHS contract for children under 18 and for urgent care. Adults can register for routine NHS bands; ask at reception and we will tell you honestly which band your exam falls into.

Q6How do you handle dental anxiety?

Mark it on your booking form and the first visit is a conversation only — no instruments, no chair. Roughly half our patients arrive anxious; raised-hand stops are agreed before treatment begins and honoured for the whole course.
