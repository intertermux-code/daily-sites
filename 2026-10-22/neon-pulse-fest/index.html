What Neon Pulse Is

A Warehouse Built for the Drop

We took a 1970s freight shed on the Hudson, lined it with 340 speakers, and pointed every window at the water. Then we booked people who play long.

Neon Pulse is not a festival with a dance tent. It is four rooms that behave like one continuous set: the Pulse Mainroom for headline techno, Afterglow for house and disco edits, the Bass Bunker buried under the loading dock, and Solar for ambient, dawn and sit-downs. You can walk between any two of them in ninety seconds without stepping outside, which matters when it is 3 °C and the river wind is doing its worst.

Sound is the whole point. Every room runs a tuned Funktion-One rig, measured and aligned by the same crew each night, and every artist plays on the same system they will hear back on their own bootleg recording. Set lengths are honest — two hours minimum for non-headliners, no thirty-minute showcase slots.

The Weekend, Night by Night

Three Nights, Three Different Temperatures

Friday is the warm-up with teeth. Saturday is the one people fly in for. Sunday is the longest night of the year, deliberately, so you can leave whenever you want and still find something playing.

01

Friday
Warm-Up

18 Dec · 18:00 → 03:00

Cheapest night, deepest room. The Bunker opens first and the Mainroom doesn’t really lock in until one.

Doors, ferry, and a slow open

Gates take the first wave from 18:00. Friday is the night to arrive early: the Solar room runs a two-hour ambient opening set while the queue is still outside, and you get to see the rig aligned before the room fills.

Kessler-Nunes opens the Mainroom

The Portuguese duo play a rare joint live set — two drum machines, one mixer, ninety uninterrupted minutes of warm acid before anyone is expected to be sweating.

Mainroom · Friday, 22:40

Bass Bunker till close

Four residents back to back from 23:00 to 03:00 with the lights off — a house rule, not a stunt. Bring a torch app if you like reading the decks.

02

Saturday
Peak

19 Dec · 18:00 → 04:30

Both headliners, both rooms, the biggest crowd and the coldest air. Pre-book the cloakroom if you’re carrying a coat.

Marisol Vane, 23:00

The Chilean producer closes the Mainroom with a 90-minute AV set built for the warehouse truss — the only scheduled show on the weekend with a full lighting rig and no encore.

The Bunker queue is real

Capacity is 480 and it is the tightest room on site. Saturday it turns away people at 01:30. If the Bunker is the reason you came, go in before midnight, not after the headliner.

Afterglow · Saturday, 02:05

Hot food runs till 04:00

The Long Table courtyard stays open all night on Saturday with six kitchens, two of them halal and one fully vegan. The soup station at 03:00 is unofficial tradition at this point.

03

Sunday
Long Night

20 Dec · 18:00 → 06:00

Booked to end at sunrise because the solstice falls three days later and we have never had the nerve to stop early.

Dubbed-out closing

A resident plays the final hour on tape delay and reverb only, from memory, with no track list. Nobody knows what happens, including them.

Sunrise on the pier deck

Solar opens onto the waterfront deck at 05:00 with heated blankets, coffee, and the room’s low end run through the outdoor stack. Statistically the quietest 40 minutes of the weekend.

Solar deck · Sunday, 05:38

Getting home

The last N train is long gone, so we run shuttles to Atlantic Terminal and to the Manhattan bridge taxi stand every fifteen minutes from 05:00 to 07:00. Free with any pass.

How to Do It Right

Four Moves Before December

  1. Lock a pass by 7 November. Phase 2 weekend pricing ends that Friday and single-day Friday is already inside its final allocation.
  2. Pre-book the cloakroom and a locker. The pier gets to 2 °C at 04:00 and coat lines stall the entrance. $12 cloak, $25 locker for the whole weekend.
  3. Charge your wristband before you arrive. Site is card-free; top up online and skip the two kiosks that are always the wrong side of the crowd.
  4. Plan one room per night. Nobody hears everything. Pick a room each evening and let the other three be a rumor.
Check Pass Availability
“I have played warehouses in Berlin and Rotterdam that were nowhere near as committed as this one. The Bunker at 03:00 with the lights off is genuinely one of the top three nights of my year.” — Odessa Blight, 2025 Bunker resident

Sound level at the Mainroom barrier measures 102 dB(A) peak; free high-fidelity earplugs sit in every bar queue, and the Solar room is engineered to stay under 85 dB so you can have a conversation in it.
