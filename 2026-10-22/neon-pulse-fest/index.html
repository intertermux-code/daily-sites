01 — what this is

A Cold Warehouse,
Three Nights of Heat

Neon Pulse takes over Meridian Yards — nine hectares of decommissioned dockland sheds on the east bank — and rewires it with 340 kW of sound and a lighting rig you can read from the harbour. No grass, no golf, no afternoon folk band. Concrete, lasers, and a set that is still running when the sun comes up.

Every ticket is registered to a name in the festival ledger, every stage keeps its own log, and every set is indexed by day, room and time — the same way this site is built. Filter it, expand it, plan your night before you arrive.

Velvet Static

Tbilisi-born, Berlin-based, and currently the most disciplined headline set on the circuit: a 110-minute live performance with four drum machines and a wall of analogue delay. Her 2025 album “Iron Garden” spent nine weeks on the Beatport techno chart.

Expect no opener, no encore, and a single continuous arc that ends in total darkness.

Set length110 minutes FormatLive A/V, 4-piece Last visitSun 2024, Warehouse 9

Kobalt Sun

The Dutch producer behind “Solar Wind” returns with a 90-minute set pitched for the biggest room on site — euphoric, 148 BPM, and unapologetically loud. His closing slot last March ran two hours over schedule and nobody complained.

Set length90 minutes FormatDJ, CDJ + mixer LabelNorthline Recordings

Nullpoint Drive

A three-piece from Glasgow that plays techno like a rock concert — modular synths, real drums, and a frontman who climbs. Their closing set is 90 minutes and finishes at 23:30 under the pier floodlights.

Set length90 minutes FormatLive trio RoleFestival closer

Ferric Lily

Resident at Concrete Belfry, twelve years running. Her late slots are the reason Warehouse 9 has a 06:00 curfew rather than a 02:00 one. Bring earplugs; the room is a reverberant brick box and she drives it hard.

Set length150 minutes FormatDJ, vinyl select Room note108 dB peak, protection available

Subrosia

Jungle roots, neurofunk present. She fronts a 90-minute back-to-back with engineer Ilya Vex, and the Reactor's 40 kHz-top sub array was re-tuned specifically for this slot.

Set length90 minutes FormatB2B with Ilya Vex RigRe-tuned L-Acoustics KS28

Circuit Monk

Harp, tape loops and a contact-mic'd water tank. His Sunday-afternoon set is the one the parents on the family hours turn up for, and it doubles as the quietest room on site.

Set length75 minutes FormatLive, seated NoteUnder 14s accompanied, 14:00–17:00

Hex Machine

Synth-punk electro from Rotterdam with a custom 4×4 LED matrix built by the crew at Studio KADE. It is the most visually ambitious show on the bill and it never repeats a pattern.

Set length80 minutes FormatLive A/V, matrix rig StrobeYes — photosensitive warning at door

Zenith Coast

The Australian duo runs the sunset hour on the Slipway rig before moving indoors. Their mix is the friendliest on the bill — big melodies, no kick-drum arms race, singalong endings.

Set length100 minutes FormatDJ, duo Also playsSlipway outdoor, 19:40
