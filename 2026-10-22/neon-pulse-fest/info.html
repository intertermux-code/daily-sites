Know Before You Go

Site Info & FAQ

A freight shed on the Hudson with four rooms, one gate, and a very specific set of rules. Read this once on the train and you will not need to ask anyone anything on the night.

The Building

Pier 94, West 23rd & the River

Fifty-two thousand square feet of 1972 concrete cantilevered over the Hudson, with a ten-meter ceiling truss that was engineered for cargo and now carries 48 tonnes of lighting and line array. Indoor throughout, one heated courtyard, and the deck facing west — which is where you want to be at 05:30.

Rooms, and what each one is like

Pulse Mainroom · 4,200

Standing, wide sightlines, one raised accessible platform at the left of the barrier. Coldest room at the doors, hottest at the console. Bars on both flanks so the queue never blocks the floor.

Afterglow · 1,600

Mirrored west wall, low ceiling, vinyl-friendly rig with a gentle top end. Seated ledge along the back for when your feet have had enough.

Bass Bunker · 480

Under the old loading dock: concrete, sub-only at 01:00, lights off from 23:00 as a house rule. One door, one staff member, and a genuine capacity count.

Solar · 700 + deck

Floor cushions, warm lighting, under 85 dB so conversations work, and a hearing loop. Direct door to the waterfront deck, heated blankets from 05:00 on the weekend mornings.

Getting There and Back

Four Ways In, Two Ways Out at 06:00

Pier 94 sits between the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway ramp and the Hudson River Greenway. Street parking on the pier approach is cleared from 16:00 to 10:00 each festival day, so do not plan on it.

Rail

Subway

A/C to Canal Street, then the C23 bus one stop west, or a fourteen-minute walk down West 23rd. From Brooklyn, the G to Nassau Avenue is nine minutes on foot.

Last train north 00:30 · Sat night only

Water

NYC Ferry

Bay Ridge–Battery Park route stops at Pier 11, two minutes from our gate. Saturday and Sunday last boat south leaves 01:40; Sunday northbound resumes 06:40.

$4.00 tap · 2 min to gate

Road

Car & Rideshare

Pick-up and drop-off live on West 23rd between the pier gate and 11th Avenue — the police close that block from 17:00. Nearest paid garages are on 12th Avenue; $40 pre-book from the order builder.

Drop-off zone: W 23rd & 11th Ave

Bike

Bike & Scooter

The Greenway runs straight past the pier. Staffed rack for 600 bikes inside the north gate, free, and Citi Bike docking on 23rd. No lock to the fence — they get cut.

600 spaces · staffed till close

Late

Neon Shuttle

Free with any pass. Runs every fifteen minutes from 05:00 to 07:00 on Saturday and Sunday to Atlantic Terminal and the Manhattan taxi stand, from the south ramp.

05:00 → 07:00 · every 15 min

Cold

Average Temperatures

Mid-December on the water runs 0 °C to 6 °C with wind that comes off the river sideways. Queue in the heated marquee — it is indoor, it is not a suggestion.

2 °C typical at 04:00

At the Gate

What Comes In, What Stays Home

Welcome

  • Small bag up to 25 × 30 cm — one per person
  • Empty reusable bottle, any material, sold-free at fountains
  • Earplugs, medication in original packaging with your name on it
  • Portable charger under 20,000 mAh, in the bag not in your pocket
  • Warm layers, since the deck is open at sunrise and you will go out
  • Photo ID dated and legible — checked once, at the gate

Turned Away

  • Large bags, backpacks and suitcases — lockers cost $25 for the weekend
  • Outside food and drink of any kind, including sealed
  • Professional cameras, detachable lenses, and tripods
  • Drones, laser pointers, fireworks, spray paint
  • Foldable chairs and blankets over 1 × 1 m in Solar
  • Anything you plan to sell — we have forty vendors and no space for more

Prohibited items are refused at the gate, not stored. If you drive in with a backpack, you drive back out with it.

On the Night

Money, Lockers, Medical, Quiet

Cashless Site

Your wristband is the wallet. Top up online before you travel and you skip the two recharge kiosks, which are always the wrong side of the Mainroom.

Lockers & Cloak

$25 for a 40-litre locker for the whole weekend, $12 per item per night in the heated cloakroom at the north gate. Weekend and VIP passes include one free cloak item.

Medical & Harm Reduction

Two staffed tents — east of Afterglow and inside Solar — open all night with paramedics, water, testing, and a place to sit with someone. No one gets asked for a ticket when they need help.

Quiet Room

A padded, dim, low-stimulation room for 60 people next to medical, with no music at all. Staffed by the access team and open from gates to close.

Accessibility, Properly

Pier 94 is step-free from the street to every room, including the Bunker, which has a lift where the freight hoist used to be. Raised viewing platforms sit beside the barrier in the Mainroom and Afterglow, and a companion or carer travels free on any tier — book the space through the order builder add-on, which costs nothing.

The Solar room has a hearing loop. Access parking bays are on West 23rd inside the cordon, and the queue lane has a seated waiting area with a heater. Tell us your access needs at checkout and the gate crew gets the note before the night starts; you should not have to explain anything twice in the cold.

Only during the scheduled AV show and the closing set — phones down, and stewards will ask twice and then look away. Everywhere else, film all you like. The rule exists because our photographers shoot the whole weekend and a wall of screens ruins both the show and their frames.

Around 2 °C on the queue at 22:00, and colder at sunrise on the deck. The marquee queue is heated and fully indoor, all rooms are heated, and Solar hands out blankets from 05:00. Dress for the walk from the train, not for the dancefloor.

The Long Table courtyard holds six kitchens — two halal, one fully vegan, one ramen bar that runs till close, plus a late soup station that opens at 02:00. Hot food stops thirty minutes before each room’s curfew. Prices are capped at 15 % above their Brooklyn storefront, and a full meal is $12 to $16.

Eighteen and over across the whole site after 18:00, with ID checked at the gate. The one exception is the Sunday matinee in Solar, 14:00–17:00 on 20 December: all ages, no alcohol served, free for anyone under 18 with a ticketed adult.

Meet point is the yellow pillar at the north end of the courtyard, staffed all night. Lost property sits at the information desk beside it and is held for fourteen days; wristbands are deactivated and re-issued there instantly with photo ID, so a lost band is not a lost night.

Zero tolerance for dealing, full tolerance for people asking for help. The medical tents provide water, testing, ear defenders and a place to sit, and no one is searched or questioned about what they have taken. If someone stops responding, get a steward — we would rather spend twenty minutes on a false alarm than miss one real case.

Headline slots are locked; everything else can move by up to thirty minutes for flight delays and rig checks. The app pushes a change the moment we know, and the same board beside the north gate is updated by hand. Curfews never move — if a set runs long elsewhere, the last hour of Solar gets cut, not the permit.

Email hello@neonpulse.fest for anything general and access@neonpulse.fest for access requests — both answered within two working days, and within the hour during festival week. No phone line: it is one hundred people in a warehouse and email is faster. Press and vendor enquiries have their own forms in the footer of the homepage.
