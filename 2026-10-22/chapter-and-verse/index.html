What We’re Pressing Into Your Hands This Week

Every Thursday we write a card by hand and put four titles on the window table. These are the ones leaving the shop fastest.

Fiction

Invisible Cities

Italo Calvino

Eleven cities, each one a single paragraph long. Nadia reads it every January and lends it to anyone who says they don’t like poetry.

Essays

The Pillow Book

Sei Shōnagon

A court lady’s lists, gossip and weather reports, written in Kyoto around the year 1000. Somehow still the wittiest book on our shelf.

Poetry

Narrow Road to the Deep North

Matsuo Bashō

Haiku as a travel diary. We keep a display copy by the till so you can read one verse before you remember why you came in.

№ 03

Three Rules We Run the Shop By

一

Leave Room on the Table

Half our floor is empty on purpose. Ma — the pause that makes the rest legible. You can spread six books out, compare them, and take your time without a queue forming behind you.

二

Sell the Worn Copy Too

Our second-hand shelf is not an afterthought. A cracked spine and a stranger’s margin note are evidence of reading, and we price them honestly — usually a third of new.

三

No Bestsellers on the Window Table

The window is for what we love, not what ships in pallets. If you want the number one, we’ll order it in by Thursday and hold it behind the till, no charge.

№ 04 · The Chapter Society

One Book a Month, Nine Strangers, Endless Tea

The Chapter Society has met on the first Tuesday of every month since 2016. Twenty-two members, four reading strands, and a rule that nobody has to have finished the book — only that nobody has to have read alone.

  • Four strands

    Fiction · Poetry · Essays · Japanese in translation

    Pick one strand or drift between them; the calendar shows what each group is reading next.

  • From $9 a month

    Or pay for the year and take a second title home

    Membership covers the book, the room, the tea, and first refusal on every repaired copy we take in.
