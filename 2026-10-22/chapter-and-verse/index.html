§ 04

The Shop Itself

Fourteen Alma Lane was a boot-repair shop before it was ours. The floor still creaks in the same four places, the back window still lets in weather, and we have kept the counter — a repaired thing of our own, sanded down from the old workbench.

We opened in 2009 with eleven shelves, a borrowed card terminal and a rule we still keep: nothing gets stocked because a distributor made it easy to buy. Every title in this building was chosen by a person who can tell you why.

Seventeen years on, we are four full-time booksellers, a part-time children’s buyer, and roughly nine hundred people who come to book club most months. The shop is small on purpose.

Ask Ada about the 1912 Eliot she found in a box of fishing magazines. She still talks about it weekly.

§ 06 — Standing invitation

Four clubs. One long table. No audition.

Fiction Firm meets the first Tuesday. Poetry Late runs late on the last Thursday. The Tomorrow Society takes the second Sunday, and Children’s Hour happens every Saturday at half past ten — parents welcome, welcomeest actually.

Places are free except the five dollars for coffee and biscuits, and nobody has ever been turned away for not finishing the book.
