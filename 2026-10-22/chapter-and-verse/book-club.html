One Rule, Then the Rest Are Habits

You only have to have started.

The rule is real and the only one. Come having read twelve pages, or the first chapter twice, or nothing but the opening line you couldn’t put down. Someone always has, and the conversation is better for it.

Bring your own copy.

Members take two titles home at a time and bring them back with their opinions pencilled in the margin. We keep four spare copies of each month’s book at the till for anyone who needs one.

Say the honest thing, quietly.

No podium, no prepared remarks, no one holding the floor for nine minutes. If a passage confused you, that is the most useful sentence available to the room.

Leave the spoilers at the door.

Half the room is usually on chapter eleven. Whoever finishes first has the job of shutting up about the ending, which everyone takes seriously and nobody manages.

The long oak table, set out for a full society meeting on the first Tuesday.

質問

Questions We Get at the Counter

What if I miss a meeting?

Nothing happens. The strand moves on, the book stays in your bag, and you catch up at the next one. Members miss roughly one in five meetings and nobody keeps score.

I haven’t read anything difficult in years.

Then the Essay Table is the wrong room and the Fiction Strand is the right one, because it is currently reading a novel about a midwestern professor that anyone can finish. Start there.

Do I have to buy the book from you?

No. Use your library copy, your ten-year-old paperback, or a member’s spare. Two titles a month simply means two titles leave the shop — the third one you found yourself.

Can I bring a guest?

Twice a year, any strand, no charge; beyond that, have them fill in this form. Two Titles members get two standing guest seats at every meeting.

What if Tuesdays don’t work for me?

The Translation Shelf meets on the third Sunday at four, and the Silent Reading Circle is free on Saturdays at ten with no membership required. There is also a Wednesday binding bench where the conversation is entirely about glue.

How small is too small for a group?

Three. If a strand drops below three we merge it with another rather than run an empty table — it has happened once, in 2021, and the members involved still read together privately.

Where the Nine Dollars Goes

Roughly four of it is the book, one is the tea and the lemon cake on the first Tuesday, and the rest is the electricity that keeps a shop this draughty warm enough to sit still in. Nothing goes on marketing, because the shop has never paid for a billboard.

If the arithmetic ever offends you, say so at the till and we will show you the ledger, which lives in the drawer under the scales and is written in pencil.
