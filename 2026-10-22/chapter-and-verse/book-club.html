Pick a Circle. Bring a Mug.

A circle is twelve to twenty-four people who agree, in writing, to finish the same book. We keep the groups small enough that nobody hides, meet them in the shop, and supply the tea, the schedule, and the mild shame that makes people read chapter nineteen.

    A season on the spine

    How a Circle Year Runs

    Six sessions, one book per month, and a shape that has not changed since 2013 because nobody has managed to improve it.

    Sep

    Session one · the choosing

    Books on the Table, Hands Up

    We lay eight candidates out and let the circle argue for twenty minutes, then vote. Everyone gets the chapter schedule before they leave the room, printed, and a copy of the book at ten per cent off.

    Copies held at the deskMembers only
    Oct

    Sessions two to four · the working months

    Chapters, Not Impressions

    Each evening has a page range and a chair with a prepared question. No one is asked how they "felt about it" — you are asked what happens in chapter eleven and why the author lied to you in chapter four.

    90 minutes · tea at the intervalTwelve to twenty-four seats

    Everyone Who Is Behind Comes Anyway

    Mid-season is where circles thin out, so session five is deliberately on the shortest reading of the year and we order the good biscuits. Attendance across all six circles last year: ninety-one per cent.

    Before you ask

    Questions the Desk Hears Weekly

    What if I have not finished the book?

    Come anyway. Nobody is quizzed at the door, and the chair's job is to get the people who did read it to stop talking long enough for the rest. That said, the page ranges exist for a reason: session five is the one you do not want to miss.

    Do I have to buy the book from you?

    No. Members get ten per cent off the circle title from the shelf behind the desk, and we will happily order a copy you already own a different edition of. Bring your own dog-eared paperback; several people in the Victorian circle have been using the same one since 2014.

    How much does it cost?

    Thirty dollars for a season of six sessions, invoiced at the first meeting, and it pays for the tea, the biscuits, the printing, and the person who stays to put the chairs back. If that is the reason you do not sign up, tick "Any circle" and write "cost" in the notes box. We have never turned anybody away for it.

    Can I join a circle that has already started?

    Mid-season, yes — you will be a fortnight behind and one conversation short, and the room makes up for it. Fully booked circles keep a waitlist; the science fiction group ran one for two months last year and started a second table rather than turn people down.

    Is the back room accessible?

    The ground floor and the reading room are step-free with a twelve-centimetre lip at the shop door; the rare room and children's loft are up stairs only. Sessions are booked downstairs whenever requested, and large-print and audio copies of every circle title are kept on the shelf behind the desk.
