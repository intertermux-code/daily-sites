本の説明

Three Ways Something Leaves This Shop

New cloth, ordered from small presses and two distributors we’ve used since the beginning. If a title exists in English and you ask on a Tuesday, it is usually on the hold shelf by Thursday.

Repaired second-hand, bought from house clearances along West Burnside and re-backed on the bench upstairs. A cracked hinge is not a fault; it means the book was opened. These are priced at a third of new and listed with whatever the previous owner wrote inside the cover.

Lent, occasionally. Chapter Society members take two titles home at a time and bring them back with their opinions. Nothing on that shelf is for sale, which is the only rule in the building we never bend.

№ 02b · From the Counter

Reading Notes, Written on the Back of Receipts

On Reading Slowly on Purpose

Tomás Rivera, bookbinder

“Every repair teaches me the same thing: people do not wear out the middle of a book. They wear out the first twenty pages and the last ten. So I read the endings first now, and go back to the beginning when I’ve decided the writer earned it.”

Why Poetry Sells in the Winter

Nadia Okonkwo, owner

“November through February we move the poetry case two metres closer to the door. It is not cleverness, it is light: the four o’clock dark makes a short poem feel like a complete visit. People buy in the same spot they stood to read.”
