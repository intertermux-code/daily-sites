§ 03

How We Decide What to Stock

Four tests, applied at the counter, in this order. Written up after two arguments in the staff room, so at least it is documented.

Test One

Can a bookseller sell it from memory?

If we cannot tell you in four sentences what a book does and who it is for, it does not get a facing position. Shelf space is the scarcest thing we own, and it goes to conviction.

Test Two

Is the edition worth owning twice?

We stock fewer titles in better texts. Two shelf slots of a properly edited Austen beat six of whichever print run arrived cheapest that quarter.

Test Three

Does it survive being handed over?

A title has to be a book one of us would physically put into a stranger’s hands as a gift. This test has killed more stock lists than any distributor argument.

Test Four

Does the shop need it next to the others?

Shelves are sentences. Le Guin beside Shelley beside Sayers makes an argument about what reading does. We buy neighbours, not products.
