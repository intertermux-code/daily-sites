Along the spine

How a Book Ends Up on This Rail

Four steps, none of them automated. Roughly one title in nine that we look at earns a place, which is why the shelf is shorter than it could be and better than it needs to be.

1

Monday · the buying day

Four Buyers, One Trolley

Every rep sample, review copy and trade catalogue in the back room on a Monday morning. We argue about the same table differently, which is the entire point of having four of us.

Monday, closed to the public~90 titles reviewed
2

Tuesday · the reading bench

Eighty Pages Before a Decision

Nothing joins the rail on a jacket blurb. The buyer reads eighty pages standing at the mezzanine bench, and if the prose still surprises them by sixty, they go back for the full proof.

Our own rule since 2011Roughly 1 in 9 accepted
3

Wednesday · the card

A Staff Card, Signed and Dated

Every accepted title gets eighty words minimum from the person who bought it, handwritten on a card pinned above the front table. Then it is shelved in the open, facing out or in as it suits.

3,102 cards to dateKept in the drawer by the till
4

Thursday · the shelf talk

Ten Minutes at Seven O'Clock

Anyone in the shop can demand we talk about a new spine out loud, standing up, for ten minutes. Free, no seats needed, and frequently wrong, which is how half of last month's staff picks changed.

Thursdays, 19:00Free · drop in

Bring us your shelf

Trade-In, Buying Day, and What We Turn Down

We buy collections outright on the first Saturday of the month, and we take trade-in at the desk any day we are open. Bring the list first if there are more than forty volumes; it saves both of us a morning on the floor.
