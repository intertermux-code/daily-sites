A ribbon warehouse, then a bookshop

Built by Hand, One Shelf at a Time

We opened with four hundred books bought on a market day, a till held together with bookbinding tape, and a lease we could not really afford. Seventeen years later there are 12,480 titles, four buyers, a bench that repairs spines, and a room that smells the same as it did on the first Tuesday.

Number 14

The Room That Was Already Wrong for Everything Else

The ground floor of 14 Cobb Street spent sixty years as a ribbon and trim warehouse, which is why the floor is dead level, the windows face east, and there is a freight door wide enough for a piano. We wanted a shop with daylight on the spines and we did not know what else to ask for.

Nora took the lease in the spring of 2009 after eleven years in a chain bookshop's ordering department, where she had grown tired of a system that put four copies of the same book in front of everyone and none of the ones she had read. She bought the first stock at market: four hundred titles, sorted on the floor of an empty warehouse with a kettle on a hot plate.

The tape on the till was holding for a week. It held for two years, and then Marguerite fixed it properly.

Est. 2009 · Cobb Street

The Winter We Almost Closed

A rent increase of forty per cent and a heating system that failed in the same fortnight. We stayed open through a January with the shutters down, ran the first Poetry & a Pint on a paraffin lamp, and renegotiated by moving the rare stock upstairs out of the damp.

Behind the desk

Four People Who Know Where Everything Is

Between them they have worked in bookshops for fifty-one years. Each takes one aisle, one buying decision, and one evening of the week.

Nora Vance

Owner · Poetry & the circles

Eleven years in a chain's ordering department before she had enough of it. Writes the chapter schedules by hand, sits on the poetry ladder, and has opinions about the 1818 Frankenstein text she will happily give you for free.

In the shop since day one.

Marguerite Oyelaran

Rebinding bench · Mezzanine

Trained in Lagos and Leipzig, arrived in 2013 with a nipping press nobody else knew how to use. Re-sewn, re-backed and re-cased 4,180 books and refuses to work on anything she has not photographed first.

Saturdays, 10:00 – 14:00, at the bench.

Tomas Reyes

New arrivals · Children's loft

Runs the front table, the proof shelf, and the Saturday morning storytime he did not volunteer for and now would not trade. Keeps the whiteboard for plots that need diagrams.

Ask him what he is reading. He will show you.

Amara Kofi

What we hold to

Five Rules, Written on the Inside of the Desk

Nailed to the underside of the counter since 2011, repainted once, and occasionally cited out loud during a staff meeting that has gone on too long.

I.

We Sell the Book We Would Reread

If a buyer cannot name a reason to hand it to a stranger, it goes back on the proof shelf. One title in nine survives that test.

II.

Repairs Before Replacements

A split spine is a bookkeeping problem, not the end of a book. The bench has saved more of these than we have sold in the last five years.

III.

Prices in Pencil, Explained Out Loud

Rare stock is graded twice, written down twice, and defended to whoever asks. Nobody here has ever been allowed to say "that is what it is worth" without a reason.

IV.

The Room Is Free for Readers

Any group that meets monthly gets the reading room for nothing. That is not generosity; it is how thirty-two hundred people ended up on the list.

V.

Never Sell a Thing You Cannot Explain

Gift cards, subscription boxes, and the shelf list all get explained in writing before you pay for them, including the parts that are not worth paying for.

Colophon

Set in This Shop

Cormorant Garamond for headings, Spectral for the text, and Caveat for what gets scribbled in the margins by whoever is on the late shift.

Finding us

Fourteen Cobb Street

Between the hardware shop that gave us the bell and the place that supplies the wine for Thursday. The freight door at the side stays unlocked from ten until gone six, which is the accessible entrance and the one with the step.
