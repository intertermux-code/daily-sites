№ 05 / 05

For eleven years the ground floor of 17 Ashgrove Row has been a bookshop, and the floor above it has been a repair bench. Nothing about that arrangement has been planned since the second week.

はじまり

№ 05a

Two Shelves, a Loan, and a Room That Was Already Right

In the spring of 2014 Nadia Okonkwo had eleven thousand dollars, a redundancy payment, and a strong opinion that Portland did not need another shop selling the same twelve books in the same window. What it needed, she thought, was somewhere you could stand for forty minutes without being hovered over — a room with a table in it.

The bindery was empty because its last owner, Margaret Wray, had retired and refused to sell the presses. The lease came with them. Nadia put two shelves against the north wall, kept the bench where it stood, and opened on a Tuesday morning with four customers, three of whom came back the same afternoon.

Muriel the lurcher arrived in 2017 from a litter in St Helens and has since decided which events are worth attending. The Poetry Circle started that same winter with three people and a flask, mostly because nobody wanted to go home.

Today the shop holds about four thousand titles chosen by four people, repairs roughly ninety books a year upstairs, and has never taken an investor, a franchise offer, or a payment for a window display. The lease runs to 2031, which is the closest thing to a five-year plan any of us has written down.

The north wall, photographed the morning we took on the fourth strand.

年表

Eleven Years, Abbreviated

2014

The Bindery Opens as a Bookshop

Two shelves, one table, Margaret Wray’s presses still bolted to the floor upstairs. Opening takings: $84.

2016

The First Chapter Society Meeting

Seven people around the long table with six different copies of the same novel. It has run on the first Tuesday every month since, minus two lockdown years.

2017

Tomás Builds the Bench Room

A repair service, properly. First job was a 1953 field guide with a split hinge and a train ticket from 1961 pressed inside; both are still in the book.

2019

We Take the Second Floor and Give It Away

Rather than more stock, the extra room became the back room: twenty-four chairs, free for neighbourhood literature groups, half price before noon.

2021

Doors Closed, Shelf Open

Two hundred and six doorstep deliveries in a year, a poetry strand that met on the phone, and the only time we ever merged a strand for being too small.

2023

The Window Table and Its Rule

Four hand-written cards, restocked every Thursday, and a standing instruction that nothing from the bestseller list goes in the window. Sales went up. We still don’t know why.

2026

Four Thousand Titles, Four People, One Dog

The Poetry Circle has moved its case two metres toward the door for the winter. The lease runs to 2031 and the presses still haven’t been moved.

“A bookshop is not a warehouse with better lighting. It is an argument you have with your neighbours, monthly, about sentences.”
Margaret Wray, bindery owner 1979–2013, on what she sold Nadia the lease for

№ 05b · The Counter

Four People Who Answer the Phone

There is no head office, no rota managed by software, and no one behind a curtain. If you call between ten and seven, one of these four picks up and knows exactly which shelf you mean.

N

Nadia Okonkwo

Owner · Fiction & the Window Table

Started the shop with a redundancy payment and an opinion about windows. Reads two books at once and finishes neither on time; chairs the full society meeting.

T

Tomás Rivera

Bookbinder · Bench Room

Rebacks ninety books a year and teaches Bindery Night on Saturdays. Believes a repaired copy is worth more than a new one and prices them accordingly.

I

Ines Abara

Counter Manager · Events

Runs the calendar, the tea, and the slate on Late Thursdays. Has never in nine years asked a customer to leave, including the man who read for forty minutes.

K

Kei Fenwick

Poetry & Translation

Keeps the Japanese and Portuguese shelves, leads the Translation Clinic, and argues for the lossy version of a haiku every single time. Writes to nobody.

What We Have Refused to Do

間

Fill Every Inch of Floor

A third of the ground floor is empty, which is how a room this size stays quiet enough to read in. We have turned down two shelving donations on exactly that reason.

侘

Hide the Damaged Books

Second-hand copies are marked with what is wrong with them and who owned them before. The worst-looking book on the shelf is usually the one somebody comes back for.

静

Play Music Over the Speaker

There is no sound system. There are shelves, a kettle, Muriel, and whatever the room is doing. On Thursdays after six there is also somebody reading badly on purpose.
