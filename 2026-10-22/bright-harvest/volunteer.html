Volunteer

Three hours, and the Tuesday sort gets done.

No experience, no paperwork, no clipboard duty. Someone meets you at the depot door, shows you the scales, and puts you next to whoever is already doing the job you want to learn.

Claim a shift below

Pick your kind of work

Six jobs, all of them learnable in a morning

Most volunteers start on the Tuesday sort because it is the shift that teaches you where every food comes from. Two of the six below can be done from your own kitchen table.

07:00 – 10:00 · Tue & Thu

Sorting crew

Unload the vans, date-label, weigh into crates. Radio on, tea at 08:30. The single most useful first shift if you want to see how the whole place works.

Physical: lifting 12 kg crates · 4 spots free

09:00 – 13:00 · Mon–Sat

Pantry host

Stand on the shelves and help neighbours find what they cook. You do not check anyone, you do not take notes, you do point at the good sourdough.

People-facing · training every first Monday

06:40 – 11:15 · Tue & Thu

Van runner

Drive the rescue route to growers and back. Standard licence and two years' driving history; a trained volunteer rides with you for your first four runs.

Licence required · 2 spots free

14:00 – 20:30 · Fri

Open Table kitchen

Chop, stir, tray up, wash, serve. 180 covers under head cook Priya Raghunathan, who has taught eighty volunteers to make one very large soup.

Food-handling card issued free on shift one

Saturdays · March–November

Garden working bee

Forty-six beds behind the depot. Watering, staking, harvesting, and the annual argument about whether to grow more coriander.

Outdoors · tools and gloves provided

Anytime, from home

Backpack packer

Take a crate home on Thursday and fill 20 breakfast backpacks at your own kitchen table. Great if mornings or crowds are not your thing.

Flexible · collect from the depot hatch

Two quick confirmations

Your first shift, minute by minute

Nothing about it is a surprise

Keep scrolling and the steps pass by while this stays put — same shape as a Tuesday morning.

06:55

The kettle goes on first

Nadia or a senior volunteer meets you at the hatch. Name badge, closed-shoes check, a mug. You pick which station you want to start on.

07:15

The first van arrives

Twelve crates of Harte & Sons brassicas. You learn the one rule of sorting: anything with mould goes to compost, anything merely ugly goes to the shelves.

08:30

Break, and the truth about coriander

Everyone stops for twenty minutes. This is when you hear why the garden committee is still arguing about herb beds, and which household has been asking for the rescue route to add a Thursday stop.

09:45

Shelves open at ten

By now your crate has been weighed, dated and labelled, and someone in Fenwick Row is choosing cabbage for dinner. You clock out on the board with the number of kilos you handled.
