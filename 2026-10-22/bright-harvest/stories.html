Community stories

Seventeen people, one valley, no tidy endings.

These are recorded with permission and published without surnames where the person asked for that. Nobody here is a “case study”. They are neighbours who told us what actually changed.

Featured

Marisol stopped feeling small at the basket hook

“I called three times before I went. I kept rehearsing what I'd say about why. Then a woman at the door said, ‘Grab a basket, the bread's on the left,’ and that was the whole interview.”

Marisol came to the valley in 2021 with two children and a job that started at 05:00. Between shifts there was no shop open that she could reach on foot, and the cost-of-living grant she applied for took eleven weeks to arrive.

She used the Choice Pantry for six months. In March 2025 she started the Friday kitchen shift, and in September she was rostered on the Thursday rescue route because she wanted to know where the “ugly” cabbage came from.

  • First visit October 2023, Ashcombe pantry
  • Now Friday kitchen and Thursday route volunteer
  • Her note “Bring a list. It makes it feel like shopping, which is the point.”

Pantry · Open Table

“The first thing I cooked for myself in a year”

Devon lost his flat after a boiler fire and lived in a hotel room with a kettle for four months. He took the Cook Well workshop on a Saturday and started buying one ingredient a week.

“Nobody handed me a bowl. Somebody handed me a pan and told me the soup was theirs too, so I might as well learn it.” He now co-hosts the second Saturday session.

Devon Marsh · Ashcombe

Rescue route · Garden

“I grow the things the vans never bring back”

Aiko took bed 19 in the depot garden after her retirement, planning to keep busy. She now grows the salad, herbs and tomatoes the pantry cannot source from growers.

“Thirty-one kilos of tomatoes last August. The Wednesday shelf used to have nothing green at all by noon. Now there's a fight over my basil, and I mean that as a compliment.”

Aiko Tanaka · Upper Wick

Open Table

“180 plates, and I only wash them”

Samuel started on Friday washing-up because he could not cook and did not want to sit. Three years on he still washes, and he has decided exactly who needs a second plate of rice.

“I know every table by the noise. The loud ones are fine. The quiet ones I go and stand next to for a minute. That's the whole job I actually signed up for.”

Samuel Boateng · Fenwick Row

Backpack Breakfast

“The bag looked like everyone else's bag”

Rachel's youngest was flagged for Backpack Breakfast in 2024. She resisted for a month, then agreed when the school explained the food goes into an ordinary book bag.

“What undid me was Monday. He came home saying the milk in his bag matched the one at home, so nobody could tell. That's a nine-year-old's priority and it told me everything about what we'd been worrying about.”

Rachel Nkemelu · Gretna Hall

Farm Rescue Route

“Forty minutes with Harte & Sons, twice a week”

Peter drives the Thursday van. He is sixty-eight, retired from haulage, and has an argument with every grower about how brassicas should be crated.

“They give us 1 in 6 of what they pick because nobody else will take the awkward grades. That isn't charity for us — it's the difference between a profitable harvest and one left in the field.”

Peter Lindqvist · Ashcombe

Pantry · Thursday shelf

“Four kilometres on foot, and the shelf is closer”

Tomás and his mother share a rented place above the old garage. The nearest supermarket is a four-kilometre walk; the Fenwick Row Thursday shelf is two hundred metres.

“I go at ten when it opens because the good bread is gone by eleven. I don't need anybody's pity, I need the Thursday bread and someone to tell me which cabbage keeps well.”

Tomás Herrera · Fenwick Row

How we got here

Sixteen years, told from the depot floor

Every milestone below was started by somebody who is still on this page or still on the roster. Scroll on — the heading holds.

Help write the next one

  1. 09

    A fridge on a porch

    Eileen Harte put a secondhand fridge at the end of her drive in Ashcombe with a hand-painted sign: “take what you need, leave what you can”. By the third week the neighbours were restocking it faster than she could clean it.

  2. 12

    Mill Lane and a real roof

    The Riverdale Credit Union waived rent on the old grain store for five years. Volunteers built the first shelving from pallets, and the porch fridge moved indoors.

  3. 15

    Choice, not parcels

    A pantry member told the stewards that pre-packed parcels meant getting coriander she could not cook and never seeing the fish. The depot refitted as a shop that August. Complaints dropped to zero.

  4. 18

    First rescue van

    A donated refrigerated minibus and Chidi Okonkwo's willingness to let us take the field seconds turned the depot from a collection point into a supply line.

  5. 21

    Open Table starts

    St Bride's Hall opened its kitchen on Fridays. Eighty covers became 180 within one term, and the waiting list to wash up became a rota.

  6. 25

    One point two four million meals

    The garden, the backpacks, three satellite shelves and a solar-powered cold room. Audited, weighed, and still short 142 volunteer shifts for the year.
