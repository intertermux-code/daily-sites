Programs

Six programs, one valley, zero paperwork.

Everything below is free, runs on schedule, and asks nothing about why you need it. Scroll through the chapters, then check the full timetable at the bottom of the page.

01

Choice Pantry

Modelled on a small grocery shop, not a hand-out line. You walk the shelves with a basket and choose fourteen items — the tinned tomatoes you actually use, the bread you like, the milk your youngest will drink. Nobody counts your parcels and nobody asks for a payslip.

  • Where 14 Mill Lane, Ashcombe · plus three satellite shelves in Fenwick Row, Gretna Hall and Upper Wick
  • When Mon, Wed, Fri 09:00–13:00 · Tue and Sat 14:00–18:00
  • Volume 2,400 households a month, 14 items each
  • You need Nothing. No ID, no referral, no address proof

Ask about first-visit support

02

Farm Rescue Route

Two refrigerated vans leave the depot at 06:40 on Tuesdays and Thursdays and visit eleven growers, one orchard and the flower market. Everything they collect — misshapen, overgraded, windfall, unsold — is on the shelves by 11:15 the same morning.

  • Rescued 486 tonnes in 2025, roughly 1 in 6 valley plates
  • Growers Okonkwo, Harte & Sons, Bramble Farm, the Ashbee orchard and seven more
  • Drivers Volunteers with a standard licence; a trained second rides along

Ride along on a route

03

Backpack Breakfast

212 children in four valley primary schools leave on Friday with a backpack of breakfast food that needs no cooking and survives a weekend where the nearest shop is a four-kilometre walk. Teachers flag the families through the school liaison, and the backpacks go home in an ordinary book bag so nobody can spot one.

  • Serves 212 children, 44 weeks a year
  • Inside Oat bars, long-life milk, tinned fruit, peanut butter, crackers, cereal
  • Cost $9.40 per child per week

04

Open Table Dinners

Friday at 17:30 in St Bride's Hall. One long table, 180 covers, a hot meal made from whatever the vans brought back that week. Pay what you can, pay nothing, come to eat, come to wash up, come to sit with someone. No list, no booking.

  • Serves 180 covers every Friday, plus the second Sunday each month
  • Kitchen Volunteer-led, supervised by head cook Priya Raghunathan
  • Access Step-free entrance from Church Lane, hearing loop, toddler chairs

Read a Friday night story

05

Cook Well Workshops

Two hours, twice a month, in the community kitchen at Gretna Hall. A valley chef hands you a bag of whatever is in season and a pan you get to take home. Every recipe is costed under $4 a plate and written so it works on a single-burner hotplate.

  • When Second and fourth Saturday, 10:00–12:00
  • Seats 16 per session, kids welcome, creche on request
  • You leave with The pan, six recipe cards, and the bag you cooked from

Reserve a bench

06

The Bright Harvest Garden

A third of an acre behind the depot, forty-six beds, grown by the people who use the pantry. We plant the things nobody donates: tomatoes, soft herbs, salad leaves, beans that dry well. Beds run from March to November and half the crop goes straight to the shelves.

  • Beds 46, each roughly 3 × 1.2 metres
  • Adopt $180 for a season, or give twelve working bees instead
  • Yield 2.1 tonnes last season, none of it purchased

Getting in the door

What using the pantry is actually like

You do not need a referral, a benefits letter, an address, or a reason that sounds good enough. You walk in, take a basket from the hook by the door, and shop. A host is standing by if you want help finding anything, and a private room is available if you would rather sit down first.

Our stewards sign a confidentiality code. We keep a count of households, not a record of who they are. Interpreter cover for Spanish, Tagalog and Polish is on call within the hour.

No ID · No forms · No queue photos

The programs, in plain numbers

1,240,000

Meals moved in 2025

Counted at the shelf, not estimated. Every item leaving the depot is weighed on the same scales we use to invoice our grant claims.

$3.25

Audited cost per plate

Produce, protein, packaging and the van kilometre that delivered it. This is the figure we use in every meal counter on this website.

460

Volunteer shifts a year

The number needed to keep the shelves choice-friendly rather than basket-throwaway. 318 filled in 2025 — we are short, and that is why the Tuesday sort is always open.
