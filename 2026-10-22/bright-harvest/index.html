Where your $25 actually goes

Four stops. Every one of them logged, photographed and counted. Keep scrolling — the story moves, this heading stays with you.

Start a $25 monthly gift

Stop 01 · Tuesday, 06:40

Okonkwo's market garden

Your gift buys the crate, not the middleman. Chidi Okonkwo sets aside the crooked carrots and split-top cabbage that a supermarket will not order — 340 kilos of perfectly good food every week.

Stop 02 · Tuesday, 11:15

The cold room on Mill Lane

Volunteers sort, weigh and date-label within four hours of harvest. Nothing sits. Our cold room holds 12 tonnes and runs on the valley's community solar co-op.

Stop 03 · Wednesday, 09:00

Choice, not a hand-out

Neighbours walk the shelves and pick what their family eats. A mother of three chooses the fish she knows how to cook; an older fellow takes single-portion soup. Dignity is a design decision.

Stop 04 · Friday, 17:30

Community dinner night

The surplus becomes a free hot meal in the hall at St Bride's — 180 covers a night, cooked by the volunteers who packed the crates. Everybody eats the same food at the same table.

Time, not just money

Three hours on a Tuesday changes a whole week

We need 460 volunteer shifts a year just to keep the shelves choice-friendly. No experience, no forms, no clipboard duty — someone meets you at the door and shows you the scale.

  • Sorting crew — 07:00 to 10:00, music always on.
  • Pantry hosts — help neighbours find what they cook.
  • Van runners — licensed drivers collect from growers.
  • Kitchen team — Friday dinners for 180 covers.
