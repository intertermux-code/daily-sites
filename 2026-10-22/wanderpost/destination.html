The Kyoto guide we write our routes from

Everything here is what we actually use when planning the eighteen-day journey: real climate numbers, the months we argue against, the four neighbourhoods worth a base, and the small manners that change how a city receives you.

京都 · KYOTO

Kyoto at a glance

到着 Getting in

Haruka, then a taxi

Kansai airport to Kyoto station is eighty minutes by Haruka express, then a nine-minute taxi to Higashiyama. We book the seat, not the train.

歩く Getting about

Feet and the 81 bus

Most of our route is walkable within four kilometres. Two bus lines and one rail line cover the rest; a bicycle is worth it only in Oharano.

言葉 Language

Twenty phrases suffice

English signage is good and English service is not. Learn the two words for thanks, the one for delicious, and how to ask where something is.

長さ Honest length

Ten days is the floor

Four days shows you queues. A fortnight shows you the city. We would never sell you fewer than ten days here and still sleep well.

    Where to put yourself down

    Four neighbourhoods, four different cities

    Kyoto rewards picking a lane and living in it. These are the four we base travellers in, and what each one is genuinely for.

    東山 · Our default

    Higashiyama

    Temples stacked on a hillside of wooden lanes. You are walking to Kiyomizu before breakfast, which is the whole argument. Noises: tourists until eight, then cicadas.

    • Best for a first Kyoto
    • Night-time is genuinely quiet
    • Three good bakeries within 400 m
    西陣 · Craft north

    Nishijin

    Weavers’ quarters, still working. Narrow lanes, no admission fees, and the sound of looms from eight in the morning. Where you go to look like you live here.

    • Best for craft and textile study
    • Local bathhouse on the corner
    • Twenty minutes by bus to anything famous
    大原 · North valley

    Oharano

    Rice terraces, a thatched hall, and the moss garden worth the bus ride. It feels like another prefecture and takes fifty minutes, so treat it as a destination, not a base.

    • Best for second-week travellers
    • Autumn colour a fortnight early
    • One excellent vegetarian lunch stop
    鞍馬 · Mountain

    Kurama & Kibune

    Cedar ridge above the basin, with an onsen in the rocks and a river dining platform in summer. Cold at night in every month, which is mostly the point.

    • Best for heat relief July–September
    • Twenty-eight minutes by rail from the centre
    • Real hike between the two villages
    烏丸 · Transit

    Karasuma

    The grid, the department stores, and the Nishiki market. Convenient and airless in the way any shopping spine is. We use it, we rarely sleep there.

    • Best if you arrive very late
    • Subway and both bus spines
    • Honest, cheap breakfasts
    嵐山 · West

    Arashiyama

    River, bamboo, monkey park and an astonishing volume of footfall. Beautiful at 7 a.m. and unwise as a base for anyone who wants a city rather than a resort.

    • Best as a day, dawn-start
    • Boat hire beats the walking path
    • Two fine old ryokan if you must stay

    A week, if that is all you have

    Seven days that do not feel like a list

    The full journey is eighteen days. This is the compressed shape we would fight for if you only had a week — two commitments before lunch, always.

    1. D1

      Arrive and do nothing

      Transfer to the machiya, walk to the grocer on Shirakawa-dōri, eat something near the canal. Nothing ticketed.

      Rest · no plan

    2. D2

      Nanzen-ji and the aqueduct

      The gate at opening, the brick aqueduct walk north, then the Philosopher’s Path while it is still mostly locals. Afternoon free.

      6 km · gentle

    3. D3

      Gardens with a gardener

      Three raked gravel courts read properly, with the person who maintains one of them. Then a single tea bowl, no photo allowed.

      Guided · book ahead

    4. D4

      Empty day

      We hand you a drawn map of three bathhouses, the 81 bus, and one good reading room. This is the day travellers remember.

      Deliberate · non-negotiable

    5. D5

      Fushimi from the back

      Rear gate at dawn, the northern ridge down to Udono-Todai-ji, and the whole vermilion corridor to yourself before eight.

      8 km · 300 m climb, return funicular

    6. D6

      Arashiyama by water

      Boat rather than train, bamboo on the far bank, Tenryū-ji at the end of it, and lunch in the riverbed instead of the lane.

      Train 18 min · boat 45 min

    7. D7

      Kurama over the ridge

      Rail north, onsen in the rocks, then the cedar path to Kibune and a long dinner on the river platforms if it is summer.

      9 km · steps, cooler by 5°C

    Where we book people

    Three addresses we trust with our name

    We hold a small number of houses because a bad one costs a whole trip. All three are walked to, not driven to, and all three have staff who know our travellers will ask for more.

    Machiya · Higashiyama

    The house we base most journeys in

    Whole-of-house rental with a private bath, a courtyard maple, and a kitchen you may use. Eight sleeping, six comfortable.

    17 nights · from £2,975 the house

    Ryokan · Nishijin

    Twenty-two rooms and one very good cook

    Tatami, futon, and kaiseki breakfast served in your room. We book the corner rooms facing an inner garden, which we ask for by name.

    £184 pp night · breakfast included

    Temple stay · northern hills

    One night, up the ridge

    Vegetarian dinner, sitting meditation at five, and a bell you will not need an alarm for. Spare and cold and worth a whole day of travel.

    £97 pp · two nights a week only

    Manners that matter

    Nine things that change how the city receives you

    None of this is about rules for their own sake. Kyoto is a working city with twelve thousand residents per square kilometre in the old quarters, and it is polite to behave accordingly.

    • No photography in private lanes, and never of a geiko without an introduction. Gion’s lanes now carry fines and the signage is not decoration.
    • Stand to one side of the staircase at Kiyomizu and let people coming down past. The convention is left in Kyoto, right in Osaka.
    • Take your shoes off at the step and turn them to face the door. A genkan is not a cupboard.
    • Eat the noodles at the counter, not walking. Standing and slurping is normal; eating while moving is not.
    • Carry a handkerchief. Tissues are used at a table and rarely in the street.
    • Do not tip. It is refused, and the refusal costs everyone a small embarrassment.
    • Speak at the volume of a tatami room, especially on the bus after nine in the evening.
    • Ask before sitting on temple steps; some are structural, some are private, most are both.
    • Learn the two words for thanks. The second, said to a shopkeeper, buys more help than any phrasebook sentence.
    礼

    Asked most often

    Questions about Kyoto, answered plainly

    Is cherry blossom season worth the crowds?

    For a first visit, for four days, yes — and only if you accept dawn or nothing. We would rather sell you late April with the petals on the water and half the people gone.

    How much Japanese do I really need?

    Twenty phrases and a phone with the rail app set up. Our route book writes the important ones phonetically, including how to say you are looking for a specific person at an office.

    Can this journey be made step-free?

    Partly, and we will tell you exactly how far. Most temple grounds have gravel, one or two steep timber staircases, and no ramp. We can plan nine of eighteen days fully step-free with a wheelchair hired locally.

    Is a Japan Rail Pass worth it for Kyoto?

    No, if Kyoto is your only destination — the pass pays for itself on long trips, not on a city stay. Buy the airport transfer separately and the rest by day.

    What happens if there is an earthquake or a typhoon?

    We monitor, we call, and we move days rather than cancel them. Every itinerary holds one float day for exactly this, and our number reaches a human at any hour of the Japanese night.

    Can we extend into Nara or Osaka?

    Nara for one night, gladly — it is forty-five minutes and behaves like a smaller, older version of the same culture. Osaka we send people to for a single meal and no longer.
