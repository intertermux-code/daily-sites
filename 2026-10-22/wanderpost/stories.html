Kyoto · 19 days · November

Mira & Tom H. · Bristol · Journey 01

“By the second week the bakery had started saving the last almond bun. That is the whole argument for going slowly, in one line.”

We arrived sceptical about nineteen days in one city. The empty fourth day is the one I describe to people: a bathhouse, a wrong bus, two hours in a reading room we found by accident, and a conversation with a retired gardener who drew us a map on the back of a receipt. Wanderpost had told us not to plan it, which took more nerve than the itinerary did.

Patagonia · 24 days · January

Devi R. · Glasgow · Journey 02

“Three attempts at the Towers. On the third the wind stopped for about eleven minutes. Nobody said anything at all.”

I had booked two other companies who both wanted to fit fourteen things into eighteen days. Wanderpost kept asking what I could give up. The four rest days felt indulgent on paper and were the reason my knees survived the last week. They also held a night at the refugio when the weather shut the pass, and refused to charge me for it.

Marrakech · 12 days · March

Owen & Carla B. · Limerick · Journey 03

“Our guide let us be properly lost for two hours on day three, then found us from a rooftop we could not see from the lane.”

The heat schedule is the thing nobody else gets right. Early market, long shade, late dinner — by day four we had stopped fighting the afternoon and started liking it. The cooking day was the loudest, funniest meal of our year. One honest criticism: the second house is beautiful but the road into it is genuinely rough, and we would have liked to be told that harder.

Lofoten · 14 days · February

Sasha K. · Leeds · Journey 04

“They would not wake us. On night eleven we went out anyway, and the whole sky was moving over the water.”

I came away having slept more than I have in five years. The no-wake-up rule sounded like marketing and turned out to be the best advice anyone has given me: dark tourism fails when you are exhausted, and worked because I was rested for it. The cloud briefing each afternoon was genuinely more interesting than the forecast was.

Kyoto · 11 days · June, rain

Priya N. · Manchester · Journey 01, shortened

“We had the wet week everyone warns about. It rained for nine days and I would do it that way again.”

Hydrangeas, moss, wet gravel, empty gardens, and a city that behaves differently when it is quiet. Wanderpost rebuilt the middle of the plan around indoor things I actually wanted — paper, lacquer, one very long lunch — rather than apologising for the weather. The route book is now on my shelf rather than in a drawer.

One line each, from the others

What the other seven said

  • Ellis & Jun, Kyoto, April “The backwards routing worked. We saw the same moss garden twice, once alone and once with a tour group going through it, and now we know which one was the visit.”
  • Marcus D., Patagonia, December “I was the single traveller and expected to be the odd one out. By the second refugio I was the person carrying the cards.”
  • The Okonkwo family, Marrakech, October “Four of us, two under twelve, and no day that collapsed when one of them refused to move.”
  • Hélène P., Lofoten, January “They promised a cloud map rather than an aurora, and delivered both. The sauna on day nine is in my will.”
  • Ross & Ana, Kyoto, May “The new leaves were a better decision than the blossom and we were told so before we paid for it.”
  • Femi A., Patagonia, February “Fourteen kilometres of wind, and the transfer driver who brought the wrong bag. Both handled before we noticed the cost.”
  • Joan W., Marrakech, February “I use a walking frame. They sent photographs of every threshold before I agreed, which is the only honest way to do it.”
