Compare the four at a glance

Pace

Kyoto is the quietest: one address, five to nine kilometres of walking a day, and a strict rule against two temples before lunch. Patagonia is the hardest and we say so — four consecutive trekking days at most, and a rest day you are not permitted to skip. Marrakech is hot, so the plan is early, then long shade, then late. Lofoten is the one we shorten on bad weather rather than push through.

What is included

All internal transfers and the letterpress route book, accommodation as named (no substitutions of a lower category), airport pickup, twenty-four-hour local phone support, and one guided day per base. International flights, travel insurance, and lunches are yours; dinners we book, and you pay on the day.

Not sure which is yours? Send an enquiry and we will tell you which one we would refuse to plan for you.

Kyoto — Living Stillness

18 days · one machiya base · April to November

Eighteen days from one address in Higashiyama, built around the hour rather than the sight. You will be at the moss garden before the second gate opens, and home for a long bath by four.

The route, day by day

  1. Arrive, transfer to the machiya, nothing else booked. Neighbourhood walk to the grocer on Shirakawa-dōri.
  2. Slow first morning: Nanzen-ji aqueduct walk, then the Philosopher’s Path while it is still mostly locals.
  3. Garden study with a Kyoto-trained gardener: three raked gravel courts, one private tea bowl.
  4. Empty day. We leave a hand-drawn map of three bathhouses and the 81 bus.
  5. Dawn at Fushimi Inari’s rear gate, then the northern ridge path down to Udono-Todai-ji.
  6. Kiyomizu at opening, the pottery lane in Gojō later; a deliberate lunch in the Kamo riverbed.
  7. Arashiyama by boat rather than by train: bamboo grove on the far bank, Tenryū-ji at its end.
  8. Empty day, or the Saturday produce market if you can be up at six.
  9. Craft day in Nishijin: one indigo dye vat, one fan maker, one calligrapher who will critique your handwriting.
  10. Kurama and Kibune across the northern ridge; the onsen in the rocks before the descent.
  11. Oharano and Saihō-ji by bicycle on flat lanes; moss read up close rather than photographed.
  12. Empty day. Most travellers spend it in the reading room of the library on Teramachi.
  13. Day trip to Nara: the hall at opening, then the quieter Kasuga gardens in the afternoon.
  14. Uji for matcha ground the morning you drink it, and one very long look at Byōdō-in.
  15. Kitchens day: dashi made three ways, with the market run included.
  16. Empty day, saved for rain, a museum, or an argument with a book.
  17. Farewell walk at dawn along the Shirakawa canal; tea at a place with no sign outside.
  18. Transfer to the station or airport with your boxes packed the night before.
  • Duration18 days / 17 nights
  • Best windowLate Mar–May, Oct–mid Nov
  • BaseOne machiya, Higashiyama
  • Daily walking5–9 km, flat to gentle
  • Group size1–4 travellers
  • Pricefrom £6,400 pp
  • Single supplement£780 for the trip

Included

  • Airport or station transfer both ways, and the luggage forward service.
  • Seventeen nights in one machiya with a private bath.
  • Four guided days, three of them with specialists, not general guides.
  • One tea lesson, one craft afternoon, one kaiseki dinner.
  • Letterpress route book posted to you ten days before departure.

Honest notes

Golden Week (late April to early May) and the peak maple fortnight in November are the two windows we will argue against. Both are beautiful and both are crowded enough to break the pace the trip is built on.

Patagonia — Walking the Wind

24 days · two ice fields · November to March

Twenty-four days spent walking in two national parks and getting to know the people who run the refugios in between. The wind decides the schedule; we build enough slack that it rarely ruins anything.

The route, day by day

  1. Fly into El Calafate; no activity beyond a slow harbour walk and a long dinner.
  2. Transfer to El Chaltén, gear check with our mountain guide, and a warm-up to the Mirador de los Cóndores.
  3. Laguna de los Tres at first light — the hard uphill, then the lake.
  4. Rest day in the village. Write, repair boots, eat the second-best bakery.
  5. Paso de la Huargua to Laguna Toro with an overnight at the estancia camp.
  6. Huargua exit, transfer north; a hot shower and two hotel nights owed to you.
  7. Bus to Torres del Paine, check in at Refugio Central, afternoon look at the Salto Grande falls.
  8. The French Valley: ascents under hanging glaciers, weather permitting.
  9. Empty day by design — the reserve weather day for the valley.
  10. Base of the Towers at dawn; long descent to Refugio Chileno.
  11. Los Cuernos to Paine Grande along the lakeshore; the best afternoon light of the trip.
  12. Gray Glacier and the icefield edge; optional kayak if the wind stays under forty.
  13. Rest day, hot springs at the lodge if the knees ask for them.
  14. Cross to Argentina; the southern ice field by catamaran, then the Vespignani camp.
  15. Italiano pass, the Glaciares NP north side, one very long day out and back.
  16. Roca and Blanco lakes, and the icebergs in shallow water.
  17. Empty day at the refugio: this is the one travellers remember for the stove and the cards.
  18. Chorillo del Salto and the west shore of Lago Argentino.
  19. Perito Moreno glacier viewing platforms, early, before the coach parties.
  20. Estancia day: horses, asado, and a talk with the person who has worked the ice for thirty years.
  21. Rest day and laundry in El Calafate.
  22. Northern ice field option, or the lower lookout above the lake.
  23. Buffer day for weather and missed flights, which is why the trip is twenty-four days.
  24. Transfer to El Calafate airport; overnight at the aerodrome hotel if the flight is early.
  • Duration24 days / 23 nights
  • Best windowNov–Mar, south summer
  • Daily walking11–18 km, up to 900 m ascent
  • PacksCarried between refugios
  • Group size2–6 travellers
  • Pricefrom £9,150 pp
  • FitnessFour hill days a week, three months out

Included

  • All park entries, refugio beds with dinner and breakfast, and luggage transfers.
  • A qualified mountain guide on the four exposed days.
  • Two nights in El Calafate and one buffer day, paid by us if you need it.
  • Water filters, trekking poles, and a spare dry bag left with you.

Honest notes

February is the windiest month and the least crowded. If you want the Towers at sunrise without other people in the frame, accept that you may need three attempts.

Marrakech — Medina & Palm

12 days · medina then palm grove · February to April, October

Twelve days split between the walls and the orchard, timed for the months when the heat is kind. The medina days are early and late with a long, unapologetic afternoon in the shade.

The route, day by day

  1. Arrive, transfer through the gate by small truck, mint tea and nothing scheduled.
  2. Orientation: the seven lanes we will use all trip, the two fountains, and the bakery that opens at five.
  3. Ben Youssef Madrasa at opening, then the Saadian tombs, then shade and a rooftop lunch.
  4. Cooking market: spice stalls, olives, one chicken, and a tagine you are responsible for.
  5. Craft day: the dyers’ vats, the slipper makers, and one afternoon of your own stitching.
  6. Empty day. Hammam in the morning if you like; otherwise the courtyard and a book.
  7. Gardens: Le Jardin Secret’s restored courts, then the Yves Saint Laurent museum.
  8. Drive out to the palm grove; check into the riad-dar and meet the man who runs the falaj water channel.
  9. Atlas foothills walk to Berber villages, with lunch where the walnuts grow.
  10. Empty day at the grove: the pool, the argument about irrigation, and the sound of the mule at dusk.
  11. Sunset on the Aguelmam Sidi Ali lake shore, and a very long dinner.
  12. Back to the medina for one last morning souk, then transfer out.
  • Duration12 days / 11 nights
  • Best windowFeb–Apr, October
  • BasesMedina riad, then palm grove dar
  • Daily walking4–12 km, mostly flat
  • Group size1–5 travellers
  • Pricefrom £4,890 pp
  • Access notesUneven lanes; step-free route on request

Included

  • Eleven nights across two houses, both booked for their shade rather than their photos.
  • Daily breakfast, seven dinners, and one long market cooking day.
  • Our medina guide for five days — a Marassi, not an agency hire.
  • Transfers to the foothills and one hammam session each.

Honest notes

Ramadan changes the shape of the day and we plan around it properly; some kitchens close and the evenings become the best part. July and August are 40°C and we will not sell them.

Lofoten — Light on the Water

14 days · one rorbu base · September to March

Fourteen days on a island frontier facing the open sea, with the winter dark treated as something to enjoy rather than to burn through in search of green light.

The route, day by day

  1. Fly to Bodø, ferry to Moskenes with your bags on the deck; the crossing is the first sight.
  2. Check into the rorbu, buy bread and coffee at the shop that doubles as the post office.
  3. Reine and the offer beach walk; the blue hour lasts two hours and we plan for it.
  4. Aurora briefing: cloud maps, the Kp index, and our firm no-wake-ups-before-eleven rule.
  5. Empty day. Most people read by the window and sleep unusually well.
  6. Olstinden ridge with a local mountain guide; rope only where the ice asks for it.
  7. Kvalvika beach across the moor, with a thermos and no return deadline.
  8. Fishing morning with a skrei boat, then the catch cooked at the table by four o’clock.
  9. Hamnøy and the old stockfish racks at low sun; hamper lunch on an islet if the wind drops.
  10. Empty day, or Henningsvær and its studio galleries if you would rather be among people.
  11. Hiking and sauna at a friend’s boathouse, cold plunge included and entirely optional.
  12. Unscheduled buffer for weather, ferries, and the one clear aurora night we all wait for.
  13. Slow final day, one long walk you choose, then the last dinner cooked for you.
  14. Ferry to Bodø and onward flight, with the bags packed the night before.
  • Duration14 days / 13 nights
  • Best windowSep–Mar for dark, Jan–Feb for sun
  • BaseOne restored rorbu, Hamnøy side
  • Daily walking6–14 km, some steep and icy
  • Group size1–4 travellers
  • Pricefrom £7,620 pp
  • Aurora promiseWe never promise it; we promise the cloud map

Included

  • Thirteen nights in one rorbu with drying room, wood stove, and good windows.
  • Both ferry crossings, all local transport, and one guided ridge day.
  • A fishing morning with the catch cooked that evening.
  • Crampons, head torches, and a printed cloud-and-forecast briefing each afternoon.

Honest notes

January is the darkest month and the most atmospheric; by early March the sun returns and the ice gets treacherous. Weather can close the ridge for three days — that is why the plan holds a buffer.
