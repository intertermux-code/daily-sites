Block A · Heavy Squat Day

Who it is for

Absolute beginners who have never loaded a bar, and intermediates stuck on the same three numbers for a year. The first fortnight is technical: empty bar, then 40% of your assessed opener. Nobody touches a hard set until the setup is repeatable.

What you get

  • A numbered member card with every working set logged and dated
  • Two coached heavy days and one volume day per week
  • Video form check on week 2 and week 5, reviewed side by side
  • An end-block max attempt with a coach spotting and calling the lift

Sled Lane · 4 × 20 m

Who it is for

Anyone who wants work capacity without hiding behind a barbell. Scale every station: the sled takes plates off, the kettlebell drops from 24 to 16 kg, the row target comes down 5%. You still do the round.

Session anatomy

  • 8-minute preparedness: thoracic, hip, wrist, breath
  • Skill slot — one strength lift at moderate load, 5 × 3
  • Main clock: 8–12 rounds, 40 seconds on / 20 off
  • Finisher: 200 m carry or 500 m row, unrounded
  • Board score written before you leave the hall

Studio B · 07:00 Clinic

Who it is for

Desk workers whose hips have closed, lifters who lose their heels at depth, shoulders that will not go overhead, and anyone returning after time off. Marcus screens you on the way in and writes the segment you actually need.

Clinic structure

  • Screen: ankle dorsiflexion, hip internal rotation, overhead position
  • Two corrective pairings, loaded and timed under the bar pattern
  • End-range strength holds rather than passive stretching
  • Diaphragm breathing set — 4 minutes, coach-cued
  • Take-home card: three drills, ten minutes, daily

Find A Clinic

Hips & Ankles

Couch stretch progressions, weighted ankle rocks, 90/90 transitions. The single highest-return pair for anyone who squats.

Thoracic & Shoulders

Open books, wall slides with a band, loaded pass-throughs to buy the overhead position back without arching the lower ribs.

Breath & Bracing

360-degree expansion, rib-cage position, then a belt-free brace you can hold across a hard double. Breath work closes every clinic.

Queries Before You Commit To A Block

I Have Never Lifted. Which Track?

Start on Powerlifting Foundations and add one mobility clinic a week. The first two weeks are technical and nobody is loaded beyond sixty percent. If the clock frightens you, Metcon is available from week three once the hinge pattern is safe.

Can I Switch Tracks Mid-Block?

Yes, once, and the coach rewrites your opener from a fresh assessment rather than carrying stale numbers across. Switching every fortnight is how people stay exactly where they are, so we ask you to sit with a block for at least three weeks first.

What If I Miss A Session?

Log it and move on. The plan is written with a spare slot each week so one absence costs you nothing. Two consecutive misses on a heavy day means the coach drops the following week’s intensity by five percent — that is built in, not a punishment.

Do You Provide Equipment?

Belts, shoes and chalk are on the rack at both entry doors and included with any tier. Bring your own shoes if you prefer a fitted squat shoe; a lifting belt you have broken in is worth owning. Wraps and knee sleeves are available to borrow on request.

Is There A Minimum Age?

Sixteen with a guardian’s signed consent and a coach-supervised technique pass. Two of our current sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds are on the powerlifting track; both compete in the sub-Junior division.
