    What a good number actually looks like

    A savings rate is share, not virtue. A person keeping 6% of $9,000 a month is not behind the person keeping 22% of $2,400 — but only one of them has a plan for a blown tyre.

    • Under 5% — you are one unexpected bill from overdraft. Fix the buffer before the lifestyle.
    • 10–15% — solid, typical, and quietly enough. Most households who feel behind sit here.
    • 20–25% — your money starts working before you do. Two years at this rate is a deposit.
    • 35%+ — early optionality. Check you are not under-spending on health, help, or joy.

    Getting two more points

    The gains are rarely in the lattes

    When households move their savings rate by more than four points in a quarter, it almost always comes from one of these three. None of them require a spreadsheet.

    01

    Rename the annual bills

    Insurance, road tax, and broadband renewals are the three largest per-hour wins in personal finance. Dividing them into twelve and parking them removes the panic month; comparing them once removes the cost entirely.

    02

    Move the fixed, not the fun

    Subscriptions you forgot and a duplicate streaming plan are worth more than cutting eating out, and cost you nothing in happiness. A thirty-second audit usually finds $40–$80 a month.

    03

    Save the raise, not the rent money

    Every pay increase should hit the vault before it hits the lifestyle. A floor that rises only when you want it to is the cheapest two points you will ever buy.
