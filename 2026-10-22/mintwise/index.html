The method

Four moves, played in order

Most budgeting apps ask you to be an accountant. Mintwise takes the shift: it captures, sorts, protects a floor, and then nudges the leftovers somewhere useful.

01

Capture

Link up to 24 accounts. Mintwise reads balances and pending transactions every 15 minutes, so the picture you see is the one your bank has.

Average accounts linked · 6.2

02

Sort

Every charge gets a plain-English name and a category you can retrain in one tap. The model learns your vocabulary, not a taxonomy from 2011.

Correct on first pass · 94%

03

Protect a floor

Set the amount you refuse to drop below this week. Mintwise schedules the fixed bills inside it and hides the rest from your spending view.

Households under floor · 3.1%

04

Nudge

Money left over at 6pm Friday moves itself to the right vault — rent buffer, tax set-aside, or the Japan account. You approve the rules once.

The first 90 days

Small, boring wins compound faster than motivation

We tracked 41,000 households through their first quarter on Mintwise. The pattern is consistent: the win isn’t a huge cut, it’s a repeated one.

  1. Week 1

    See the true number. Mintwise finds the $312 in subscriptions, duplicate trials and stale memberships most people never total up.

  2. Week 3

    Set a floor you can keep. One number, enforced before spending. Participants who set it in the first month are three times likelier to still be saving in month six.

  3. Week 6

    Let the overflow run. Rounding, leftovers and payday surplus move themselves. No transfers to remember, no discipline required.

  4. Day 90

    Bank the result. Median household: $1,940 saved, savings rate up 11.4 points, and one recurring bill renegotiated away.
