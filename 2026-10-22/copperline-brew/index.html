The Short Version

We Took a Derelict Wire Mill and Learned It to Brew

Marta Ellison and Dev Raghunathan bought Mill No. 4 in the winter of 2013 for the price of a used car and eleven weeks of asbestos abatement. The building had drawn copper wire for telephone exchanges until 1979; the annealing ovens were still bolted to the floor when the first grain truck backed in.

We kept everything we could. The pulleys became light fixtures, the wire spools became barstools, and the copper line that once fed the telephone grid gave the brewery its name and its colour. What we added was a four-vessel German brewhouse, a cold room cut into the old shipping bay, and a twelve-bial tap wall plumbed by the same guy who once fixed the mill’s lathe.

Twelve years on, Copperline pours 2,400 barrels a year to a room that seats ninety. We still anneal our own wort chillers, still label every batch by mill-shift number, and still close at midnight because the block wants sleep.

“A brewery should look like the building it lives in. Ours looks like wire, weathered and warm.”
Marta Ellison — Co-founder & Head Brewer
