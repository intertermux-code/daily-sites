Mill No. 4

A wire works that made telephone cable for thirty years, then sat empty for twenty-five, and now makes beer the same way it once drew copper: slowly, under heat, with someone watching the gauge.

Walk the Floor With Us

How It Started

A Closed Garage, an Auction Listing, and Twelve Thousand Dollars

Marta Ellison was brewing forty-gallon batches in a rented garage in Swannanoa. Dev Raghunathan repaired vintage machine tools and sold the parts he could not save. They met in 2011 over a seized gearbox and spent the next two years arguing about whether a brewery should look like a brewery.

Mill No. 4 came up at a county tax auction in November 2013. It had been empty since 1989, its annealing ovens still bolted down, its copper bus bar ripped out by scrappers except for one forty-foot run above the floor. That surviving line — weathered to a dull reddish brown — became the logo, the colour, and eventually the name.

The first year was not brewing. It was eleven weeks of abatement, a new sprinkler system, three failed inspections, and a grant from the state for adaptive reuse of industrial buildings. Copperline poured its first public pint on 12 April 2014: Kiln Side amber, from a fermenter that had been in service for nine days.

“We did not renovate a building. We gave a machine a new job.”
Dev Raghunathan — Co-founder & Operations

Twelve Years, Abbreviated

From Garage Batch to Twenty-Four Hundred Barrels

  • 2014First pour. One kettle, four fermenters, a tap wall built from the mill’s old conveyor rollers.
  • 2016Kiln Side wins bronze. A regional medal that let us raise prices by forty cents and keep the whole staff.
  • 2018The cold room. We cut a shipping bay into the hillside and doubled our lager capacity overnight.
  • 2020Cans and curbside. No taproom for ninety-one days; we delivered forty-one thousand pints of beer in growlers ourselves.
  • 2022Wood room opens. Eleven bourbon barrels from Kentucky arrive; Ironwork No. 7 becomes the December release.
  • 2024Malt agreement. Copperline signs a five-year purchase with Hollar Creek Farm, thirty-one miles east, for all floor malt.
  • 2026Ninety seats. The patio under the tin roof opens, and the mill now brews every day of the week except Monday.

Who Works Here

Every beer gets a mill-shift number on the label, which means somebody signed the sheet. Here are the four whose signatures are on most of them.

Co-founder · Head Brewer

Mill Rules

Five Things We Do Not Negotiate

  1. Grain within forty miles. Hollar Creek Farm grows our barley and rye. If the harvest fails we brew less, not farther.
  2. No pasteurising, no filtration to clarity. Beer leaves the mill as the yeast left it, which is why a can may hold a film of sediment.
  3. Every batch is dated, not guessed. The label carries the mill-shift number and the pack date so you can taste it young.
  4. The room is for the block. Sound stops at 11 PM on weekdays, and the patio lights go off at ten.
  5. Water is the ingredient we watch. We draw from the same well the mill did and treat it per style, never per mood.
