Process — five phases

Plot, build,
hold the line.

Every phase ends with a decision, not a presentation. Dates move when the scope moves, and you hear it the day we know.

Phase 01 · Weeks 1–2 · Paid

Plot

Interviews with your team and customers, a teardown of the market, telemetry review. Output: a written strategy note with named bets, effort curves and kill criteria — plus a fixed price for the build.

Phase 02 · Week 3

Frame

Information architecture, content model and the flow that carries the business case. We agree on the metrics before we design, so nobody argues about success after launch.

Phase 03 · Weeks 4–9

Make

Identity, interface and motion designed in parallel with the component library. Weekly demos on a live URL with real content, because a static mock has never found a responsive bug.

Phase 04 · Weeks 10–14

Ship

Engineering to production with CI gates, accessibility tests and performance budgets. Staged rollout, analytics verified, content migrated, and a written launch runbook your team owns.

Phase 05 · Ongoing

Hold

Ninety days of support included, then an optional retainer: experiment slate, system health metrics, and quarterly re-audit of the brand and front end against what we agreed.

Working principles

Rules we
keep on purpose.

These are the four things a client notices in week two, and the four reasons we turn around the odd brief.

Every pod is staffed with people who can defend a decision out loud. We do not bill a partner and deliver a graduate, and we cap each studio at six concurrent builds so that stays true.

Progress is a working link, tested on a mid-range Android phone, not a slide about a slide. Demos are recorded and archived so an absent stakeholder never needs a summary.

If a deadline is fictional or a feature is a mistake, you hear it in the week we think it, with the option to prove us wrong. Twice a year this costs us a project. It has never cost us a client.

Documentation, tokens, naming and training ship with the product. A project where we remain the only people who understand it is a project we failed to finish.
