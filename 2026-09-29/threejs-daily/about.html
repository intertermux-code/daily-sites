About

Three things, one sky.

3jS started as a complaint: every to-do app had the same shape — an infinite list with a small checkbox on the left. Infinite lists cannot be scanned. They can only be scrolled.

The problem

A day has a shape. Lists don't.

Everyone knows roughly what their day looks like, and almost nobody can see it. The information is all there — it is just laid out in a straight line with no beginning, no end, and no sense of scale. Twenty items and two items look identical, so you cannot feel the weight of a day you have overfilled.

A circle fixes that. Give the day a circumference, put the hours on it, and overcommitment becomes a visual fact instead of a vague dread. An hour with six things on it is visibly crowded from across the room.

The idea

And then make it a place

A day is not a list or a bar chart. It is a cycle — it comes back around every twenty-four hours, and things move through it. That is a solar system, not a spreadsheet. So the second decision was obvious: build it in three dimensions, in the browser, with the time of day driving the light.

The result is an interface where the primary gesture is spatial. You do not open an editor to move a meeting; you pick the light up and slide it. The animation when you finish something exists because finishing something should feel like something.

How it's built

Five files and a vendored library

No framework, no build step, no package manager. Open the folder in a browser and it runs.

01

orbit.js

The renderer. Builds the starfield, the painted equirect backdrop, the fresnel corona, the shader-driven track, and one group per task. Owns the camera rig, the raycasting, the particle pool and the animation loop.

02

app.js

The store and the panel. Reads and writes localStorage, renders the task list, the HUD and the ring progress, and bridges selection between a list row and a 3D node in both directions.

03

styles.css

One token set: surfaces, ink, three accents and a type scale. Every page and the planner share it, including the reduced-motion block and the bottom-sheet layout under 940px.

04

fonts.css

Space Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono, self-hosted as six variable woff2 files with unicode-range splits. No font CDN, no render-blocking third party, identical type on a plane.

05

three.module.min.js

three.js r161, MIT licensed, sitting in the folder rather than on a CDN. The site renders its own sky from a local file, so it works on a plane, on a dead connection, and years from now.

Privacy

There is nowhere for it to go

The shortest possible version: 3jS is a folder of static files. There is no backend, no account system and no analytics script anywhere in it.

  • Your tasks live in this browser's localStorage under 3js.v1, and are read by nobody else.
  • No network calls are made by the page at all. Fonts, renderer and artwork are all served from this folder, so nothing phones home and nothing is left half-rendered on a bad connection.
  • No cookies are set, and nothing is fingerprinted.
  • Clearing your data means clearing site data in your browser. There is no copy on a server to worry about.
