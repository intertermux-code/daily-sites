The grill decides
what the menu says.

Fourteen pages, one fire, and a chalkboard behind the pass that changes by 6 p.m. if the market does. Prices include the wood; service is added at 18 percent and shared with the whole room.

Vegetables & sides — Charred greens, 12 · Ember potatoes, 11 · Wood-grilled bread, 6 · Dressed cabbage, 10 — are listed at the bottom of every course on the printed card.

To begin, mostly with smoke

Everything here spends time over coals before it meets a plate. Two to the table is plenty; four is a feast.

  • Ash-Buried Beets

    16

  • Grilled OctopusCherry wood

  • Tartare, Cut by Hand, Fire Oil

  • Ash-Grilled Oysters

Where It Comes From

Nine farms, one fishmonger, and a wood pile.

We buy whole animals because the grill rewards it — bones, fat and trim all get used the same afternoon, and nothing sits in a walk-in waiting for its turn.

Beef comes from Palisade Pastures east of the river, dry-farmed vegetables from Corvina in Sherwood, and shellfish from Yaquina Bay boats we name on the chalkboard. Oak, cherry and apple wood is offcut from two sawmills in Sauvie Island; we never buy charcoal, and we never light the pit without it.

Allergen and dietary questions are answered by the person cooking, not a printed key. Tell us when you book and we will write the plate for you.
