The Room

A quiet room that smells of smoke long before you order.

We opened in a former lumber office with the floorboards still warped from the river damp. We kept the warps, the soot-brick, and the habit of building a fire at 3 p.m. whether the room is full or not.

Chef Maren Aldridge runs the grill herself on service nights. She buys whole animals, breaks them down in the morning, and cooks the trim that same afternoon — which is why the menu changes weekly and the kitchen never wastes a bird.

“Fire is not a technique here. It is the only appliance we trust.” Maren Aldridge, Chef & Owner

Four Exhibits

From coal bed to last glass, the evening unfolds sideways.

01 / 04

  1. I

    The Coals

    Three logs of oak burn down from 2 p.m. We rake the coals into three beds — searing, roasting, and gentle — and cook by moving food between them rather than turning a dial.

  2. II

    The Grill

    One open hearth, six grates at different heights, and a cast-pan shelf over the embers. Fish goes skin-side onto the hottest bar; everything that needs patience sits high in the smoke.

  3. III

    The Counter

    Eight stools face the fire. Order the tasting, and you watch every course leave the coals within arm’s reach — the loudest, best seat in the room and the only one we take by phone.

  4. IV

    The Last Hour

    Kitchen fires down at ten. The room keeps its candles lit and pours the good stuff — a smoked old fashioned, or the 2011 tawny we only open on Saturdays.
