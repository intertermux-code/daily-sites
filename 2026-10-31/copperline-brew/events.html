November & December · The Busy Two Months

The Room Fills Up on Purpose

Three barrel releases, one hop market, a Thursday residency that has outlasted two landlords, and the Tuesday crowd who bring their own games. Everything below happens on the apparatus floor unless it says otherwise.

This Friday · One Keg, Thirty Bottles

Barrel Reveal:
Second Alarm

Fourteen months in Kentucky bourbon barrels, pulled by hand from cask No. 17 and canned into wax-dipped 12 oz bottles on the floor in front of you. Thirty of them, each numbered on the back seam, two per person while they last.

Fri, Nov 13 4 pm – 8 pm Free entry Apparatus floor
Hold Two Bottles Read the Beer’s Note

Winter Board Launch

Friday · 5 pm · All twelve lines on · $8 pints until 7

Drying Loft comes back off the cold side, Copperline Sour gets its cherry dose, and the board renumbers for the season. We tap all twelve at once, which is the only night of the year the cellar door stays open during service.

Copper Hour Live: The Loomis Three

Wednesday · 7–10 pm · Two sets · No cover

Organ trio, one upright bass, no amplification past the second set. Our resident midweek show, because Thursday is the packed one and we like giving the quieter rooms a night.

Nov 21

Thanksgiving Pie & Porter Night

Saturday · 2–6 pm · Kitchen open early · $5 pint of Drying Loft

Bring a pie, we provide the coffee and the porter at a price that makes no sense. The last four years averaged 140 pies, which is the reason the front doors come off their hinges.

Dec 03

Hydrant Anniversary Tap-In

Thursday · 12 pm · 600 bottles, one weekend

We brewed the first Hydrant double NEIPA on the day we signed the firehouse lease, and we still tap it on the anniversary. Six pounds of hops per keg, poured into whatever you bring. Bottles go fast; the tap lasts a surprising amount of the day.

Meet Hydrant
Dec 12

Nine Mile Makers Market

Saturday & Sunday · 11 am–5 pm · Patio and loft · 24 makers

Pottery from the east side, bread from the two bakeries that take our spent yeast, leather, records, and a mulled-cider stand that has been requested in writing every year since 2021. Kids allowed, dogs allowed, cash mostly unnecessary.

Apply for a Stall
Dec 31

Last Pour of the Year

Thursday · 8 pm–1 am · Bar menu, no kitchen · Live horn player

We stay open three hours past close on the night, pour the remaining barrel stock by the snifter, and at midnight somebody rings the old engine bell on the tower. Everyone who has ever worked here is invited, which is why the room gets strange in the best way.

Jan 09

Homebrew Night: Judged by Marge

Friday · 6–9 pm · Free entry · Prizes from the merch table

Bring six bottles of anything you made. Blind judged on the bar, scored on paper, and the winner gets a seven-barrel batch brewed here with their recipe on the board for a month. Twenty-eight entrants last January.

Every Week, Same Time

The Standing Slots

Tuesday · 6 pm

Long-Table Games

Bring the box, we supply the table and the light. Winner takes the last pour of whichever keg is closest to empty. Regular crowd of about twenty, and nobody has ever been turned away for being bad at it.

Wednesday · 6 pm

Brewer’s Panel

Five glasses, five prototypes, the whole crew at the end of the bar. Occasionally we let two guests in. This is where Wire & Ember died twice and then worked.

Thursday · 7 pm

Copper Hour Live

Local trios, two sets, no cover, second round on the band. The loudest the room gets before Friday, and the reason the sound system is worth the money it cost.

Saturday · 10 am

Canning Floor Watch

Watch the run from behind the rail with a coffee or a pint, depending on the hour. Free, unpromoted, and the fastest way to understand why we print two dates on every lid.

Saturday · 2 pm & 4 pm

The Twenty-Minute Tour

Hot side, cellar, barrel rack, then two pours at the long bar. Four people at a time, free, and honestly the best thing we do for no money.

Sunday · 12 pm

Slow Sunday Kitchen

Brunch plates until 4, records on the turntable, and the two barrel pours open for the first time that week. Dogs on the patio, children welcome, nobody in a hurry.
