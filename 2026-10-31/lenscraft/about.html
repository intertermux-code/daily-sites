Fourteen years of standing very still in other people’s best rooms.

I started in a newsroom in Dundee, shooting funerals of industry — a shipyard closing, a fishing town’s last season. When I moved to weddings I kept the same habit: wait, watch, and only raise the camera when something is true.

The short version

I photograph the day, not the idea of the day.

Lenscraft began in 2012 with a borrowed body and a £90 secondhand 50mm. The first wedding I shot properly was in a working boat yard on the Water of Leith — forty-two guests, one marquee, rain at four in the afternoon and a break in the cloud at five past six that lasted eleven minutes. I have planned around eleven-minute windows ever since.

What people usually want is proof that the day felt the way they remember it. That is not the posed portrait by the stone wall, although you will get those too. It is the moment your cousin stops pretending to cry at the reading and actually does. My job is to be there, quiet, with the right settings already dialled in.

I work alone. There is no second shooter unless you ask for one, no assistant holding a reflector, no music played over the vows. It keeps the frame honest and keeps the cost where it belongs — on the photography.

House rules Six, and I keep them

Rule 01

Available light first

One small flash lives in my bag for dark churches and dance floors. Nothing else is lit, and nothing is faked in post.

Rule 02

Four minutes for groups

Family photographs are organised from a list, named, and done. Guests never queue and the cake is never warm by the time we finish.

Rule 03

No shot list of a hundred

Thirty must-haves maximum. Anything else is a note to me, and I will find it or tell you I did not.

Rule 04

Two backups, always

Cards write to a second body mid-shoot. Files land on two drives and an off-site copy before I sleep.

Rule 05

Twenty-eight days

The date is contractual. If I miss it you keep the balance fee — and I have only missed it once, in 2019, in a snowdrift above Callander.

Rule 06

You keep the raw files

Full print release and archive access for as long as I am trading. If I am not, your files migrate with the studio and you will be told where they live.

Log book

How the practice accumulated.

Nothing here was planned. It is what happens when you keep saying yes to the awkward venues.

2012

First wedding, boat yard on the Water of Leith

Paid £350 and a plate of food. The eleven-minute sun break produced the two frames still on my studio wall.

2015

Left the newsroom, went full-time

Twenty-two weddings in the first independent year, shot entirely on one 35mm and a 50mm.

2018

Formatter’s Yard, Studio No. 4

Moved the editing bench into a Leith bindery with a north window. Clients visit for album markup rather than a video call.

2021

The Highlands residency

Nine months based in Lochbroom, photographing elopements in weather that made me a faster worker.

2024

WPPI & Fearless Photographers distinctions

Three documentary frames placed at WPPI Open; two stories published in the Fearless archive of the year.

2026

Wedding number 348

A twelve-person ceremony in a working bothy at 700m, delivered in twenty-six days with the doors still shut against the midges.
