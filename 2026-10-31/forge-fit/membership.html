Starting point

Check where you begin

BMI is a crude screen, not a diagnosis — it cannot see muscle, and it is useless on a lifter. But it is a fair first indicator for people arriving off the sofa, so use it to pick a starting track and then ignore it.

Formula: weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. The bands follow the WHO classification used by the NHS. If you lift regularly, take more from your waist measurement and your barbell numbers than from this figure.

What the number means here

Under 18.5 — build first: Foundation strength, two sessions a week, eat more than you think you need.

18.5 to 25 — straight into any track. Most new members start on Powerlifting with one engine class.

25 to 30 — conditioning-led for eight weeks, then barbell volume once your joints have adapted.

Over 30 — start with Mobility & Rebuild plus low-impact engine work. Ada screens you before load.

Better measures than BMI

Waist
Measured at the navel, same time of day, weekly. This moves before your weight does.
Resting HR
Where the engine is today. Ines tracks it every eight weeks with the two-kilometre test.
Bar speed
How fast the working set leaves the rack. It falls before strength does.
Sessions kept
How many of the week you actually attended. It is the strongest predictor in the building.

Before you join

Membership questions

Yes. Seven days, unlimited classes, movement screen included, no card details. We ask one thing: show up in trainable clothes and arrive five minutes early for your first session.

Flat, firm shoes for the platforms and a belt if you have one. We lend belts, wraps and sleeves at the desk, and lifters in flats are the norm here — squat shoes are a preference, not a requirement, for your first two blocks.

Household plans exist: two members on Full Forge for £122 a month, three for £170. Everyone gets their own programming, log and coach — sharing a plan is about billing, not about training together unless you want it to be.

Come in anyway. If your BMI is under 18.5 we will talk to you before putting you on a block, and we may ask your GP's blessing. Strength training is one of the best things you can do at a low bodyweight — it just needs eating planned alongside it, and Ada will help with that.

Six spaces behind the kiln building, free after 17:00 and all weekend, plus a bike bay with ground anchors inside the gate. The 53 and 142 buses stop two minutes away on Heathmill Road.

Peak is Monday and Thursday between 17:30 and 19:30, when up to eighty people pass through. Classes still cap at fourteen, so the queue is for the bench press rather than the squat rack. Wednesday evenings and Sunday mornings are quiet enough to hear the clock.
