Track 01 · Barbell strength

Powerlifting

Squat, bench, deadlift. Nine-week blocks that stack, each one starting from the number you produced the week before. This is the track that turns “I think I got stronger” into “I added 12.5 kg to my back squat on the 14th of March.”

A normal week

Monday
Heavy squat — top set at RPE 8, then two back-off sets at 92 percent, plus hamstring accessory work.
Tuesday
Bench volume — speed press in threes, then paused reps and pressing accessory circuits.
Thursday
Deadlift — competition stance, singles through to a top set, back and grip accessory work.
Saturday
Optional accessory day: split squats, rows, core, and any lift the coach flagged as your limiter.

Sessions run 70–85 minutes. Rest periods are timed, not chatted through.

Who it is for

Anyone who has lifted for at least two months, or a total beginner happy to spend the first block on an empty bar learning positions. You do not need a number to start.

What you get

  • A written block in your account, week by week and set by set
  • Coach-checked technique on all three lifts every session
  • Test day at week nine with a filmed top set you can review
  • Optional meet prep: opening attempts, commands, kit rules

Honest warnings

Blocks are linear and a little boring. If you want sweat and sprinting, this track will feel slow — take the engine class twice a week instead of skipping it, and read the HIIT track below.

Track 02 · Conditioning

HIIT Engine

Forty-five minutes, hard edges, monitored effort. Intervals are built on row, ski erg, assault bike, carries and light dumbbell complexes so your joints are not punished for your lungs. Bands on the screen, numbers in the log.

Session anatomy

0–8 min
Ramp: rowing, hip hinge patterning, two graded build-ups to working heart rate.
8–30 min
Main block. Either six by ninety seconds or ten by forty-sixty, rotated weekly so no single modal becomes your limit.
30–38 min
Strength finisher: kettlebell carries, push press, core. Load stays moderate because you are already fatigued.
38–45 min
Down-regulation: nasal breathing, measured heart-rate drop, mobility holds at the positions you loaded.

Who it is for

Shift workers, parents with a hard stop, and strong people with a small gas tank. Every interval has a written scaled option, and “scaled” here means a lower target, not a lesser session.

What you get

  • Live heart-rate bands projected so pacing is visible, not felt
  • Split, watts and stroke rate written to your log after every round
  • A benchmark test every eight weeks: two kilometres for time
  • No burpees as punishment, ever

Honest warnings

Two engine sessions a week is the ceiling we recommend alongside a strength track. Doing five is how members end up with sore shins and a stalled squat, and we will pull you back.

Track 03 · Range & durability

Mobility & Rebuild

Loaded range-of-motion work, breath drills and joint preparation. Half the room is here because something hurts; the other half is here so nothing ever will. Sessions end with you able to do something you could not do eight weeks ago in the exact same body.

How a block runs

Screen
Fifteen minutes with a coach: ankle dorsiflexion, hip rotation, thoracic extension, overhead position, grip. You get a written card of your restrictions.
Prescribe
Four drills chosen from your card, held isometrically at end range. Nothing generic, nothing copied off a video.
Load
The new range is immediately loaded — paused deep squats, kettlebell halo, split-stance press — because unused range is lost range.
Re-test
Week four and week eight. If a restriction has not moved, the drill changes; we do not repeat what is not working.

Who it is for

Desk-bound lifters, anyone over forty returning after a layoff, runners with locked hips, and strength members whose squat depth has quietly gone backwards.

What you get

  • A personal restriction card, re-issued every eight weeks
  • A five-minute daily routine you can genuinely fit before work
  • Return-to-bar guidance after injury layoff, in writing
  • Referral to our physio partners when something needs a clinician, not a coach

Honest warnings

This track will not give you a big total on its own, and it is the first thing members drop when training gets busy — which is precisely when it matters most.
