The Forge method

Four steps, repeated forever

Nothing about this is mysterious. It works because we refuse to trade the boring version for the exciting one.

  1. Screen, then load

    Your first session is a movement screen with a coach — ankle, hip, thoracic, grip. We write down what you cannot do cleanly before we put 100 kg on it.

  2. Train the three lifts

    Squat, bench, deadlift three times a week in the strength track, with conditioning slotted around them. Accessory work is prescribed, not chosen from a menu.

  3. Log the set, not the feeling

    Weight, reps, rest, RPE. Your training log lives on the yard tablet and exports to your phone, so week six is a decision rather than a guess.

  4. Test every nine weeks

    A measured test day, a photo of the board, a deload, and a new block built on what the number actually said. Most members stay because the graph keeps going up.

The building

A yard, not a studio

Three hundred square metres of rubber, steel and north light in a converted kiln works. Chalk allowed. Music loud. No lockers the size of a shoebox — actual benches, actual sinks, a bar that closes at 21:30.

Platforms
Four competition-grade lifting platforms with calibrated Eleiko bars.
Conditioning
Eight Concept2 rows, four SkiErgs, four assault bikes, a prowler and a carry lane.
Recovery
Two physio rooms, plunge shower, and a mobility mezzanine with mats for twelve.
Access
Gate code from 05:30 for Full Forge members; staffed desk until 21:00.

Why we built it this way

Coaching is the product

Most gyms sell you floor space and hope the music does the rest. We sell attention. A coach on the floor can only watch so many people at once, which is why our classes cap at fourteen and why the ratio sits on the wall next to the desk: one coach to seven members during every staffed session.

That constraint is expensive and we protect it. It is also the only reason beginners here do not learn bad habits from the person in the next mirror.
