Six ingredients, and the decade it took to need only six

Verdant began in a rented bench above a bookbindery in 2016 with one rule: publish the dose or do not sell the bottle. This is the ingredient record — where each raw material comes from, what it measurably does, and what we stopped making to keep the shelf this short.

Reading this pageSections are numbered as they appear in our lab book. The quotations in the margin are copied from batch notes written between 2019 and 2026.

§ 01

Origin, in one bench

MarginaliaThe first product we sold was a fifteen-batch cleanser made for a friend whose face reacted to everything. Batch 15 is still the formula on shelf number 01.

Founding note

Nina Okafor started Verdant because a label would not say how much

Nina spent nine years formulating for other houses in Emeryville, where the same serum was re-released four times with the active hidden behind the word “complex”. In 2016 she moved to Portland, rented a bench above a bookbindery, and wrote the first specification on the back of a library card: every active gets a percentage on the front label, or it does not ship.

That specification is still the only hiring document that matters here. We are eleven people: four in the lab, three in production, two in the studio shop, two who answer the email. Nobody in marketing.

What followed was subtraction. We launched with nineteen formulas in 2018. We now carry ten. Five were discontinued because they duplicated another step, and four because the active could not be stabilised at a dose that would justify the bottle.

§ 02

The Ingredient Index

Six raw materials do most of the work in this range. Each entry lists the dose we publish, the source we buy from, and the evidence we accept for it.

StandardWe require a certificate of analysis per delivery and an assay of the named active. If a batch misses its window, it is rejected and the supplier is told why.

Ing. 01 · 4% dose

Centella Asiatica

Madecassoside 0.38% assay

Fermented whole leaf from a co-op in Kerala. Triterpenes shorten the time a disrupted barrier takes to close; fermentation removes the sugars that make a standard extract sticky, which is how the dose gets to four percent.

Ing. 02 · 2% dose

Colloidal Oat Lipids

Avena sativa, Idaho-grown

Milled from a single-variety oat in the Palouse. Avenanthramides reduce visible redness within forty minutes on patched skin; the lipids carry them into the cream without a heavy feel.

Ing. 03 · 0.5% dose

Willow Bark Extract

Salix alba, 12% salicin

Slow-extracted cold rather than boiled. At half a percent it keeps follicles open across a nose and chin without the pH drop that makes a chemical exfoliant sting on a compromised barrier.

Ing. 04 · 15% base

French Green Clay

Illite, sun-dried in Vendée

Dried in the open air so the mineral structure stays intact. It absorbs surface sebum and holds water at the same time, which is why our mask lifts congestion without leaving skin tight for an hour.

Ing. 05 · 0.4% dose

Bakuchiol

Psoralea corylifolia seed

A retinol alternative that behaves differently in the molecule and similarly on the skin: our twelve-week panel measured the same smoothing of fine lines with less reported flaking. Cold-pressed, no solvent residue.

Ing. 06 · 3 weights

Hyaluronic Complex

Sodium hyaluronate, 50k / 300k / 1.5M Da

Three molecular weights in one serum: one that sits on the surface, one that holds the upper layer, one that goes deeper and pulls less water with it. Single-weight serums are why so many people feel drier in week three.

§ 03

How a Formula Becomes a Bottle

Four gates, roughly nine months. Fewer than half the prototypes we start reach the fourth.

Rejected this yearA niacinamide fluid that crystallised in the pump at 4°C, and a heavier night cream our own panel called “a hat on a hat” under Mineral Veil.

Gate 01

Spec on an Index Card

One sentence: what the formula is for, the dose, and which existing bottle it replaces if it passes. If the sentence cannot be written, the project stops there.

Gate 02

Twenty Bench Batches

Each batch is 200 grams and each is logged with its pH, viscosity and smell. Stability runs at 4°C, 25°C and 45°C for eight weeks before anyone else touches it.

Gate 03

Sixty Volunteers, Thirty Nights

Half with dry skin, half with oily or sensitive, split across four tone ranges. We record sting, pilling, and what changed by night fourteen and night thirty.

Gate 04

Label, Then Shelf

The front label carries every active percentage before the brand name. Only then does the formula get a number on the index and a place in the shop.

§ 04

A Short Chronology

Missing years2020 and 2021 appear here once. We spent most of them reformulating preservatives so nothing in the range needed refrigeration.

2016

One rented bench

Nina Okafor formulates a cleanser for a friend with reactive skin. Batch 15 becomes shelf number 01 and has not changed since.

2018

First shelf, nineteen formulas

The range opens online with a full complement. Two years of subtraction begin in the lab book, not on the website.

2019

Fermentation moves in-house

Twenty-one-day centella fermentation starts in Portland. The dose in Dew Point Serum rises from 1% to 4% because the leaf sugars are finally gone.

2021

Preservative rewrite

Every formula reworked to stay stable at room temperature for thirty months. Refrigeration requirements disappear from the labels.

2023

Refill programme opens

Glass vessels, aluminium caps, prepaid return labels. Fourteen thousand bottles have been refilled rather than replaced since.

2026

Ten formulas, published doses

The shelf settles at ten. Volume IV of the ingredient index becomes the printed card that ships in every box.
