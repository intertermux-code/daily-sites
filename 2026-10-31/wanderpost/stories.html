Traveller stories, one at a time

Kyoto · 21 days · Spring 2025

“On day nine I stopped taking photographs. Not because there was nothing to see, but because I was shelling broad beans with Ito-san and that was the afternoon.”

Marianne DoyleRetired teacher · Cork

She had worried on the phone about three weeks in one city. “Day four I was bored. Day five I found the bookshop with the cat. By day eleven the shopkeeper asked after my sister, who I had mentioned once, drunk on plum wine, on day two. That is the trip. The temples were good too, obviously.”

Patagonia · 24 days · January 2026

“We spent four hours in a doorway waiting out a wind that could stand you up. Rodrigo made tea. Nobody complained once, which I still find strange.”

Tom SandhuCarpenter · Manchester

“I trained for the walking and nothing prepared me for the waiting. Day nineteen the cloud lifted for two hours and we saw the whole back wall of the towers. Everyone on that trip still talks about those two hours. Nobody talks about the bus.”

Marrakech · 14 days · October 2025

“Youssef made us get lost on purpose on the first evening. I was furious by 18:20 and then a woman in a tile shop walked me back to my own door.”

Adaeze NwosuGP · Bristol

“Ten days in the medina sounded like a punishment until the third morning, when the fishmonger greeted me by name. In London I have lived in one flat for eleven years and nobody has greeted me by name. The mountain week at the end was the quiet part I did not expect — I wrote more letters there than anywhere else on the trip.”

Lofoten · 16 days · February 2026

“The aurora came at 21:40 on our second night while half the group was already asleep. Ingrid did not wake anyone. That told me everything about this company.”

Callum ReidMarine engineer · Greenock

“Five hours of daylight and you spend it in a kayak. The light on the water at 14:00 is the best photograph I have ever failed to take. We also had two full days in a cabin drinking coffee and reading, in February, in the Arctic, and it was the correct decision.”

A note from Aiko Nakamura · 61 Kyoto departures

“The trip does not belong to the itinerary. It belongs to whichever afternoon somebody decides to stay in bed, and the rest of us drive them soup.”

Aiko NakamuraKyoto guide since 2013

“I have led this route eleven times and never once on the same days. In April the blossoms arrive a fortnight early or late and the plan changes. Travellers used to be anxious about that. Now they ask me at breakfast which mountain is clear, and we go there. This is the whole trick, and it is not mine — the travellers taught it to me.”
