Section 01 — Programmes

Three programmes, stage by stage

Open a row to see the sequence, the clinician responsible, the chair time involved, and the fixed total. No stage begins until you have signed that stage.

An implant is a small titanium root that fuses to your jaw, then carries a crown made to match your neighbours. We plan it on a scan, print a guide, and place it through a keyhole in the gum. One clinician — Dr. Okonjo — stays with your case from the first image to the final bite check.

  • Stage 1 · Consultation and 3D scan. Bone volume, nerve position, and gum height are measured. You learn within the week whether you are a candidate at all.
  • Stage 2 · Guide and grafting. A surgical guide is printed from your own anatomy. If bone is short, grafting happens here and is quoted as its own line.
  • Stage 3 · Placement. Sixty to ninety minutes, local anaesthetic, nitrous if you want it. Most people drive themselves home.
  • Stage 4 · Healing. Eight to fourteen weeks with a temporary tooth you can eat with.
  • Stage 5 · Crown delivery. Hand-layered porcelain from our Portland laboratory, adjusted and warranted for ten years.

Dr. Voss caps her active list at forty cases so reviews never run late. She simulates the end point at your first visit and shows it to you before you agree to anything — and she will say when trays are the wrong tool.

  • Stage 1 · Assessment. Bite analysis, gum thickness check, and a scan that takes four minutes.
  • Stage 2 · Simulation. You watch the teeth move on screen, then decide between aligners and ceramic braces.
  • Stage 3 · Active treatment. Trays change weekly; reviews every six weeks, with photo monitoring between them.
  • Stage 4 · Refinements. Two rounds are included. A stubborn tooth costs nothing extra to chase.
  • Stage 5 · Retention. Both retainers plus twelve months of checks are in the quoted price.

Dr. Petrakis starts with the least invasive option that answers your complaint. Bonding is tried before porcelain, and whitening before bonding. You wear a wax mock-up at home for a week and only then do we touch enamel — usually three to five tenths of a millimetre.

  • Stage 1 · Conversation and photographs. We record what bothers you, not what we think should be fixed.
  • Stage 2 · Wax trial. A laboratory model of your new smile, copied into a temporary shell you wear for a week.
  • Stage 3 · Preparation. Shaped under local anaesthetic, scanned optically, no putty trays.
  • Stage 4 · Fit. Hand-layered porcelain, shade-matched in daylight, and a free re-match within twelve months.
