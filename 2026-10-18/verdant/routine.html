02

The method

Why Three Steps Beats Ten

A skin barrier is a wall of cells held together by lipids. Every extra product adds surfactants, solvents and fragrance load that the wall has to process. Most persistent irritation in our clinic inbox traces back to a routine with too many moving parts, not to one bad product.

Three steps are enough to clean the surface, deliver one active that does measurable work, and replace the water and lipids you lost. Everything else is preference — which is fine, as long as you know which is which.

01

Cleanse Once, at Night, for Sixty Seconds

Lukewarm water, a hazelnut of gel, and a full minute of gentle circles. Morning skin does not need cleansing again — a splash of water is enough and saves your barrier.

02

Treat on the Days Your Skin Asks For It

Two drops pressed into damp skin, not rubbed. If your face stings it, skip it for three days. Actives are a habit, not a challenge.

03

Hydrate and Protect Before You Leave

Moisturiser locks in the serum, sunscreen locks in everything. Wait two minutes between them and you will not get pills under makeup.

03

Expectation

What Six Weeks Actually Looks Like

Weeks 1–2

Nothing dramatic. Expect the tight feeling after washing to fade first, usually around day five, as the barrier stops losing water every time you cleanse.

Some people see a small purge in week two. It settles. If it has not settled by day twelve, stop the treatment and write to us.

Weeks 3–4

Tone starts to read as more even because the surface is holding water evenly. This is the point where most people photograph themselves and think nothing has changed. It has.

Do not add a fourth product here. That is the week routines break.

Weeks 5–6

One full cell cycle. Marks from old spots visibly lighter, redness less reactive, and the morning face looks rested rather than processed. Now judge it — and change one thing at a time if you change anything.

04

Before you buy

When Not to Buy Anything From Us

If you have eczema that weeps, a rash that appeared overnight, or an unwelcome change in a mole, a cosmetic will not help and delay will cost you. See a dermatologist first — we say this roughly twice a week in the studio inbox and we mean it.

If you are mid-prescription — tretinoin, isotretinoin, anything that thins the barrier — bring the routine down to cleanse, moisturise, protect, and add nothing else without asking whoever prescribed it.

And if your current three-step routine has been working for a year, keep it. We would rather you spend the money on a good hat than on a bottle you do not need.
