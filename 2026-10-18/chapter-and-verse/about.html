The Shop

Thirty-One Years on Cobbett Lane

We began with one room of second-hand classics above a shoe repair and a rule we still keep: no table of bestsellers near the door.

It Started With a Filing Cabinet

In February 1994 Becky Ellis rented the back room above her father’s shoe repair on Cobbett Lane, put one filing cabinet of catalogued paperbacks by the window, and opened on Saturdays only. She had £340, four hundred books, and a conviction that the town would rather be told what to read than handed a laminated list.

That first year she sold ninety-one books. She remembers the number because she wrote each one on the inside of the counter drawer in pencil, and the drawer is still under the till.

The lease next door came up in 1997 and she took it, then the unit above that in 2004, and then, against every piece of advice from every bank in Bath, the whole ground floor in 2011 when the landlord decided he would rather have flats. The three rooms we have today are the shop’s fourth footprint and its first one where the ceiling does not leak onto the poetry.

A bookseller’s job is not to stock what sells. It is to keep enough room for what will not sell for years. Becky Ellis, founder, from the 1994 opening notice

The second-hand desk came from a house clearance in 2001 — eleven hundred volumes from a retired classics lecturer in Combe Down, most of them annotated, all of them cheaper than the new paperbacks she was trying to sell beside them. That accident became our trade: we now buy around four thousand second-hand books a year and re-shelve roughly three and a half thousand of them.

The Mosaic Wall began in 1996 because a customer taped a quotation to the stair railing and nobody had the heart to remove it. Two hundred and fourteen fragments later it is the only part of the shop we will not let anyone tidy, including us.

The Floorplan

Three Rooms, Each With a Job

Nothing is filed twice and nothing is filed where it cannot be found by the person who shelved it.

Room One — Classics & Second-Hand

Seven thousand titles floor to ceiling, the buy-back desk, and the two cases of books we refuse to stop selling. The long chair by the window is not for sale and is not to be moved.

6,940 titles · est. 1994

Room Two — Poetry, Essays, Letters

Where the readings happen, forty chairs and no microphone. Runs on a ladder we borrowed in 2003 and never gave back; its owner has since died and the ladder has become part of the shop’s furniture.

2,110 titles · est. 2004

Room Three — The Reading Room

Fourteen desks, a hearing loop, the repair bench, and the author events when the room is big enough to hold them. Hired out on Monday evenings to other people’s reading groups.

14 desks · est. 2011

Chronology

Everything That Changed the Shop

  • 1994

    One Room, Saturdays Only

    Becky opens above the shoe repair with a filing cabinet of four hundred catalogued paperbacks and £340.

  • 1996

    The First Fragment

    A customer tapes a Cicero quotation to the stair railing. The Mosaic Wall is born, accidentally, and is never removed.

  • 1997

    Next Door

    The unit beside us comes up. Room One doubles, and the shop takes its first part-time bookseller, Dominic, who is still here on Fridays.

  • 2001

    The Combe Down Clearance

    Eleven hundred annotated classics arrive from a retired lecturer. Second-hand becomes the trade rather than the sideline.

  • 2004

    Upstairs: Poetry

    Room Two opens for poetry, essays and letters. The borrowed ladder arrives with it.

  • 2007

    Circle Four

    Eight readers start meeting in the back room for long Victorian novels. Twenty-eight circles follow within eleven years.

  • 2011

    The Whole Ground Floor

    The landlord plans flats; we take the lease instead, against advice, and Room Three opens in the former stockroom.

  • 2015

    Lamplight

    The Thursday reading night begins with one lamp and no microphone because the sound equipment did not arrive. It stayed that way.

  • 2019

    The Repair Bench

    Priya joins two days a week and starts re-sewing cloth volumes on Mondays. The repair fund pays for itself within a year.

  • 2022

    Loan Copies

    Forty books held free for book club members who cannot buy. Funded entirely by the second Sunday kettle.

  • 2026

    Eleven Thousand Four Hundred

    Our largest stock ever, six nights of events a week, and the pencil drawer under the till still being written in.

Standing Orders

Six Rules We Have Never Broken

  • Popular books are shelved with their genre like everything else. We will tell you where they are; we will not put them in your path. This rule has cost us money in exactly the way everyone warned.

  • If a cheaper reprint is worse — abridged, revised against the author’s last text, or set in a size you cannot read — we say so, even when it loses the sale. Two thirds of those sales come back as a better book a week later.

  • Every staff pick is written by hand on a card and pinned to the shelf edge beside the book, not stacked on a display plinth with a printed header. Cards are replaced monthly and the old ones go on the wall.

  • Sit on the floor if you like. Read a whole poem standing in the aisle. On a busy Saturday Dominic has been known to ask a customer to move along, and he was wrong, and we have never repeated it.

  • A broken shelf copy gets sewn, not scrapped. Anything older than 1900 gets photographed before we touch it, and the photograph goes up on the Mosaic Wall with the customer’s name if they had one.

  • The long chair by the window in Room One exists for readers, not for stock. It has been empty at opening and full by nine for thirty-one years, and it is the reason people come in at all.
