Getting Here

Foot — Six minutes from the Marlowe Square platform, past the fountain and the second set of traffic lights.

Bus — The 94 stops at Marlowe & Fern, thirty metres from our step-free side entrance on the alley.

Bicycle — Two racks under the plane tree; the pavement there is wide enough to lock a cargo bike.

Car — Two hours free on the Fern Street meters after eleven. We cannot promise a space, and the street is narrow on Saturdays.

One form, five reasons

Send Us a Note

Tell us which of these you want, and we will do the rest. Nobody here will send you a marketing email in reply.

  • Two working days is our answer promise; a Tuesday note about an out-of-print title usually comes back Thursday with two prices.
  • No account needed. We keep holds on paper at the counter, in a tray marked with your name.
  • Change your mind freely. Cancel a hold or a chair by replying to the message — one line is enough.

Prefer to hear a human? Ring (617) 555-0148 between 10 and 7, Tuesday to Sunday. Someone who has read the book in question usually picks up.

“The Murasaki book with the purple cover” is a real sentence we receive weekly.

On the pavement, all year

The Free Shelf Outside

Two steps to your left as you leave: a plank bench, a crate, and the chalk rules we wrote in 2020 and never needed to change.

  • Take one, leave one when you can. Nobody watches, and taking two is fine in December.
  • No textbooks, no encyclopaedias. The bench is for things you want to read twice, not revise from.
  • Wiped down Wednesday mornings. Marek rebins the damp paperbacks and we shelve them upstairs at cost.

If you are coming for the bench rather than the shop, Sunday between eleven and noon is the quietest hour to dig.

Answered at the till most days

Questions About the Visit

Can I sit in the shop and read without buying?

Yes, and please do. The window bench and the eight chairs are for exactly that. Saturday until 11:00 is the quietest hour of the week; Thursday after seven is closed to browsing because a club is in the room.

Do you gift wrap, and do you gift cards?

Japanese paper, one fold, burgundy string, free — ask for furoshiki style and Marek will use a cloth you can keep. Gift cards from twenty dollars, valid forever, no address required to buy one.

Will you take a look at my shelves?

Theo does free valuations on Tuesdays, one shoebox at a time. For a whole house library he will visit and charge fifty dollars, which he donates to the school library fund. Write first so he can bring the right book of references.

Can I bring my dog?

The two steps are a nuisance for anything short-legged, but the alley entrance is level and there is a water bowl by the shelf. Dogs who do not chew the paperbacks are welcome; the one that ate a haiku collection in 2019 is known and forgiven.

Do you ship, and how long do holds last?

We ship anywhere in the US at flat five dollars, and free over thirty-five. Holds at the counter last seven days; if you need ten, say so and we will write the later date on the card.
