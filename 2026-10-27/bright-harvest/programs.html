Chapter 02 — How food moves here

Four doors, one kitchen, no waiting list.

Every program below exists because a neighbour asked for something we weren’t doing yet. None of them require an ID, an income statement, or a story you don’t want to tell.

  • No ID required
  • Open choice shelves
  • Nine ZIP codes
  • Three languages

§ 01

Neighbourhood Pantry

Open-choice shelves in Riverside, Moreno Valley and Corona

Walk in, take a basket, choose what your family will actually eat. There is no pre-packed box and no one counting what you put in the bag. Pantry members tell us the difference between a can of soup and a head of cabbage is the difference between surviving and cooking.

Three of our shelves sit inside clinic waiting rooms, so a blood-pressure appointment can end with a bag of potassium-rich food. Two more run after dark for shift workers.

  • Fresh produce, dairy, eggs, bread and protein at every stop
  • Halal and kosher packages available on request, no questions
  • Diabetes-friendly baskets built with the county dietitian
  • Baby formula, purees and toddler snacks restocked weekly
  • Wheelchair-accessible entrances and a seat at every counter

Peak hour is Saturday 4–6pm. If you like a quiet shelf, come Wednesday morning — the produce has just landed and the aisles are yours.

§ 03

School Pantry Backpack

18 campuses · 2,180 children every Friday

A child who eats on Monday and nothing until Tuesday does not learn the same lesson. Our backpacks go into a campus office in an ordinary library bag, so nobody knows which kid is carrying dinner home for the weekend.

Teachers flag the need; our coordinator packs; the principal hands it over. Contents rotate so a family isn’t eating the same macaroni for the ninth week, and every bag carries one fresh thing — an apple, a carrot bunch, a roll of tortillas.

  • Packed to a dietitian’s weekend plan: 5 breakfasts, 5 lunches, 5 dinners
  • Shelf-stable only, because most of these kitchens have a hotplate
  • Summer handoff to the library feeding sites in July
  • Sibling packs added free whenever a family asks once

§ 03

The Mobile Market bus

Route 6: a retired transit bus with a produce bay and a scale that reads in pounds

Nineteen thousand Riverside County residents live more than a mile from a grocer and two miles from a bus stop. We stopped calling that a transportation problem and started calling it a delivery one.

The bus carries 1,400 pounds per run. Everything is priced on the chalkboard — $0.25 a bunch of greens, $1 a pound of tomatoes — and everyone pays in tokens we hand out at the door. Nothing about it feels like charity except the sign, which says Come back Thursday.

Onboard we keep a scale, a stool, a water cooler, and a county eligibility worker four hours a week so people can sign up for CalFresh without missing a day of work.

Bus 6 log, 14 August: “Sold out of peaches in 22 minutes. Add a flat.” — Marisol

§ 04

Kitchen & Bread Line

400 hot meals a night, three bakeries holding loaves back for us

Some food can’t be handed out raw. When a grower’s surplus is past its market moment, our teach kitchen turns it into soup before it turns to compost — 400 portions nightly, plated in the same hall where we hold citizenship clinics on Saturdays.

The kitchen is also a classroom. Every Wednesday evening six neighbours learn to cook a week of dinners for a pantry budget, and take home the ingredients and the printed recipe. Graduates come back as volunteer line cooks; four now work for us.

  • Menu built the morning of, from whatever the trucks bring in
  • Allergen labels in English and Spanish on every steam table pan
  • Bread line at 4:15pm — Millstone, Kearney Flour and Casa Vega loaves
  • Hot meals packaged cold for the 9pm shelter shuttle

§ 05

What’s on the shelf this month

Late October in Riverside County — the tail of the autumn harvest

Fresh

From the rows

Romaine and cavolo nero, carrots still in the dirt, Delicata and sugar squash, Daikon, beets, the last of the grape tomatoes, navel oranges from the Citrus Hill grove.

Picked within 36 hours of landing

Chilled

Dairy & protein

Two-percent milk, yogurt cups, block cheddar, eggs by the half-dozen, chicken thighs frozen at the locker, tinned sardines for the shelves without a fridge.

Cold chain held the whole way

Pantry

Store-carrying

Whole-wheat tortillas, jasmine and short-grain rice, rolled oats, dried beans, pasta sauce, canned tuna, shelf-stable milk, peanut butter, baby purees.

Restocked every Monday

Asked for

What we keep short

Baby formula, gluten-free bread, low-sodium options, halal meat, personal-care items and school supplies. Tell the desk and we’ll add it to the next purchase order.

Named by pantry members, not by us

Ash from the produce room: “People think a food bank is cans. Ask anybody here what we wish we had more of and they’ll say basil, milk and tortillas.”

§ 06

How to get help today

Three ways in

You never need a referral

  • Walk in — 1420 Kearney Street, Mon/Wed/Fri 3–6pm, Sat 9am–1pm.
  • Call or text — (951) 555-0148. A person answers between 7am and 6pm; we call back the rest of the time.
  • Ask a partner — 41 clinics, schools, churches and laundromats carry our shelf card. Any of them can hand you a basket.

Bring nothing. If you’d like to tell us how many people you feed so we can size your box, that helps — but we will also just look at your basket.
