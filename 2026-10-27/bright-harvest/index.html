§ 02

Why we exist

The Central Valley grows enough food to feed this county four times over. Still, 71,000 households within forty minutes of our warehouse choose between the electric bill and a vegetable. The distance is not growth — it is logistics, and logistics is a problem we can solve on a Tuesday.

Bright Harvest began in 1998 in Rev. Darlene Okafor's church lot with one station wagon and a cooler of donated collards. We kept the wagon. We added three refrigerated trucks, a walk-in produce room, and a stubborn rule: dignity first, paperwork never.

  • Fresh produce, dairy and protein — not shelf-stable filler
  • Open-door service with no ID, income test or immigration questions
  • Food sourced within 40 miles wherever the season allows
  • Pantry shelves stocked by the neighbours who shop them

§ 03

One day on the sorting floor

From first pickup to last crate, the clock runs on the produce, not the other way round

  1. 5:15am

    The trucks leave

    Three refrigerated vans roll out to a 14-stop loop: two growers, six grocers, one bakery, one meat locker.

  2. 8:00am

    Weigh-in and quality check

    Every pallet gets weighed, photographed and graded. Anything we would not serve our own families goes to the compost partner across the alley.

  3. 10:30am

    The floor turns

    Volunteers build family boxes by weight class — 12, 20 and 35 pounds — so a household of five leaves with five days of dinner.

  4. 1:00pm

    Pantry run

    Route 6 and Route 9 split east and west, refilling 23 partner shelves before their doors open at 3pm.

  5. 4:45pm

    Open shelves

    Anyone can walk into the Riverside hall, take what feeds their week, and leave without a word about why.

§ 06

Your part in it

Give

$34 feeds a family of four for a week

Your gift buys the cold storage, the diesel and the produce that a food drive can't: fresh greens, milk, eggs and protein. Monthly gifts are what let us commit to a route before the season starts.

  • $18 — a Friday backpack for two kids
  • $34 — one family box, fully fresh
  • $120 — a refrigerated van for a full morning loop

Choose an amount

Serve

Two hours on the floor, and it's done by lunch

Shifts run 7am–12pm and 12pm–5pm, six days a week. No experience, no minimum age with an adult, and no clipboard lecture. You'll sort, weigh, build boxes or ride a route.

  • Sorting floor — indoors, seated work available
  • Route runner — 18+, lifting to 40 lb
  • Teach kitchen — Food Handler card provided

Sign up for a shift

Donating food instead? Unopened, unexpired pantry goods are accepted at the Riverside dock Monday to Saturday, 7am–4pm. Perishables need a scheduled pickup — call (951) 555-0148.
