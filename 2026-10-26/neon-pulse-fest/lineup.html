01.2 Stage Key

What each room actually sounds and feels like, so you can plan the night instead of wandering it.

Pulse Main

Apron, 9,000 capacity, crane lighting rig. Peak-time techno and the headline closes. Loudest room, coldest wind.

Neon Vault

Cold-storage basement, 3,400 capacity, concrete reflections. Hard, dry, deliberately unkind. Queue only, no re-entry after 04:00.

Cyan Warehouse

Bay 7, 5,200 capacity, cyan tube ceiling. Sixteen-act back-to-back chains with no gaps between sets.

Afterglow Pier

Open timber dock, 1,800 capacity, ocean behind you. Ambient, dub and vocals. Phones away past the third piling.

01.3 How The Index Was Programmed

We book in three passes. First the closes — six artists who can hold a harbor for ninety minutes at two in the morning. Then the back-to-back chains, which we assemble by asking who genuinely wants to play beside whom. Finally the openers, drawn from the Los Angeles scene, paid at the same per-minute rate as the headliners.

Forty-eight acts, twenty-two countries, and eleven artists making their California debut. Two sets are unannounced by request: the Friday Vault takeover at 04:00 and the Sunday pier close. Doors reveal them on the night.

Genre tags here are ours, not the artist’s. They exist to help you pick a room, not to argue about music.
