Venue & FAQ

Forty-One Acres, No Surprises

Harlow Yards is a former rail maintenance site: concrete, steel, low ceilings in two rooms and open sky above the rest. Here is every practical thing we know about it.

Site map & zones Twelve questions

Venue

Five buildings, one fence line

The site is a rectangle roughly 700 metres along Tong Road, with the main bay at the west end and the dome at the east. Everything is walkable in four minutes, and the walkways are lit and gritted from Wednesday before gates until Monday clearance.

Getting there

Come by train. It genuinely is faster.

There is no public car parking inside the fence — the yard is a working freight corridor on weekdays and the access roads cannot take 18,000 vehicles. The two Park & Ride sites hold 900 cars between them and are the only legal option within two miles.

Camping

Two thousand pitches on the old loading apron

Camp North Sidings opens at midday Friday and clears by 10:00 Monday for late-checkout holders, 08:00 for everyone else. Pitches are marked at 3×3 metres in blocks of forty, each block with its own water point, waste bin bank and marshalling pair who will remember your face by Saturday lunchtime.

Ground is compacted gravel and it is December, so a groundsheet and a mat with a real R-value matter more than a warm bag. Heated dry rooms at both ends of camp run 24 hours with benches, a boot dryer bank and phone charging rails — the warmest place on site that is not a bar.

Rule 01

No vehicles in camp

Unload at Gate A on Friday, then leave the car at Park & Ride. Blue-badge holders keep their vehicle on pitch — tell the marshal at the gate and they will hold space.

Rule 02

Gas and fires

Small butane stoves are fine, inside a tent footprint only, never in a porch. No paraffin heaters, no braziers, no open flame of any kind on the apron.

Rule 03

Quiet hours

03:30 – 08:00 on the apron itself. Generators are banned outright; speakers go on the stage-side of the fence where they belong.

Rule 04

What we lend

Groundsheets, tarpaulins, blankets and mats, free from the kit store at Gate A with a wristband scan. Thirty sets each, and nobody has ever been turned away.

Rule 05

Breakdowns

If your tent fails at two in the morning, walk to the medical point at camp centre. We keep twelve emergency pods heated and staffed through all three nights.

Rule 06

Leave it as you found it

Take your pitch furniture out on Monday. There is no landfill on site — everything is sorted into six streams by the clearance crew, and pegs get collected for reuse.

Accessibility

Companion passes at half price, and someone who answers the phone

Our access team is the same four people every year, and the box office line is staffed by them rather than a call centre. Ring 0113 496 0026, Monday to Friday 09:00 – 17:00, or email access@neonpulse.fm and expect a reply inside one working day.

  1. Book through the access line

    Claim a weekend tier at 50% with any companion pass at 50%. No evidence needed at booking — we ask, honestly, that you only take it if you need it.

  2. Tell us what you want on site

    Pitch type, step-free stage routes, a space on the BSL-interpreted set list, a low-stimulus viewing deck, or Gate A parking right next to the ramp.

  3. Collect at Gate A

    Access wristbands and the printed step-free route map come from the desk inside Gate A, which is also where escort vehicles drop off and pick up all night.

Step-free routes

Pulse Mainframe, Reactor Dome, Warehouse 9 and the Rooftop are level or lift-served. The Subroom is stairs only; a mirrored viewing window and a duplicate audio feed run into the East Platform at gate level, staffed by the same crew.

Quiet room

A 40-person sensory-rest space in the old tool store, next to Gate B, open 24 hours through the weekend. Dimmable, carpeted, no music, water and ear defenders always stocked, staffed by two welfare volunteers trained in de-escalation.

Hearing & sight

BSL interpreters on fourteen announced sets across the weekend, hearing loops at all five bars and both information desks, and a companion guide-dog relief area at the north-east corner of camp with its own water point.

Medical & changing

Two medical centres (Gate B and camp centre), staffed by NHS paramedics all night. Changing Places facility with hoist and profiling bed beside the Gate A toilets; accessible showers with grab rails in camp blocks 4 and 19.

If something on site is not working

Tell any marshal in a magenta bib, or send a text to 07700 900261. That number is read by the duty access officer every fifteen minutes from Wednesday load-in to Monday clearance — including at 04:00 on Sunday, which is when it gets used most.

Safety

You will get home. That is the whole brief.

Searches at Gate C are quick and conducted by the same team that worked the 2019 warehouse nights: bag check, wristband scan, no drugs, no weapons, no glass. Pyrotechnics on Pulse Mainframe are cold-spark and indoor-rated; the two engineers running them hold the site licence.

Free water at eleven points, free condoms and ear defenders at both information desks, and a peer-led welfare crew in cyan bibs who work the floor rather than sitting behind a desk. If you see someone who has had too much, or who is scared, saying the word PULSE to any staff member brings a welfare pair to you within three minutes.

Lost property is logged at Gate B and photographed onto the dispatch feed each morning at 10:00. Phone charge lockers hold 240 devices; forty of them stay free for anyone who needs to make a call.
