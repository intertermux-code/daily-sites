The brief

A festival built like a mixing desk

Neon Pulse began in 2019 as a 300-capacity warehouse night with a borrowed line array. Seven years on, the obsession is unchanged: one room, one system, one crowd, and nobody checking their phone.

Ground rules & FAQ
“We do not want the biggest room in the north. We want the room where the low end moves your sternum.” — Tobin Vasey, founder & booker

Curation, not stacking

Three quarters of the bill comes from artists who have played the site before or run a label, a studio or a sound system within sixty miles of Leeds. We book sets, not schedules — every act gets at least ninety minutes.

The system

Four engineers from Hull-based Foundry Audio spent eleven days voicing the yard. Pulse Mainframe runs a 24-box flown array with a sub-harmonic rig buried under the stage deck; the Reactor Dome is a full sphere of sixty-eight point sources, delay-tuned to the second. Nothing is limiter-smashed at 4 a.m.

Five rooms

Every stage is a different building

There are no marquees at Harlow Yards. Each room kept its original shell — loading bay, signal box, coal store, cooling hall, and the freight rooftop above them all.

Getting there

Six minutes from the station, zero cars on site

Harlow Yards has no public parking inside the fence — deliberately. Shuttles run continuously from Leeds City, New Station Street, from 15:00 Friday until 08:15 Monday, and the night cost is £4 return. Step-free routes reach every stage; the access guide, BSL interpreters and quiet room are all documented on the info page.
