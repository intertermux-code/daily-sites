Staffing a file

Who actually does the work.

One advisor owns your file start to finish, and a second reads it before any offer goes out. Nobody else in the firm is permitted to call you.

2Readers per offer

Capacity is a rule, not a boast

Each advisor carries a maximum of six active client files at a time. When Marguerite filled hers in March we turned down four buyer engagements and referred them to firms we trust. That decision costs money and is the only reason the phone still gets answered.

The second reader

Before an offer leaves the office, an advisor who has never met the client reads the whole file and marks it up. Three of the last twenty offers changed materially at that stage — one removed a contingency nobody had thought to ask about, one found an unrecorded right-of-way in the title report.

  • Second reader rotates; it is never the listing agent on the same deal
  • Their mark-up is given to you, unedited, with the offer

Back office, in-house

Two people who are not paid per transaction and never will be. They hold the escrow calendar, chase the inspectors, order the reports and keep the bound chronology for every home we have placed since 2004.

  • Rosalind Feeney — Transaction coordinator, nine years
  • Marcus Deveraux — Register & archives, four years

Our standing rule

“Would I buy it?” is asked out loud, in front of you.

Every advisor signs a one-page standard that is older than the website: no fabricated urgency, no borrowed buyers for a listing, no dual representation without a written conversation first. It is pinned to the wall by the printer, and we hand you a copy at the first meeting.
