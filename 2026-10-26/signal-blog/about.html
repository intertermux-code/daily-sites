A Newsroom That Answers Only to Its Readers

Signal has eleven people, no advertisers, no investors with a seat, and one revenue line: what readers pay. That arrangement is the whole editorial policy. Everything else on this page follows from it.

We report on the machinery of computing as physical, expensive, and governed — and we show the documents.

Technology coverage usually chooses between product hype and insider gossip. Signal chooses the third thing: the transformer, the docket, the water permit, the maintenance schedule, the pull request that nobody reviewed. We publish from primary sources wherever a reader could check us, and we say plainly when we cannot confirm something.

Origin

Seven Years, Slowly

Signal started as a mailing list of twelve people reading the same interconnection docket. It stayed small on purpose for three years, and only took money from readers.

  1. 2019

    A newsletter for eleven subscribers

    Marguerite Vale and Cormac Fyfe begin sending a monthly note about grid filings to anyone who asks. The first issue is 900 words and has one chart, drawn by hand.

  2. 2020

    The first paid plan

    Readers ask to pay. A $60 annual rate is set by counting what it cost to buy a transformer dataset and dividing by the people already reading. 340 subscribers by December.

  3. 2022

    Standards written down

    After a correction on a cable-landing story, the desk publishes its first standards document, including the rule that any figure we derive from public filings gets the filing number in the caption.

  4. 2024

    Four cities, one rota

    Correspondents in Berlin, Lagos, Stockholm and Tokyo join. The desk agrees to publish a longread every second Thursday and never more than four files a week, so nothing ships unrepaired.

  5. 2026

    Issue 42 and an open budget

    41,200 readers fund a staff of eleven. The March operating statement shows 92 per cent of revenue from subscriptions, 8 per cent from print issues, and nothing from anywhere else.

Editorial standards

The Five Rules Every File Clears

Nothing here is aspirational. Each rule is checked by Ilse Brandt before publication, and each one has stopped a story at some point.

Rule 01

Primary documents first

Every factual claim traces to a filing, dataset, recording, or on-site observation. We cite the document number in the caption, not in a footnote we hope nobody reads.

See how we file

Rule 02

Two sources or a named limitation

Anything we could not corroborate goes in the story with the words “we could not confirm” attached. No attribution games, no mystery executives without a described role.

See how we file

Rule 03

No advertising, ever

There is no sponsored slot, no affiliate link, no “brought to you by.” If a company pays Signal money it is for a print subscription on the same terms as a reader's.

See the funding

Rule 04

Corrections in place, dated

We amend the text where it was wrong, mark what changed, and keep the log public. A silent edit is treated as a standards violation, not housekeeping.

Read the log

Rule 05

Accessible by default

Keyboard-operable, tested with a screen reader, colour-contrast checked, and never dependent on motion to convey meaning. Every redesign ships with an audit attached.
