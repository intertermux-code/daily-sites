What arrives

Inside Every Thursday Letter

Roughly 2,400 words, one chart, one document. Nothing else. Delivery is plain-text-first, so it renders the same in a terminal as in a mail client.

  1. The file of the week

    Our longread, with the reporting trail: which dockets, which interviews we could name, and which claims we still cannot stand behind.

  2. Desk notes, four or five

    Short observations from individual editors, including the ones that did not become stories yet and the reason they have not.

  3. The number

    One figure worth arguing about, with the method underneath it in two sentences so you can check it yourself.

  4. Corrections, if any

    We publish changes in the letter rather than editing quietly. In 2026 that has happened six times, and we have meant every one.

Before You Sign Up

Seven honest answers

How often do you actually email me?

Once a week, Thursday at 06:00 Eastern, plus a separate note only when we publish a correction to something already sent or when an issue ships early. That is four to seven additional emails in a typical year.

Is the free letter really free?

Yes. Every story on this site is readable without an account and without a paywall interstitial. The subscription tiers pay for the reporting, not for access.

What happens to my email address?

It lives in our delivery system with a preference record and a delivery log. We do not share it, sell it, or hand it to analytics vendors, and we do not open-rate individual readers. Reply unsubscribe and the record is deleted within a day.

Can I choose only some desks?

The letter always carries the week's main file and the desk notes, but the topics you tick decide what we surface in the "watching" column and whether we send you the beat briefing when one of those desks runs a series.

Do you accept press trips, gifts, or review hardware?

No paid travel, no gifts above $25 in value, and equipment we keep is disclosed in the story. Companies may supply documentation, which we treat as a source with an interest.

Will you give my details to a campaign?

Never, and we do not run third-party ad slots in the letter. When we ask readers to help — a subscription drive, a fundraising goal for a reporting project — it is our own copy from our own address.

What if the form rejects my address?

It checks for a plausible local part, a domain, and a two-letter-or-longer suffix. If your company mail uses an unusual internal domain and we flag it, write to desk@signal.press and we will add you by hand.
