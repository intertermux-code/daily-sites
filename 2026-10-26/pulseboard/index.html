01

Arrive

Your events land exactly as your engineers already emit them. Pulseboard reads the warehouse tables and streams you already have, then labels each field against your existing taxonomy. Nothing gets re-instrumented, and nothing gets copied into a second system of record.

  • Snowflake, BigQuery, Postgres, Redshift
  • Kafka and Kinesis tails for real-time
  • Column-level mapping preview before save

resolved

02

Define

A metric is written once, in plain language, with its filters, its exclusion rules, and its owner attached. Version history shows who changed it and which board was using it that week — so a definition argument ends with a link instead of a meeting.

  • Git-style diffs on every metric change
  • Active, deprecated, and draft states
  • Named owner and review date on each one

metric weekly_activation {
  when  event = "key_action_done"
  window 7 days rolling
  exclude internal_workspaces
  owner  @dana

03

Arrange

Boards are composed the way a story is told: the headline number at the top, the drivers underneath, the caveats in the margins. Drag a segment in and every chart on the board follows it, because they share one definition rather than one query.

  • Layouts for TV, laptop, and reading
  • One filter propagates to all charts
  • Comment threads pinned to a data point

04

Answer

Pulseboard watches the board while you are not in the room. Thresholds, week-over-week drift, and missing partitions each have their own alert route — Slack, email, or a webhook into the pager you already run. Every alert carries the board link, so replies start from the same picture.

  • Alert rules inherit metric definitions
  • Anomaly sensitivity you can dial, not guess
  • Weekly digest of what moved and what you decided

/01

One definition, every surface

Write “active account” once. The board, the alert, the API response, and the weekly email all quote the same line of versioned truth — including who owns it and when it was last reviewed.

  • Diff history per metric
  • Deprecation warnings in-place
  • Definitions exported as YAML

/02

Answers at p95, not at “soon”

Pre-aggregated rollups sit in front of your warehouse, so a nine-quarter trend opens in under two seconds on a bad network. Heavy scans run in the background and page you when the result is ready.

  • Incremental materialisation
  • Query budget per workspace
  • Cache warmth you can inspect

/03

Reviews that end with a decision

Every board keeps a decision log: what moved, who raised it, what the team chose, and which metric proves it worked. Next Monday starts from last Monday’s outcome instead of last Monday’s screenshot.

  • Notes pinned to data points
  • Owner and due date on each action
  • Automatic before/after annotation
