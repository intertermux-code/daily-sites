Features, in detail

Three load-bearing ideas, built properly.

Most analytics tools offer an endless list of widgets. Pulseboard offers a smaller set of things that decide whether your numbers can be trusted: one definition per metric, queries fast enough to answer live in the room, and a record of what your team actually did about it.

Reading time
About six minutes, with the spec panels.
Who builds this
Analytics engineers, growth teams, finance analysts.

Feature 01

One definition, everywhere it is quoted

The metric is the source of truth. Charts are simply views of it.

Write weekly activation once. Attach the window, the internal-workspace exclusion, the owner, and the review date. Every board, alert, export, and API response that quotes the metric reads from that single line — so a change is felt consistently, and instantly traceable.

Definitions are versioned like code. Deprecating a metric does not delete a chart; it annotates every consumer with the replacement suggestion and the date the old rule stopped being maintained. When an executive asks why March looks different from April, the answer is a diff, not a recollection.

Plain-language rules

Filters read as sentences your non-technical colleagues can confirm in a review.

Impact preview

Before saving an edit, see the 14 boards and 6 alerts that will change and by how much.

Review cadence

Each metric carries a named owner and a next-review date; stale ones surface themselves.

Portable as YAML

Export the whole model, keep it in your repository, and diff it in a pull request.

Specification details

Your warehouse tables are mapped once into an entity graph: accounts, users, workspaces, and the events that pass between them. A metric is expressed against that graph, not against a SQL snippet, which is why the same definition survives a schema migration and can be reused across boards without being re-typed.

  • Column mapping is previewed and versioned before it is saved
  • Joins are declared once as relationships, then reused everywhere
  • Backfill runs automatically when a new event property appears

Edits to a metric that is referenced by an alert or a subscribed board require a second reviewer. Both the before and after values are computed for the last 90 days, so you can see that the change moves trial conversion by 0.4 points before anybody on Monday’s call finds out by accident.

  • Dual approval on alert-referenced metrics
  • Side-by-side recompute over a chosen history window
  • Automatic notice to every board subscriber

Boards can be published to a read-only link, embedded in your internal wiki, or pushed as a weekly digest. Every view carries the definition link in its footer, so a screenshot taken out of context still tells the reader which rule produced the number.

  • Read-only links with expiry and watermarking
  • Embed blocks for Notion, Confluence, and Google Slides
  • CSV export includes the definition text in a header row

Feature 02

Answers in the room, not after it

p95 board render is 1.9 seconds. That is a product requirement, not a marketing figure.

Rollups are materialised incrementally as events arrive, so a trend spanning nine quarters reads from a small, warm table rather than scanning your fact table for the fourth time that morning. When someone asks an unusual question that needs a heavier scan, Pulseboard runs it in the background, keeps the board interactive, and pings the person who asked when the answer is ready.

Because speed is a property of the model, not of the machine, the same board that opens in two seconds for a four-person startup opens in two seconds for a 900-seat platform team. Each workspace gets a visible query budget, and cache warmth is inspectable rather than hidden behind a spinner.

Incremental materialisation

New partitions extend existing rollups instead of rebuilding the history.

Background for heavy asks

Long scans never block the board you are already presenting.

Budget you can see

Warehouse cost per team and per board, reported back to you weekly.

No sampling, ever

Every event is counted; if a figure is estimated, the label says so in words.

Performance details

Roughly 240 milliseconds is browser render and font layout, 380 milliseconds is the rollup read, and the remainder is network from the browser to the region nearest your team. We measure it from real sessions, not from our own instrumentation, and publish the p50, p95, and p99 in the status page every month.

  • p50 0.7s · p95 1.9s · p99 3.4s over the last 90 days
  • Charts stream in as each query resolves, not all at once
  • Static board snapshots for presentations, generated on demand

Each metric declares the freshness it needs. Launch-day counters run on the streaming tail with about eight seconds of lag; retention cohorts refresh nightly with the warehouse; finance-facing measures refresh on the close calendar you set. A board shows the as-of time on every number, so nobody mistakes a nightly figure for a live one.

  • Streaming tail: 8s median lag on active metrics
  • Nightly build: reconciles against warehouse partitions
  • Explicit as-of stamps and missing-partition alerts

Feature 03

A board that remembers what you decided

Analytics ends at a number. Pulseboard ends at an outcome, with a name attached.

Comment threads pin to a data point rather than to a page, so a discussion from March still sits exactly where it happened. Close a thread with a decision and Pulseboard records the owner, the intended effect, and the metric that will prove it. Two weeks later it annotates the chart itself: “Trial re-engage experiment shipped here — activation +6%.”

The weekly digest is written from that log, not from a chart dump: what moved, what your team chose, and what has not been followed up. It is the difference between a dashboard people look at and a practice people keep.

Point-anchored threads

Context stays glued to the week and segment it was about.

Outcome annotation

Shipped changes appear on the timeline automatically, dated.

Follow-up ledger

Open decisions with owners and due dates, visible to the whole team.

Written digest

A short prose summary of the week, edited by you before it is sent.

Review workflow details

Presentation mode walks the board in the order you arrange, one exhibit at a time, and keeps the discussion in the side panel. Anyone joining late reads what happened in the room instead of asking you to repeat it, and the notes become next week’s agenda without a separate document.

  • Guided order with keyboard navigation and a presenter timer
  • Decisions recorded with owner, due date, and success metric
  • Agenda carried forward automatically from the last review

Alerts inherit the metric they watch, so a threshold always means the same thing as the number on the board. Sensitivity is a dial you can justify, weekend seasonality is learned rather than assumed, and every alert carries the board link plus the last decision that touched that measure.

  • Threshold, drift, and absence-of-data rules per metric
  • Routes to Slack, email, or a webhook into your pager
  • Snooze with a reason, reviewed in the weekly digest

Workspace, board, and metric each have their own permission, and row-level rules can hide a region or a customer group without duplicating the definition. Every read is logged with the person, the board, and the filters used, which is what makes an audit a five-minute task instead of a quarter of memory work.

  • SSO with SCIM provisioning on Scale plans
  • Row-level rules per team, region, or account tier
  • Readable audit log, exportable for compliance review

04Plugs in quietly

It sits in front of the stack you already paid for.

Pulseboard does not ask you to move your events, replace your warehouse, or re-instrument your app. Connections are read-only by default and every mapping is previewed before it is saved.

Warehouses

Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Postgres

Connect with a read-only role. Column mapping is previewed, then versioned, so a schema change is a reviewable diff instead of a broken chart at 8am.

Streams

Kafka, Kinesis, and your own tail

Subscribe to a copy of the stream for live measures. If the tail falls behind, the board says so on the number rather than quietly showing stale truth.

Surfaces

Slack, email, webhooks, embeds

Meetings happen where work happens. Alerts, digests, and board links route into Slack or your wiki, and everything is readable on a phone.
