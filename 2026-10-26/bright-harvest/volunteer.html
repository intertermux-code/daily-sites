Volunteer

Two hours, closed-toe shoes, COFFEE included.

140 people a day work the dock, the vans and the market aisles. Most of them came for one Saturday and never fully left. No experience, no minimum age with a guardian, no commitment beyond the shift you sign up for.

Shifts from 2 hours Groups & corporate days welcome 1,860 volunteers last year 418,000 hours logged

Six jobs that need hands

Where you can put yourself to work

Pick the one that sounds like a good Saturday. Every role gets a fifteen-minute station briefing; nobody is handed a box and left guessing.

Route driver

CDL not required — a standard license and a clean record get you in a refrigerated van by 5:15 am. You collect what grocers flag and bring it in cold. Best-paid in gratitude, worst-paid in sleep.

Tue–Sat · 5:15–9:00 am · 18 open

Sort & inspect

The floor where the rescue grade becomes dinner. You taste-check, date-check, weigh and crate. Seated stations available, and this is where most first-timers start.

Daily · 9:00–11:30 am · 42 open

Market aisle host

On Mobile Market days you stock cases, keep the produce looking like a store, and help a shopper decide between two kinds of squash. Standing, social, loud in a good way.

Thu–Sat · 2:30–6:00 pm · 20 open

Kitchen prep & dish

Wash, chop, set, cook, clean. You work next to a chef who grew up three blocks away, and you eat what you made. Food handler card provided on site for free.

Wed & Fri · 10:00 am–1:00 pm · 9 open

Site runner, your block

One of the 34 partner sites needs two people to unload a pallet and open the doors. You stay in your own neighborhood, and you will recognize most of the faces by week three.

Varies · 2 hr windows · 27 open

Backpack packer

Friday afternoons in summer, forty bags an hour, a radio on. Perfect for kids old enough to count, and the only role where we allow music and a dance break.

Fri · 2:00–5:00 pm · 15 open

Bring this

  • Closed-toe shoes, non-negotiable on the dock.
  • A refillable water bottle — we stock the cooler.
  • Hair tied back for kitchen stations.
  • Your phone, so the shift reminder can find you.

Do not bring

  • Food from home. Our intake rules are strict for a reason.
  • A guilt trip about not being able to lift 40 lbs — half our stations are seated.
  • Expectations that this is quiet. It is a warehouse at 9 am.

Access & inclusion

  • Step-free entry at the north door, paved path throughout.
  • Quiet station available on request, no explanation needed.
  • Spanish, Vietnamese and Tagalog floor leads every day.
  • Service animals always welcome; ear plugs at the desk.

What happens after you sign up

Your first four weeks, mapped

No orientation video, no six-week onboarding. You work beside someone who has done it, and you learn the way anybody does.

  2. 1
    Within 24 hours

    A real person emails you back

    Not an autoresponder — Priya or Marcus, with your shift, the north-door map and one honest sentence about what the job involves. Reply to that email to change or cancel.

  3. 2
    First shift

    Fifteen minutes at the desk, then to work

    Sign the safety sheet, get gloves and a name tag with no last name on it, and meet your floor lead. You will not be handed a pallet and left alone; somebody stays with you the whole window.

  4. 3
    Week two

    Pick a second thing, or the same one

    Most people try the floor first and fall for the Market aisles. Say the word and we will move you. This is also when the driver list opens up if you want more responsibility.

  5. 4
    Week four

    You get the schedule and the gossip

    Regulars see next month’s routes early and get asked to lead a station. Three of our six floor leads started as a single Tuesday morning, which is the entire sales pitch.
