The route

From Soil to Supper, in Five Moves

A head of lettuce has about ninety minutes of value left when a grocer pulls it from the shelf. Here is how we spend that time.

  2. 01
    05:40 — dawn

    A farm or grocer flags the surplus

    Partners tap one button in the Bright Harvest app: 240 lbs of seconds-grade tomatoes, eight cases of day-old sourdough, a pallet of romaine that missed a contract. Our dispatcher sees it before the coffee is done.

    Meet the recovery network
  3. 02
    06:15 — cold chain

    Twelve refrigerated vans roll out

    Nothing travels in a hot trunk. Our vans hold 38°F from pickup to dock, and every crate gets a temperature log scanned at both ends. Two of our drivers started as volunteers on this exact route.

  4. 03
    09:00 — the hub

    Volunteers inspect, weigh and sort

    Inside the old Riverside cannery, 140 volunteers a day run taste, date and damage checks. Anything we would not serve our own kid goes to the compost partner instead. Everything that passes gets weighed — that number becomes next year’s report.

  5. 04
    13:30 — 34 sites

    Food moves to where people already are

    School parking lots, a barbershop, two clinics, a laundromat, a mosque, a fire station. Neighbors pick up a week of groceries in under ten minutes, no paperwork, no questions, no photos for our newsletter.

    Read what that feels like
  6. 05
    17:00 — supper

    Somebody cooks a real dinner

    The last stop is a kitchen table. Community Kitchen classes turn the odd-looking produce into meals families repeat at home, and our Kids’ Harvest bags go back in backpacks for the weekend. That is the whole loop.

Update from the dock

What the shelves are short on this week

We buy to fill the gaps that gifting cannot cover — dairy, protein and baby food are the three lines where donations beat donations-in-kind every single time.

  • Low-fat milk and eggs — 40% of a weekly need, almost never rescued.
  • Canned tuna and chicken — 3,100 cans to get back to even.
  • Infant formula, stage 1 and 2 — 214 families on the list.
  • Cultured yogurt — the item our Food Pharmacy doctors ask for most.
