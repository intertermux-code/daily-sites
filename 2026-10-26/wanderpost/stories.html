Sixty-one of our last eighty departures sent something back

The post comes back.

At the end of every journey we ask travellers for one paragraph: what they would keep, what they would cut, and the small thing nobody could have planned. We add it to the guide with their name on it. These are five of them.

01 — Five journeys, in the travellers’ words

Kyoto · Deep Autumn · November 2025

Day four had nothing in it. We were meant to be somewhere. Instead Ilse left us on the engawa with tea and shut the door, and by four o’clock I had stopped taking photographs of it — which had been the entire point.

The thing I would tell the next person: the early starts are not a punishment. We were at Nanzen-ji at seven with eleven people and I have never seen a Japanese garden empty before. After that I did not mind that the afternoon was ours.

Marguerite Oyelaran · Lagos and London · travelled with her sister

Patagonia · The Long Wind · November 2025

Fourteen nights in one valley sounded like a mistake on the phone. By day nine the staff knew which chair I sat in. I stopped being a visitor somewhere around the second horse morning.

We had the wind taken away from us twice and each time Ezequiel simply moved the plan — a boat one day instead of the towers, a long lunch instead of a walk. Nobody complained once, and on day eleven the towers appeared for two clear hours.

Tomás Ferreira · Lisbon · solo traveller, room 6

Marrakech · Slow Medina · March 2026

The briefing on the first afternoon changed the whole week. Not “ignore the men in the square” but how to greet them, how to refuse a tea, how to be rude kindly. Once I knew the words, the city stopped trying to sell me things and started talking to me.

Three nights up in Aït Bouguemez was the part I would keep if I could only have one. Bread at six in the morning, then nothing, then Abdellah’s walk with the shade mapped out like a general.

Hannah Lindqvist · Malmö · travelled with her father

Lofoten · Blue Hour · January 2026

Nine hours of blue, no noon, and I have never been less bothered. On the fourth night Einar knocked on the cabin door at half eleven with two flasks and said come quickly, and that was the whole holiday in one sentence.

Would cut: nothing. Would keep: the sauna at Hamnøy and the fact that the schedule never once said “early start” — because it was dark anyway and you got up when the light told you to.

Ruth Nakamura · Vancouver · tenth Wanderpost journey for her husband

Kyoto · Deep Autumn · October 2025

I asked them, in writing, to take the temples out. Half of them. So we had one a day and a mountain and a bath and a calligrapher, and I came back knowing the name of a street rather than a list of buildings.

The edit was easy. They sent a draft with the gaps marked in pencil and my only change was to ask for a third night in Ōhara. They had already held one, in case.

Alice Boateng · Accra and Leeds · travelled with two friends

03 — From the planning desk

Three notes we wrote to ourselves this season.

14 October · Ōhara

The colour line is moving earlier

Second year running the valley peaked six days ahead of the forecast. We have started holding the late-October window longer and moving the city days later, which is why our November departures now finish on the mountain rather than beginning on it.

2 November · Kōyasan

Five o’clock bells, eight rooms

Kongōsan-in gave us the eastern rooms for 2026, which face the graves rather than the car park. Ask for them by name when you book — or leave it to us, and we will already have written it down.

19 January · Reine

Nine departures in eleven saw it

Our aurora hit rate for the current winter, counting only nights someone was awake for. It is not a promise and we will never sell it as one; it is a reason to keep the thermoses in the boot.
