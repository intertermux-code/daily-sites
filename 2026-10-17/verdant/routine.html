02 / Method

How to Apply It,
in the Order That Works

Thinnest to thickest, always. Water-based first, oils last — an oil in front of a serum blocks it out.

TimeStepProductsDurationOrder

Cleanser for thirty seconds on damp skin, then pat — never rub — to towel-dry. Two spritzes of mist into damp hands and pressed over the face. Moisturiser the size of a5 coin, and sunscreen last if you are going out within the hour.

The whole thing should take less time than the kettle takes to boil.

If you wear sunscreen or makeup, cleanse twice — the first pass dissolves it, the second cleans the skin. Three drops of serum on a dry face, pressed not dragged, then the balm while the serum is still tacky. Two drops of oil last, only if your skin still feels tight ten minutes later.

Night is where the barrier does its work. Skipping the seal step undoes the treatment step.

A layer thick enough that you cannot see your skin through it, over the T-zone only, off at eight minutes — before it cracks. Take it off with a warm muslin cloth rather than paper towels, and moisturise within sixty seconds while the skin is still damp.

03 / Questions

Asked Before
You Asked It

The five that reach the studio inbox most often.

Hydration changes within a week — you will feel it. Congestion and tone take a full skin cycle, roughly twenty-eight days, and pigmentation takes two. Anyone promising a result in three nights is selling you a temporary plumping effect, not a formula.

Change one product at a time and wait six weeks before judging it. If you introduce four on the same evening and something stings, you will not know which one to blame.

The Cleanser, Mist, Oat Milk Moisturiser, Night Balm and Face Oil are all built to sit alongside tretinoin or adapalene — no acids, no fragrance, no essential oils above 0.3%. Skip the Clear Serum on retinoid nights; niacinamide at 5% is generally fine but there is no reason to layer actives when your barrier is already working.

The Weekly Mask is the one to pause for the first fortnight of a new retinoid.

Cleanser, Mist, Serum, Night Balm, Moisturiser, Eye Concentrate and Face Oil are all fine. The Weekly Mask contains willow bark — a natural salicylate — and while the systemic exposure from an eight-minute mask is negligible, we would rather you ask your midwife than ask us.

Nothing in the line contains retinoids, hydroquinone or salicylic acid at leave-on levels.

Every product has to earn its place against the eight already there. A toner with an active, a night cream, an essence, a booster — each one would overlap with something you already own, and overlap is what makes a routine abandoned by March.

We have retired two products and never added a ninth. That is not modesty, it is arithmetic: the line covers cleanse, prep, treat, seal and one weekly reset.

Write to the studio within thirty days and we refund the full price, whether the bottle is half-used or full. We do not ask you to mail back an opened product you reacted to — keep it, bin it, whichever you prefer.

About one order in forty comes back, most often a fragrance sensitivity to the cedar mist. If you tell us what happened, the next recommendation is better for it.
