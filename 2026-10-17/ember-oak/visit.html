05 Visit · Old Town, Portland

Look for the smoke and the green door.

212 Kiln Street sits between the sawmill and the river, in a building that was a brick kiln until 1961 and a furniture warehouse until we bought it. The entrance is on the east side — there is no sign, just a green door and a lamp.

Sunday family fire

One seating at 4:30 pm, everything served in the middle of the table, no individual mains. Cheaper than the weeknight card and the room is full of children. Book ahead.

Closed weeks

We shut for one week in January and one in August so the kitchen can go and eat somewhere else’s fire. Both dates are posted on the reservation page.

Holidays

Open on Thanksgiving Eve and closed Thanksgiving Day itself. Christmas Eve is a set menu, New Year’s is two seatings with a late bar.

Smoke closure

In August and September, if the Air Quality Index goes above 150 we close the fire and the room. We post it on the door and email anyone holding a booking that week.

03 Getting here

Come by streetcar. Park once you have eaten.

Kiln Street is one-way eastbound between N Front and N 2nd. Drop-off is on the north side of the door.

  • AStreetcar  — Line A to Kiln Street / Old Town, two minutes on foot. Runs every 12 minutes until 11:30 pm on weekends.
  • MAXMAX Red / Blue  — Get off at Old Town/Transit Mall, walk four blocks north on N Front Avenue.
  • BUSLine 4 & 44  — Both stop at N Portland & Kiln, one block from the green door.
  • BIKEBike parking  — Six racks on the north side of the building, under the awning and visible from the counter stools.
  • CARParking  — The free lot behind the sawmill at 301 Kiln Street, forty spaces, open until 1:00 am. Street meters on N Front are free after 8:00 pm.
  • RIDERideshare  — Set the pin to “Kiln Street between Front and 2nd”. Drivers get lost on the one-way; we will meet you at the car if you call.
  • PDXFrom the airport  — MAX Red direct to Old Town, 38 minutes, then a four-block walk. The last train back leaves at 12:34 am.

04 Access, comfort and the fine print

An old warehouse has charming problems. Here is what to expect.

If something on this list will make your evening difficult, tell us when you book and we will move things around.

Wheelchair access

Step-free entrance on the north side via the ramp at 301 Kiln. Twelve tables in the hearth room and both counter stools at the ends are 34-inch clear. The cellar table is down fourteen steps and has no lift — we can run the same menu upstairs in the corner booth instead.

Noise & light

The room is brick, timber and glass, so it carries sound. After 8:00 pm we lower the lights and turn the music down. Hearing-protection plugs are at the host stand, no question asked.

Allergies & diets

A full vegetarian and vegan menu exists. Gluten-free bread is baked in-house. Shellfish, nuts and wheat are handled on the same grate, so we cannot promise a separate cooking surface — we can promise the chef will cook your plate first, on clean tools, every time.

Children & dogs

Children are welcome until 8:00 pm; we keep a basket of pencils and a bread dough to shape. Well-behaved dogs are allowed in the entry corridor and at the two sidewalk tables, water bowls out.

Restrooms

Two all-gender rooms and one accessible room off the main stair, plus a changing bench. Key needed for the accessible lock — the host has one.

Payment

Card, Apple Pay, cash, and gift cards. No card is required for parties of six or fewer. A 20 per cent service charge is added for seven or more and shared across the whole team.

06 Asked at the door

Six questions we answer every week.

Do you take walk-ins?

Yes — six of the twelve counter stools are held for walk-ins from 5:00 pm, and the corridor bar serves the full menu until close. On a Friday the counter is usually gone by 5:40.

Is there a dress code?

No. It is a fire restaurant, and smoke is part of the deal, so think about the coat you want to come home in. People wear suits and people wear boots. Both are correct.

Can I watch the fire from the counter?

The counter faces the grate directly and is the only seat in the building where you can see the coals being raked. If you want to be cooked for rather than cook-adjacent, take the hearth room.

How long will we be there?

Two hours for a two-course dinner, closer to three if you start at the board and finish with the ice cream sandwich. We run one seating, so nobody asks you to leave.

Is the menu the same every night?

The spine holds — the ribeye, the carrots, the board, the custard. The edges move weekly with the market, and occasionally a dish disappears mid-service because we ran out of it.

Can you smoke my fish or cook something I brought?

Genuinely, yes, within reason. Ask Gerald at the bar before 6:00 pm. There is an $18 preparation charge and we will not touch anything raw that has been sitting in a car.
