Mission

We Report the Machinery, Not the Message

Technology is now physical: cable, glass, copper, transformers, water, land titles and shipping lanes. Most coverage stops at the announcement. Ours starts there and follows the paperwork until it hits a wall.

We were founded in 2014 by four reporters who had been asked, in three separate newsrooms, to stop filing infrastructure stories because they were “not enterprise.” Twelve years later the enterprise is a 46-gigawatt interconnection queue, and we are still the only publication in this country that treats a permit as a newsmaker.

Signal publishes on the open web for everyone. Members pay because the reporting is useful, not because it is locked. If our work is wrong, we say so at the top of the story, with a date.

What we will not do

  • Accept money from a company in exchange for coverage, a quote, or a review copy retained past the review period.
  • Run sponsored content, affiliate product roundups, or branded “insights” from a PR firm.
  • Use a generative model to draft, summarise or headline a news story. Tools see copyedit help only, and we disclose it.
  • Publish a claim on one source, or a document we have not authenticated against a second.
  • Offer anonymity to a source whose only purpose is to settle a score.

“A newsroom that cannot tell you who wrote the story, who edited it, and who got it wrong, is not a newsroom. It is a distribution channel.”

Marcus Vance, Editor-in-Chief, from the 2020 member letter

Standards & Ethics

Corrections log →
Sourcing

Two Documents or Two People

Nothing publishes on a single source. Anonymous sources require an editor's sign-off, a stated reason for the protection, and a description of the motive we are accepting on their behalf — which appears in the story, not only in our notes.

Corrections

Every Error, Printed and Dated

A material error is corrected at the top of the affected story, dated, and logged below. We do not silently edit. We do not “update for clarity” in a way that changes a conclusion, and we say what changed.

Conflicts

Holdings Declared in the Byline

Staff and contributors disclose any equity, advisory role or paid speaking in a company they cover, in a standing box under the byline. Family employment gets the same treatment. Signal reporters do not take trips, meals above $40, or hardware they keep.

Machine assistance

No Generated Reporting

Models may help us transcribe, search a corpus, or check a spelling. They do not write, summarise or headline news copy. Any story that used a model for a non-trivial step says so in the reporting note, and we publish the prompt on request.

Twelve Years

How the Paper Got Here

Four reporters, one subscription drive, and a stubborn bet that the physical layer of the technology industry was the story nobody else would have the patience for.

  1. 2014 · Issue 001

    Four reporters and $84,400

    Alicia Grant, Marcus Vance, D. Nakamura and R. Osei leave a regional daily and raise a subscription drive in nine weeks. The first issue is eleven pages and goes to 1,902 readers.

  2. 2016 · The fiber beat opens

    Someone has to cover conduit

    Elena Sokolova joins to run physical networks. Within a year the desk is reading county bond filings, which is still the best three hours of any Signal reporter's week.

  3. 2018 · First national citation

    A transformer story reaches a docket

    Our reporting on equipment lead times is entered into a state utility commission proceeding. The commission's order cites the piece, the underlying dataset, and one chart we had to fix twice.

  4. 2020 · Conversion

    The newsroom buys itself

    Staff convert Signal to a worker cooperative, buying out the two angel holdings over fourteen months. The standards page is rewritten the same week and has been amended three times since.

  5. 2022 · The wire goes daily

    6:14 a.m. Pacific, every weekday

    The Daily Wire becomes a full workday commitment rather than a Tuesday habit. Readership doubles in eight months; so does the correction queue, which we consider fair.

  6. 2024 · Models on the desk

    We automate ourselves, then grade it

    A six-week experiment lets a model draft 4,000 earnings summaries. It published 61, was wrong twice in ways a human would not have been, and the postmortem remains our most-read story.

  7. 2026 · Issue 214

    312,400 readers, 21,447 members

    Twelve staff, nineteen indexed beats, one open request board, and the same rule we started with: the reporting stays on the open web.
