The Last Mile Is a Fiber Run

Three towns in eastern Oregon spent four years and $11.4 million building the network their incumbents would not. What they found at the bottom of the trench became a template nobody in the boardrooms agreed to copy.

Elena Sokolova · Infrastructure Correspondent · Published Oct. 14, 2026

Pendleton, Umatilla and Milton-Freewater share a watershed, a school district bus route and, until this spring, nothing else. What they did not share was a fiber path. The three cities sit forty minutes from the nearest major interchange, which is roughly the distance an incumbent will drive to reach a customer and slightly farther than it will drive to reach a promise.

In the winter of 2022 the county offered them a grant with a condition: spend it jointly or lose it. They chose jointly, hired a civil engineer who had never laid a strand of glass, and learned the two facts that govern every project of this kind — that conduit is cheap and crossings are not, and that the hardest part of a network is never the network.
