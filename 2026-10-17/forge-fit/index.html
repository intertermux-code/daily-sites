§ 01The Foundry

We Built the Gym We Wanted at 6 a.m.

Forge started in 2014 in a former steel workshop with two racks, a stack of borrowed plates and eleven members. Twelve years on we hold the same rule: the barbell is the program, not the warm-up for something else.

Every athlete who joins gets a measured baseline — squat, hinge, press, pull, engine — then a written block plan they can read from any screen in the building. Whiteboards are photographed and emailed nightly, because the best training logbook is the one you actually open.

You will not find a juice bar, a tanning bed, or a sales floor here. You will find calibrated plates, competition racks, monotone flooring that grips shoes, and coaches who count your rest periods.

Note. Peak hour is 06:00–07:30 and 17:30–19:00. Outside those windows the floor holds 40+ athletes without a queue for a rack.

§ 02Programs

Three Ways to Train at Forge

Pick one lane or run two side by side. Each program has its own session template, its own strength standards, and its own place on the weekly schedule.

02.1Squash · Bench · Deadlift

Powerlifting

Twelve-week blocks built around the competition lifts. Submaximal volume, planned attempts, and a meet-day simulation before you ever book a platform at a real meet.

4 sessions · 75 minRead the block
02.2Engine · Intervals · RPE

HIIT & Engine

Twenty-to-forty-minute intervals on sleds, ergs and kettlebells, prescribed by heart-rate zone rather than by how loud the room gets. Rest is coached, not improvised.

5 sessions · 45 minRead the block
02.3Range · Tissue · Longevity

Mobility & Repair

Loaded range work for lifters whose hips and shoulders have gone quiet. Thirty-five minutes, barefoot, ending with a screen you can self-test each Friday.

6 sessions · 35 minRead the block
