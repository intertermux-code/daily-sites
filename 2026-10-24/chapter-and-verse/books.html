Secondhand, and how it arrives

Roughly a fifth of what you see on the rail used to belong to somebody else. We buy from estates, from the two retired professors on Hill Lane, and from anyone who brings a box in on a Sunday afternoon when the door is unlocked.

Secondhand cloth gets a new numbering in pencil on the front endpaper, and anything with a previous owner's name inside goes on the bookplate table at a third off. Last year we resold eleven hundred of them.

A customer once returned a secondhand East of Eden because the margin notes were better than the book. We refunded her and kept the copy. It has been on the plate table ever since, at a third off, unbuyable.

If It Has an ISBN, We Can Put It in Your Hands

The distributor van comes Tuesday and Friday. Anything in print arrives within three working days, and we do not charge a handling fee for the ones that are hard to find — which is mostly small presses, translated poetry, and the American paperback that no one here will import.
