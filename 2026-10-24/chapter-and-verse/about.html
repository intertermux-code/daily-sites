Folio 01 · Opening Line

We Took Over a Stationer's and Never Stopped

In 2009 the unit at 14 Ferndale Row sold pens, wedding invitations and a disappointing amount of lottery tickets. It now holds eleven thousand four hundred and eighty books, four lamps at the back, and one rule: nothing goes on the rail that we cannot argue for at the counter.

Founded 2009 Three booksellers, one cat Same lease, same lamps

§ 02

The First Six Years, Abbreviated

Written up from the green ledger, which is kept in the drawer under the till and is not for reading.

Marguerite Vance spent eleven years as a library buyer and got bored of ordering books she would never see a reader hold. She signed the lease on Ferndale Row with eleven thousand in savings, a loan from her brother, and the shelving from a closed pharmacy in the next county. The first month takings covered the rate, the light and almost nothing else.

She hired Tobias Reyes in 2010 to run poetry because he kept coming in and correcting her on it. Ilse Marchetti arrived in 2012 with a secondhand box and a proposal about children's readings, and stayed long enough to argue the shop open on Sunday afternoons.

The long room came from the stockroom in 2012 — an argument with the landlord about a wall, resolved by knocking it out ourselves over five nights. The book club started a year later with eleven members meeting around two trestle tables. It has never been advertised and has grown by being handed to someone at a bus stop.

“If we can shelve it, we can sell it. If we can't say why it matters, we won't.”
The pharmacy shelving is still in bay C. You can see it if you look at the third upright: a manufacturer's stamp, 1961, and a pencilled price for aspirin that nobody has painted over. We are not allowed to paint over it.

§ 03

Seventeen Years, Filed by Year

The dates that changed the shape of the shop, in order, with the things we would not give back.

  • 2009

    The lease is signed

    Nineteen hundred square feet, a stationer's stock, and pharmacy shelving from the next county. Opens on the second Saturday of March with three hundred and twelve titles on the rail.

  • 2011

    The line goes up above the till

    Painted by a customer who owed us nothing and argued with the sign-writer about the ampersand. Still there; still correct.

  • 2012

    The stockroom becomes the long room

    Five nights to take a wall out. Thirty-four chairs, four lamps made locally, and the first candle-lit reading, which was meant to be one-off and has run every autumn since.

  • 2013

    The Reading Room opens

    Eleven members, two trestle tables, one chosen title a month. The letterpress note starts as a folded A6 because the paper was already cut.

  • 2015

    Poetry takes the floor

    Bay P doubles after a customer asked four days running for a book none of us had read. Six hundred titles now, and the sales per square foot beat fiction.

  • 2019

    The flood, and the local shelf

    Water in bay H to knee height. Regulars cleared it in a weekend; we started the local-writer shelf with the salvageable ones, and have kept adding to it.

  • 2022

    The re-binding bench

    Petra Hallam takes the Sunday bench. Two hundred and six bindings rebuilt, including nine from the pharmacy shelving's oldest arrivals.

  • 2026

    Where we are now

    Six hundred and twelve members, seventy-four nights a year, twenty-four labelled bays, and one contested decision about whether crime belongs by colour or by detective.

§ 04

Who Answers the Door

Three booksellers, one cat with an opinion about the poetry bay, and whoever is shelving on Monday.

Marguerite Vance

Founder · fiction, translated

Eleven years as a library buyer before this. Picks Calvino out of every new shipment and refuses to shelve anything she has not finished.

Tobias Reyes

Poetry & events

Hired in 2010 for correcting us about Mary Oliver. Runs the long room, the argument nights, and the ledger that counts the chairs.

Ilse Marchetti

Secondhand & children's hour

Arrived with a box of paperbacks in 2012 and a proposal. Buys the estate lots, prices the plate table, and reads the ghost stories in October.

Dante

Counter cat · bay P

Sleeps where the sunlight reaches at two o'clock. Has opinions about the poetry bay and no interest in anything shelved above waist height.

§ 06

Five Rules We Keep

Written on the back of the till in 2011, retyped once, unchanged since.

One — Nothing face-out without a defenceIf a bookseller cannot say why in one breath, it goes on a shelf and stays there. Rule i

Two — We do not take publisher moneyNo paid placement, no sponsored table, no advance-reading obligation. The rail is bought at full price. Rule ii

Three — Nobody is made to feel smallfor not having read the thing everyone has read, or for wanting the thing everyone has read twice. Rule iii

Four — Poetry and crime get the good floorThe two bays a chain would cut are the two we measured first. We have never regretted the argument. Rule iv

Five — The shop is the room, not the stockThirty-four chairs cost us a bay and a half. That is the correct trade and we would make it again. Rule v

Finding Us, and Getting In
