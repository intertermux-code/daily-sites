Twenty-Eight Years on the Same Street

We opened in a former dry-goods floor with nine hundred books and one chair for customers. The chair is still here. It is in the Quiet Room, and nobody is allowed to move it.

一番

A Lease Nobody Else Wanted

In the spring of 1998 Vera Lindqvist and Mitsuo Matsuda took the ground floor of 188 Linden Row because the landlord could not let it to anybody who minded the damp patch on the north wall. They had met shelving the basement at a university press and shared one belief: that a shop should be somewhere a person can sit down without buying something.

The first year sold nine hundred titles, most of them secondhand, most of them bought at the counter by people Vera remembers by name. Mitsuo drew the signage in ink on a sheet of packing paper; we still print the Thursday note from a scan of it.

What has not changed is the arithmetic. We are four full-time staff and two students, we take in more books than we can sell, and we would rather lend you a chair than rush you out of the door.

七章 — seven chapters

How the Shop Got Its Rooms

Drag sideways, or use the arrows. The damp patch took eleven years to fix.

1998

Nine Hundred Books, One Chair

The ground floor opens with secondhand shelves built from the dry-goods counters themselves. Vera writes every receipt by hand for the first four months because the till was sold to pay for the roof.

2003

The Quiet Room

We take the neighbouring unit and put poetry, translation and philosophy up four stairs, under a pine table with six chairs and a lamp for each. Silence becomes a policy, and then a habit.

2009

The Repair Bench

After the January flood we spend six weeks drying four hundred volumes. Mitsuo’s son Hiro arrives to help and never leaves; the bench in the back corner becomes a Wednesday class, then a workshop.

2014

Thursdays at Seven

A Bashō reading for eleven people turns into a standing appointment. We move the front-room shelves twice to get the acoustics right and leave them there, which is why the fiction run bends.

2020

Books on the Porch

Closed for eleven weeks, we keep four hundred holds on a long table in the doorway, numbered, picked up at a distance, with the shop light on all night so the street had somewhere to look.

2023

Four Clubs, One Table

The reading groups outgrow the back room and take over the whole shop on alternate evenings. Membership passes sixty; we buy a second kettle and stop pretending we only drink tea.

2026

Four Thousand, One Hundred and Twenty

Five rooms, four full-time staff, twenty-eight years of the same lease renewed on the same day each spring. The damp patch is fixed. We miss it, a little.

三つの心得

Three Things We Optimise For

間 — ま

Ma — Room to Stay

The empty part of the plan is not wasted. Two of our five rooms have more floor than stock, and the shop is quieter for it. Sit as long as you like; nobody will stack books on your chair.

詫び

Wabi — The Cracked Hinge

Foxing, a sun-faded spine, a name on the flyleaf: these are records of a window and a reader, not faults. We price secondhand accordingly and say plainly why.

敬

Kei — Everybody’s Book

No title is a joke here and no question is too basic at the counter. Three loan copies per club, seven off for members, and a chair for whoever turns up on a Thursday.

Transit
Line 15 stops at Linden & 8th, ninety seconds away. Bike rack on the north wall, two rings, always free.
Entrance
Step-free from Linden Row, door 34 inches without the display stand moved. Ring the bell if the awning is down.
Upstairs
The Quiet Room is four stairs with a handrail and a chair at the top. Ask and we will carry whatever you are looking at up there.
Sitting
Six chairs at the pine table, two stools behind the counter, one old armchair nobody is allowed to move.
Sound & light
No music in the Quiet Room, low light after eight, and a captioned reading on the first Sunday of alternating months.
Payment
Card, cash, shop credit, or a debt you settle when you next come in — Vera’s system, still in use.
