Before You Ask at the Counter

And if the answer here is not the one you need, ring us. Somebody is at the register from ten until seven, and the phone is never the thing we mind answering.

Plan your visit

Do I have to finish the book?

No. Roughly a third of any given night has not finished it, and that is when the good questions come out. We do ask you to have opened it, and to say plainly where you stopped.

What does the eighteen dollars cover?

A chair at every night for twelve months, seven off any title on the club list, first refusal on the three loan copies, and a name on a handwritten card. Nothing else — there is no fee for guests, and you can bring one whenever you like.

I have never done this. Is that a problem?

It is the reason we run the first two nights free. You will not be called on, and there is no test. If you want to sit at the end of the table and say nothing for an hour, nobody will notice, and one night you will not be able to help yourself.

Which edition should I buy?

We name it in the confirmation email, and it matters more than people expect — we read the 1818 Frankenstein, not the 1831 revision, and the Jowett Republic rather than a modern paraphrase. Any edition gets you in the room; the right one gets you in the argument.

Can I come to more than one club?

Yes, and about nine members do. The eighteen dollars is per person, not per table. Just tell the host which nights you are claiming so the chair count stays honest.

What happens in December and January?

December has three nights, then we shut from four o’clock on Christmas Eve until the 28th. January restarts with two new clubs and a marathon read of the Odyssey. The long dark is when attendance is best, if you want to know.
