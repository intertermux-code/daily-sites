Comp Lifts — powerlifting block

  • PrerequisiteOn-Ramp graduation, or a screen plus three months of logged training
  • TestEstimated 1RM total in week 12, optional meet in week 14
  • TrackingRPE + bar-speed check on every top set, logged on the floor tablet
  • Kit usedPlatforms 1–4, Eleiko bars, monotane boards, bands

Twelve weeks of wave-loaded competition work

Comp Lifts is the block that produced our last nine regional totals. You train the big three twice weekly on a heavy day and a volume day, with one technique day and one accessory day built around your weakest lift.

  • Day A — Heavy: top single at RPE 8.5, then back-off triples at 92 %, two accessories on the weak link.
  • Day B — Volume: five sets of five at a fixed percentage, plus high-rep hinge and row work.
  • Day C — Technique: paused reps, board work, competition commands and rack positioning with a coach watching.
  • Day D — Accessory: hypertrophy slots, sled carries, and trunk work that survives a heavy squat cycle.
  • Weekly estimated 1RM recalculation, so percentages track the lifter you are this week — not the one from week one.
  • Deload built into weeks 4, 8 and 12. Nobody asks permission to take them.

Who it is for

Members who want a bigger total and will accept that technique work is not optional. If you have never logged a training cycle, run On-Ramp first and come back — the block assumes you can hold a brace under a hard set.

Engine Room — HIIT and conditioning block

  • PrerequisiteNone. Intervals scale to any starting fitness.
  • Test3-minute step test week 1, repeat week 8 and week 12
  • TrackingChest-strap bands, zones shown on the floor clock
  • Kit usedAirdyne, ski erg, sled, KB, concept2 row

Conditioning that protects the lifting you came for

Thirty minutes, in and out, no queue for a machine. Engine Room exists so that Comp Lifts lifters can breathe through a five-set squat session and so general members get their cardiac work done without hammering a knee that is already loaded.

  • Monday — Base: 30 to 40 minutes at Zone 2 on bike or row. Conversational pace, no heroics.
  • Thursday — Intervals: 8 to 10 × 90 seconds hard, 90 seconds easy. Load scales, the ratio does not.
  • Saturday — Mixed: sled pushes, carries, kettlebell swings and a short finisher. Lowest skill demand, highest output.
  • No burpees from the floor on a fatigued spine, no timed max deadlifts, no whiteboard punishment.
  • Every session has a written scale for shoulder, hip or knee restrictions.

What changes by week eight

Most members see a 12 to 20 beat drop in recovery heart rate after a hard interval, and a step-test improvement they can actually feel in the last set of squats. That is the metric we program against, not a calorie number.

Range Lab — mobility and prehab block

  • PrerequisiteNone. Heated mat room, twelve mats.
  • TestAnkle, hip and shoulder screen at weeks 1 and 6
  • TrackingRange photos optional, position notes in your logbook
  • Kit usedMats, bands, kettlebells, foam rollers, wedges

Range you can load, not range you can photograph

Range Lab is where members fix the three restrictions that decide how deep they squat and how long they keep training: ankle dorsiflexion, hip internal rotation and thoracic extension. We test, we prove the new position with load, then it is yours.

  • Opening: breath and bracing drill, five minutes, no stretching cold tissue.
  • Block A: joint-specific mobility with a band or wedge, three positions held under tension.
  • Block B: strength at end range — kettlebell windmills, ATG split squats, controlled get-ups.
  • Closing: carry and hinge patterning so the new range shows up in your lifts the same day.
  • Best paired with one lifting session per week; the range disappears if you never load it.

Bring this if you are injured or returning

If a physio, GP or surgeon has given you a discharge summary, bring it. Range Lab is the safest entry point to the floor and the block our coaches use for return-to-barwork.

On-Ramp — eight-week beginner block

  • PrerequisiteNone. This is where everyone starts.
  • TestPosition holds under fatigue at week 8, then block choice
  • TrackingSame logbook you keep for the rest of your time here
  • Group capSix lifters per coach, always

Eight weeks, three sessions a week, a coach on you the whole time

On-Ramp is mandatory at Forge — for the office worker who has never touched a barbell and for the ex-rugby player returning after eleven years. The point is not to earn the right to train. It is to learn five patterns cleanly enough that load becomes a boring variable.

  • Weeks 1–2: brace, hinge, goblet squat, press, carry. Empty bar and kettlebell only.
  • Weeks 3–4: barbell positioning with a coach, tempo work, first steady back squat sets.
  • Weeks 5–6: deadlift from blocks, overhead press progression, sled and row conditioning.
  • Weeks 7–8: real work sets, a re-test of your baseline screen, and a block recommendation in writing.
  • Sessions are at 06:15, 12:15 and 18:15 so any shift pattern can fit three weeks in.

Where you go after week eight

Roughly six in ten On-Ramp graduates choose Engine Room first, then add lifting. About three move to a Monday/Wednesday Comp Lifts split, and one in ten stays on Range Lab alone while they finish rehabbing something. All three are correct answers.

Coaching method

Six rules that never change

Rule 01

Technique loads first

The bar moves before the weight goes on. Every session opens with positional work, and a coach clears it before your top set.

Rule 02

If it is not written down, it did not happen

Reps, load, RPE and how it moved. Your logbook is the program; the app is just where it lives.

Rule 03

Deload is programming

Three scheduled recovery weeks per block. You do not negotiate them with your coach and you do not skip them.

Rule 04

Small groups, real coaching

Six lifters per coach on technique slots. That number is the reason beginners progress quickly here.

Rule 05

Conditioning supports lifting

Engine work builds capacity without wrecking the joints you load on Monday. Sleds, bikes and rows, not punishment.

Rule 06

Nobody trains alone in week one

Eight coached weeks before you run a block unsupervised, whatever is on your training history.

Common questions

Before you pick a block

Can I run two blocks at once?

Yes, and most members do. The standard pairing is Comp Lifts twice weekly plus Engine Room on Tuesday and Saturday. What we will not program is two max-effort lifting blocks in the same week — your accessory slots will collide and something gets sacrificed.

I have a past injury. Which block?

Start with Range Lab plus On-Ramp. Bring your discharge summary or physio notes to your first session. Two of our coaches work alongside NHS physiotherapy, so we can program within a documented restriction and hand back a progress note at week six.

Do I need my own shoes or belt?

Flat, hard-soled trainers are fine for your first eight weeks — cushioned running shoes are the only real no. We loan lifting belts, wrist wraps and squat shoes in sizes 6 to 13 at the desk, and a pair of chalk is included on every membership.

What if I miss a whole week?

Freeze your block for up to three weeks per year on any membership tier. Miss less than seven days and your coach drops you back at the last deload. Miss longer and we restart the accumulation phase at a reduced number — this is normal, and nobody here keeps a tally.

Can I just train the platforms?

Once you have graduated On-Ramp, yes. Open floor runs all day; platforms are first come, first served, with a fifteen-minute cap when the floor is busy. Two members have trained nothing but open platforms for six years and hold very good totals.
