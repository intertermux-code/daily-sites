What to expect in a Comp Lifts session

Marcus runs on a written plan that you see before you warm up. He will ask what you slept, check your brace at the rack, and put the bar on you only when your position has held twice at RPE 7. Top sets are spotted by choice, never by default, and he keeps the meet commands honest — pause, rack command, down command — even in week two of a block.

If you arrive with a number that does not move for three weeks, he changes the accessory slot rather than the lifter. It is the least dramatic way to make progress and it works often enough that eleven members have stayed with him for more than four years.

  • Bring: logbook, flat shoes, belt if you own one (loan belts at the desk)
  • Ask him about: opener selection, bench arch, failing at lockout
  • He will not: let you add weight into a grinding rep on a technique day

Why Engine Room is not cardio punishment

Priscilla builds sessions where the work is honest and the exit is clean. You will not be timed on a max deadlift, you will not do burpees from a fatigued spine, and the interval length is decided before you walk in. The only variable you control is output, and she will show you the band you should be in.

Her Monday base sessions are the most under-booked classes in the building, which is the point: the members who do them are the ones still lifting hard at week forty of a block.

  • Bring: a drink, clothes you do not mind ruining, and shoes with a hard sole
  • Ask her about: zone 2, breathing under load, what to do with a sore knee on row day
  • She will not: let you turn a base session into a race

Junior Strength, and how we keep it boring on purpose

Jonah’s squad does not chase highlights. Twelve to sixteen weeks of patterning, sled racing, jumps, carries and controlled barbell work, with no one tested on a one-rep max before they turn sixteen. Athletes come here from school rugby and netball and leave able to brace, hinge and land properly.

He also runs the On-Ramp technique slots, which is why his hinge teaching script is on every coach’s tablet. If you are nervous about being watched by a room of teenagers, the same script is what you get on Monday morning.

  • Bring for juniors: trainers, water, a shin pad if you have one for the sled
  • Ask him about: school season timing, how much protein a growing athlete actually needs
  • He will not: put a bar on a spine that cannot hold a position yet

Return to the barbell after surgery or a long break

Dee takes the referrals other gyms turn away. Bring your discharge summary or your physio notes and she will build the first four weeks around the documented restriction, then hand back a progress note at week six so your clinician can see exactly what you loaded and how it responded.

The Sunday quiet room exists for people who find a full mat room too much. Three people, heated floor, twenty-four degrees, and a coach who is not counting down a timer on you.

  • Bring: any discharge summary, your scan report if you have one, socks for the mat
  • Ask her about: how deep to squat with a restricted ankle, when soreness is not a setback
  • She will not: stretch cold tissue or promise a range in one session

The person who keeps open floor safe

His Thursday carry session is the most social hour in the building: heavy sled, farmer handles, and a group that mostly talks about the price of a pint between sets.

  • Bring: nothing special. Ask for the late-floor keycard at your third visit

  • He will not: leave the floor for a phone call while six people are under bars

Composition work that keeps your lifts

Elisabet handles the part of the brief most gyms get wrong: eating around training. She sets protein and fuel ranges against your actual session count, makes weight-class cuts for members who compete, and will tell you plainly when a target is unrealistic.

If BMI is one of your starting points, run it on the membership page and bring the printout to your composition review. She will place it in context instead of treating it as a verdict.

  • Bring: two weeks of normal eating, written down, and your training log
  • Ask her about: making weight, training fasted, what a realistic month looks like
  • She will not: hand you a plan that drops your working loads

Coaching standards

What every coach here owes you

Std 01

A written plan

You see the session before you start it, including the loads and the reason for them.

Std 02

Six people, one coach

Technique groups never exceed six. If a class runs over, we open another slot.

Std 03

Current on safeguarding

Every coach holds an enhanced DBS check and renews first aid annually.

Std 04

Still training

All six coaches log their own sessions. Teaching a position and holding one are the same job.
