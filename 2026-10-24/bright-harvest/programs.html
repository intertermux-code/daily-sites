Four Programs, One Full Table

We do not run a single warehouse line. We run a market on wheels, a kit shelf, a school backpack and a hot kitchen — because a family missing dinner at 6 p.m. and a kid missing Sunday lunch need different fixes.

  • No ID required
  • No income test
  • Halal, kosher & vegetarian stock
  • Spanish, Vietnamese & Russian on site

Program spine

Every Program, Start to Finish

Follow the line. Each entry is a real program with its own hours, its own numbers and its own way in.

  1. 01
    Grocery shopping, minus the register

    Mobile Market

    A 38-foot refrigerated bus converted into a produce aisle. It parks in four towns every week and neighbours shop it like a store — choosing their own vegetables, fruit, bread, eggs and meat off open shelving.

    How it runs

    • Four permanent stops: Fordell, Eastgate, Cedar Hollow and the Harlan Road trailer park
    • Ninety minutes per stop, two trucks rotating so no site waits a week
    • Household picks up to 25 lbs of food, chosen by the shopper, not boxed for them

  2. 02
    Seven days, one bag

    Family Kits

    Twenty-one meals for a household of four, assembled the morning you pick up so the produce is that day’s. Designed with a dietitian so a kit can actually feed a week without a second trip.

    What is inside

    • Breakfast: oats, milk, eggs, bread, jam, fruit
    • Lunch: deli protein, cheese, tortillas, carrots, apples
    • Dinner: a roast or dry beans, potatoes, two vegetables, rice, oil, stock
    • Fridge-stable extras and a printed meal card with four one-pan recipes
    0

    kits distributed each week

    Swap versions always stocked: baby (formula, diapers, purees), diabetic-friendly, low-sodium, halal, kosher, vegetarian.

  3. 03
    Friday afternoon, quietly

    School Plate Program

    Two routes: a backpack of weekend food sent home with a child on Friday, and a produce drop into twelve cafeteria kitchens so Monday lunch is better than the week before.

    How a child gets in

    • A teacher, nurse, counsellor or family advocate refers — never a parent form
    • Backpacks go home unmarked, in the child’s own bag, no line at the office
    • Referrals open year-round; peak loading is the week schools let out and the week they return

  4. 04
    Hot food, real plates

    Cedar Street Kitchen

    A 40-cover community kitchen next to the warehouse. Tuesday and Thursday suppers are free, served family-style, and cooked from whatever the rescue run brought in that morning.

    Beyond the plate

    • Wednesday cooking classes: five ingredients, one pan, $0 cost, childcare on the bench
    • Friday prep crew: four paid trainees on a 12-week food-service pathway
    • Catering surplus van collects event leftovers at 6 p.m. and cooks them the same night

  5. 05
    38 agencies, one ledger

    Partner Pantry Network

    Most food leaves our dock in another organisation’s hands: church basements, school libraries, a barbershop in Fordell, a laundromat in Eastgate, two senior towers and a tribal health clinic.

    What partners commit to

    • Open at least four hours a week and post those hours on the county list
    • Never ask for ID, income proof or immigration status
    • Report pounds and household counts weekly — that is how the truck routing stays honest

Referring a family or student

Professionals refer through the county portal or by calling (541) 555-0162, ext. 2. You need a first name and a school or site — no income figures, no case numbers, no documentation of need. Referrals are how most neighbours reach us without ever filling anything in themselves.

What to bring

  • Nothing. No ID, no proof of address, no income statement
  • A bag or two, if you have them, so we use less cardboard
  • Your household size out loud — that is the whole intake
  • Any allergy or diet you want the shelf to remember for next time

Fair questions

Asking for Food, Honestly

Will someone judge me for coming?

No. Two-thirds of the people at our shelves work, and a third have never used food assistance before this year. Our volunteers are briefed on one rule: the first sentence is always hello, never a question.

How often can I pick up?

Weekly at any partner shelf and at the Mobile Market — there is no cap and no log to track you. Family Kits are available every seven days per household; if you need food before day seven, call the hotline and we will find a second site nearer you.

I do not have a car. What then?

Bus 12 and Bus 4 reach six of our sites. Two partners deliver to units on request, mostly seniors and people with disabilities — ask when you call the hotline and we will schedule the drop for the same week.

Do you have baby food, formula and diapers?

Yes, in the swap version we call the Little Kit: formula (ready-to-feed and powder), purees, oat cereal, diapers in four sizes and wipes. Stock arrives weekly from the regional health partners; if a size runs out we order more that day.

Can I get food without telling anyone I do not speak English well?

We have Spanish, Vietnamese and Russian speakers on the hotline at all times, and bilingual volunteers at the Fordell, Eastgate and Cedar Hollow sites on distribution days. Say the language you want when you call and we will hold the line for whoever answers.
