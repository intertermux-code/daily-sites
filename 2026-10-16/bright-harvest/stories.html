Featured story: Marisol Vega

Collected food · 2024–now

Marisol Vega

Parent of two · Eastside · Harvest Box member since March 2024

Marisol moved to Riverbend in the autumn to take a night-shift job at the Meridian packing plant, four hours from the family she had been caring for. By February the rent had gone up twice, her car needed a transmission, and the shops stopped being a place she could walk into without counting.

“A colleague mentioned the Wednesday truck at the school. I sat in the car for ten minutes because I thought someone would ask me to explain myself. Nobody did. A young man handed me a bag and said the figs were the good ones this week.”

“The box had fennel in it. Nobody had given my family fennel since I moved here — I cried in the parking lot, then went home and made dinner.”

She now collects a five-person box on alternate Fridays and shops the Eastside stop twice a week. Her son’s class is in the Backpack Program, which she found out about from him, not from a letter: “he came home describing the cucumber one and I had to sit down.”

In June she started the Saturday sorting shift herself, three months after her first box. “I wanted to be the person who hands someone the bag instead of the person waiting for one. Also I am very good at the figs.”

Told to Ana Reyes in the Northside kitchen, September 2026.

How we tell these stories

Nine people appear on this page. Getting them here took about four months and a lot of second drafts, and it is the part of the job we are most careful about.

  1. Consent, in writing, twice.Once to be interviewed and once again after seeing the finished piece. Either can be withdrawn, and two stories here have been pulled and rewritten at the teller’s request.
  2. They choose the photograph.Every teller picks from the frames taken that day, including the option of no picture at all. Nobody is photographed at a site while collecting food.
  3. We pay for the time.A $150 honorarium, plus travel and childcare for the interview. Their words are edited for length and clarity, never for sentiment.
  4. No before-and-after.We do not publish a story that ends in gratitude toward us. If the teller wants to thank someone, that is their call — and three of them did.

Want to tell yours? Write to stories@brightharvest.org or hand a note to any site volunteer. We are especially looking for people who have used the Backpack Program as an adult, winter delivery drivers, and grocers who switched from a compactor to our van.
