Program 01 · Six stops a week

Mobile Market

A 16-foot refrigerated truck converts into a produce stall in about twenty minutes. Crates of rescued fruit, vegetables, herbs, bakery loaves, yogurt, and eggs are laid out market-style and priced at nothing at all. You walk the rows, choose what your family will actually eat, and weigh your own bag.

Stops rotate through Eastside, Mill Row, Braddock Court, the Fairgrounds lot, Southbank Library, and the Grange Hall at Wentworth. Each holds for ninety minutes. No sign-up, no queue paperwork — arrive, shop, leave.

“The first time I went I stood at the cucumbers for five minutes, waiting to be told what to do. Nobody ever did.”

Program 02 · Weekly or fortnightly

Family Harvest Boxes

Boxes are packed to a menu written by our kitchen team, then filled to match: a protein, a starch, three vegetables, fruit, and a card with a twenty-minute method. A household of five gets roughly fourteen meal components. Everything is chosen so that a person with one pot and no oven can cook it.

Collect from any of 24 partner sites — pantries, churches, schools, a laundromat, and two clinics. Tell us once about allergies, halal or kosher needs, infant feeding, or a household that eats vegetarian, and every subsequent box respects it.

Homebound? The delivery van covers four ZIP codes on Tuesdays and Fridays, and volunteers hand-carry to the door for anyone who cannot get to a site.

Program 03 · Every night at 6 p.m.

Community Kitchens

Three neighbourhood kitchens serve a hot dinner cooked largely from rescued food, family-style, at one long table rather than in a line. Two staff cooks and a rotating volunteer crew plate 400–450 covers a night. Kids eat free, and there is always a vegetarian lead dish.

Stay for coffee. There is homework help at the Northside table on Mondays and Wednesdays, and a phone-charging drawer by the door at all three sites.

Program 04 · Fridays during term

Backpack Program

Schools and our staff quietly identify children who rely on school meals during the week. On Friday each child leaves with a backpack holding five no-cook meals, a fruit, and something to share with a sibling — nothing branded, nothing that marks them out at the coat hook.

Refills happen at school, and a summer version runs through the lunchroom at two sites. In term time we also send a short note home with the enrolment form for harvest boxes, for families who want more than the backpack.

Program 05 · The engine behind the other five

Rescue & Recovery

Two refrigerated vans run a 6 a.m. loop and two more at 2 p.m., collecting grocery short-dates, cosmetically imperfect produce, surplus bakery, and mislabelled cases that would otherwise be binned. Everything lands at Millwright Avenue, gets checked against a safety card, and is out to a site within 24 hours.

Last year this kept 2.1 million pounds of real food out of landfill and cut our purchase bill by $310,000 — which is why $3 buys a meal here.

Program 06 · Twice a month, evenings

Cooking Commons

Two-hour classes in the Northside teaching kitchen where a local cook builds four dinners from one harvest box, and everyone eats what they made. The point is not instruction — it is that a person who has never cooked a dal discovers that the box contains everything needed for one.

Children are welcome at the counter; aprons, a homework table, and a bilingual instructor are standard. Participants consistently report wasting less of the box once they have cooked with it.

Common questions

What people ask at the door

Not answered here? Our team reads every message and replies within a working day.

Do I have to prove that I cannot afford food?

No. Bright Harvest has never run an income test, and we do not collect immigration status, benefit details, or a reason for being there. Partner agencies confirm only that a household lives or works in Midland County and wants food.

Two thirds of our Backpack Program families work for a living. Food insecurity in this county is usually about the price of fuel, a broken gearbox, or a rent rise — not about a paperwork category.

How often can I come?

As often as you need. Harvest boxes are packed weekly or fortnightly by household size, but the Mobile Market and the community kitchens have no cap at all, and the mutual-aid fridge never closes.

Can I choose what goes in my box?

At the Mobile Market you choose every item yourself. With harvest boxes, packing crews swap anything you will not eat — tell the site volunteer once and it is noted on your card for the rest of the year.

We can accommodate allergies, halal and kosher requirements, diabetic and renal-friendly patterns, infant formula, and pureed textures. None of it requires a doctor’s letter.

I run a shop, farm, or canteen. How do I hand food over?

Call the dock on (555) 519–0142 and say what you have, how much, and how it is stored. Most businesses get a standing collection slot the same week, and we provide the crates and the liability cover.

Small growers can drop at the warehouse any weekday morning without an appointment.
