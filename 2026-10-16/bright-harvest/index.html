The short trip from waste to dinner

01

Rescue, before the truck leaves

Our four refrigerated vans run a 6 a.m. and a 2 p.m. loop past 61 grocery partners and 18 farms. Anything that will not sell by weekend comes back to Millwright Avenue the same morning, still cold.

1,400 lbs average per run

02

Sort on the long tables

Volunteer crews check every item against a safety card, trim what is bruised, wash what can be saved, and log the weight. Anything doubtful is composted, never bagged. Two shifts a day, ninety minutes each.

No experience needed — we train on the floor

03

Pack a box that cooks well

Family Harvest Boxes are built around a recipe, not leftovers: a protein, a starch, three vegetables, and a card with a 20-minute method. Diet and culture are recorded on the first visit, so a box never arrives insulting.

Halal, kosher, vegan, and infant options on request

04

Deliver it with a choice

Pick up at one of 24 partner pantries, shop the Mobile Market for nothing, or have it brought to the door if you are homebound, working two jobs, or caring for someone who is. Nobody signs a means test.

What we run

Six programs, one county

Everything below is free, always. No ID, no income paperwork, no limits on how often you come.

Mobile Market

A refrigerated truck that parks in six neighborhoods each week. Fill your own bag with produce, dairy, and bakery — you choose, you pay nothing.

1,930 shops per week

Family Harvest Boxes

Recipe-first boxes for households of two to eight, collected at 24 pantries, churches, and schools across Midland County.

7,400 boxes monthly

Community Kitchens

Hot dinner at 6 p.m. in three neighborhoods, cooked with rescued ingredients and served family-style. Sit as long as you like.

412 seats nightly

Backpack Program

Friday afternoons, 2,600 children at 26 schools leave with a backpack of five weekend meals that fit quietly inside a schoolbag.

26 partner schools
