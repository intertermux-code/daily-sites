What a Good Rate Looks Like

Three Landmarks Worth Chasing

A savings rate is not a grade. It is a measure of how much of your time you keep. These are the three thresholds we point members at, in the order most people reach them.

Ten Percent

The Floor

Below ten percent, a $600 car repair erases a whole month of progress. This is the rate that turns emergencies into inconveniences, and it is reachable for almost everyone in a single afternoon of cancelling.

Twenty Percent

The Target

Twenty percent is where retirement stops needing luck. At the median Mintwise salary it buys about six weeks of freedom a year, and it is the rate roughly one in five members clears within their first nine months.

Thirty-Five Percent

The Compounding Lane

Hold thirty-five percent and a forty-year career compresses toward the low twenties. Almost nobody gets there by eating less — they get there by automating the transfer on payday and never seeing the money.

Keep Scrolling to Fan the Deck

Save the percentage, not the leftover. Automate it on payday. A buffer is not a goal, it is a floor. Raise the rate once, keep it forever. Never budget a dollar you have not earned.

No Black Boxes

The Math, in Plain English

Every number above comes from four lines of arithmetic. We would rather show you the working than ask you to trust a coloured chart.

  • Savings = income − expenses. Every listed category is subtracted from take-home pay. If the answer is negative, the ring turns red and the verdict tells you the shortfall in dollars, not percentages.
  • Rate = savings ÷ income. A household saving $400 on $3,400 has the same rate as one saving $1,000 on $8,500. The percentage is the honest comparison, not the dollar figure.
  • Buffer months = savings ÷ needs. Only categories marked as needs count, because a want you can cancel is not a bill you must pay. Six months is the conventional target; three is a working floor.
  • Debt is not in this calculator. Mintwise treats a loan payment as an expense and the balance as a separate plan, because paying $300 a month toward a card is not the same as keeping $300.

Stuck on the Ring

Six Levers, Easiest First

Median rate lift, from members who tried each one

Lever One

Move the Transfer to Payday

Saving what is left at the end of the month works for almost nobody. Sending a fixed percentage the morning your pay lands lifts the median member 4.1 points in the first quarter.

Lever Two

Audit the Small Recurring

Subscriptions under $20 are invisible and rarely used. Members who cancel three of them gain 1.4 points without touching a single purchase they actually enjoy.

Lever Three

Price the Weekly Ritual

One $34 Friday takeout is $1,768 a year — 3.4 points at a $52,000 salary. Keep the ritual, change the price, and the rate moves while the habit survives.

Lever Four

Refinance the Boring Debt

Shifting a 22 percent card balance to 9 percent frees the difference straight to savings. Two points of rate, no behavioural willpower required.

Lever Five

Bank the Raises Whole

Your lifestyle grows to fill any raise. Committing the first half of each increase to savings before it lands is the single largest lever in this list at 5.8 points.

Lever Six

Set a Floor, Not a Goal

Goals are aspirational and get quietly abandoned. A floor you refuse to go under — “never below twelve percent” — is checked weekly, and Mintwise tells you on Wednesday if you are about to break it.
