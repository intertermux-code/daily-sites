Entry III

Four rooms, and what each one is for

The building was a rope works, then a furniture warehouse, then, for six years in the 1990s, a bad restaurant. What it gave us was depth: a front shop that catches the street light, a Long Room for the arguments, and a cellar that stays dry. We did not knock through a single wall — readers wanted the shop to feel like several shops, and they were right.

The Castle holds fiction and the Gothic case, shelved by country rather than genre so that a reader looking for Shirley Jackson finds Henry James next to her. The Cellar is poetry, small presses and translation, lit by one bulb and kept deliberately cold. The Long Room does non-fiction, philosophy and the essayists, and hosts every event you see on the register. The Window is where we put the books we cannot stop pressing on people.
