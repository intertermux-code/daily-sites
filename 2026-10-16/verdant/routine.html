01 The Method

Every routine we
recommend has three

Cleanse, treat, seal. Anything more than that has to earn its place, and most things do not.

Step 01 — Cleanse

Sixty seconds, lukewarm

Hot water and foaming strips lipids you cannot replace quickly. Massage for a full minute by the sink, then rinse and leave the skin damp.

Step 02 — Treat

One active, on damp skin

Serum goes on while the cleanser is still slippery — that is what the toner is for. Four drops, pressed not rubbed, then ninety seconds before the next layer.

Step 03 — Seal

Cream, or SPF, never neither

Morning ends in mineral sunscreen. Evening ends in the ceramide cream. Skipping the seal is the single most common reason an active appears to fail.

Note — margin, p. 4 Introduce one new product every six days. When something goes wrong, you will know which bottle did it.

03 After The Quiz

What to expect,
week by week

Your selection arrives with a printed schedule. This is the same timeline, so you can judge your skin against something other than a mood.

  • Week 1Patch-test behind the ear for six days, then introduce the cleanser and cream only. Skin should feel less tight by day five.
  • Week 2Add the treatment twice weekly, on evenings, over damp skin. Expect nothing. This week is about tolerance.
  • Week 3Move the treatment to every second night. Any flaking around the mouth means you go back a step, not that you stop.
  • Week 4One full turnover done. Tone and texture changes become visible in the same mirror, in the same light, at day twenty-eight.

04 Assumptions

What this quiz
does not know

Four limits we would rather state than have you discover on day nine.

Retinaldehyde is not something we recommend during pregnancy, and the quiz does not know you are. Azelaic acid is generally considered the safer active for pigment in that period. Ask your dermatologist, then write to us and we will swap the recommendation at no cost.

Eczema, rosacea, psoriasis and perioral dermatitis respond differently than the categories in this quiz assume. Our formulas are built to be tolerable alongside treatment, not to replace it. Anything that stings on the third application should be stopped, not pushed through.

If a friend’s regimen works for their oily skin at 24, it will not necessarily work for yours at 41. Sebum output falls with age, and the barrier repairs more slowly. Take the shorter routine and add, rather than the reverse.

Then the index has nine, and the second bottle you add is usually the toner. But the honest answer from eleven years of formulation is that most people get better skin from using three things consistently than nine things optimistically.
