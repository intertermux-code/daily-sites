Everything in the bottle has a name.

Six ingredients do most of the work at Verdant. Here is where each one comes from, what dose it goes in at, and what we tested before it earned its place.

01 Origin

It started as
a shorter list

In 2019 our founder Ida Fontaine kept a notebook of every ingredient that had ever made her skin sting. It ran to forty-one names. The first Verdant formula was written as the negative space around that list.

We are not a beauty company that happens to make skincare. We are three formulators, a lab technician and one very patient order clerk, working out of a ground-floor studio in Copenhagen with a filling line in Valby.

Ida came from a dermatology research group; Tobias from flavour chemistry, where you learn fast that a dose is a dose and that more is rarely better. Between them they had spent eleven years watching effective ingredients get discarded because they were boring to market, and ineffective ones get kept because they photographed well.

So the rule was set before the first batch: publish the dose, prove the claim, and remove anything that exists only to make the product feel like a treat. That leaves a short list, a slightly dull colour and a texture you can layer at seven in the morning with wet hands.

Note — margin, p. 1 The forty-one names in that notebook are now printed on our standards page. We add to it whenever a customer tells us what hurt.

02 Ingredient Index

Six that earn
their place

Ordered by how much of the range they carry, with the dose we actually use and the source we buy from.

01

NiacinamideVitamin B3, nicotinamide

The workhorse. It supports ceramide production in the upper epidermis, moderates sebum and visibly evens tone over one full turnover. We use 5% because the dose–response curve flattens there and stinging climbs above it.

Appears in: Clear Index 04, Hydrate No. 3, Quiet Lids 09.

5.0%in serum

02

Ceramide NP, AP, EOPFermented lipid complex

Three of the ceramides your stratum corneum already contains, blended in the ratio found in healthy skin and delivered in a cholesterol-and-fatty-acid base. Fermented rather than animal-derived, which is why it costs more and travels less.

Appears in: Ceramide Field 05.

3.0%total lipids

03

Azelaic AcidDicarboxylic acid, grain-derived

Our answer to post-blemish marks and flushed cheeks. It is pregnancy-compatible, plays properly with retinaldehyde, and at 10% in a suspension it does the work prescription strengths do for most people, more slowly and with less irritation.

Appears in: 10% Marks 08.

10.0%suspension

04

RetinaldehydeRetinal, the aldehyde of retinol

One conversion step from retinoic acid, where retinol needs two. Encapsulated so it stays active to the last pump, and buffered with bisabolol because the honest truth is that tolerance, not potency, decides whether you keep using it.

Appears in: 0.04% After Dark 06.

0.04%encapsulated

05

Matcha Leaf WaterCamellia sinensis, shade-grown

We replace part of the water phase with cold-brewed leaf water from one estate in Uji — the same farm that supplies tencha for ceremony. It contributes polyphenols and, more usefully, a slip that lets serums spread on damp skin without pilling.

Appears in: Hydrate No. 3, First Light 01.

4.0%leaf water

06

Colloidal OatAvena sativa, Jutland-milled

Milled from a farm forty minutes from the studio. Beta-glucans and avenanthramides take the edge off a reactive face, which is why the cleansing milk is what we tell people to reach for in the first two weeks of any routine change.

Appears in: First Light 01, Second Cleanse 02.

6.0%in cleanser

03 Method

Four steps between
a formula and a batch

Every batch is 1,800 units. If one fails a step, it does not leave Valby — we keep it for the studio and write down why.

Step 01

Source

Two suppliers minimum per material, both audited annually. Anything we cannot name a farm for does not go in.

Step 02

Formulate

Six to fourteen prototype versions. We test pH, viscosity and layering against the other eight products before efficacy.

Step 03

Use-test

64 people, twice daily, 28 days, instrument readings at day 0, 14 and 28. Both dry and reactive skin types, always.

Step 04

Fill

Small batches in Valby, coded to the pallet. Stability samples pulled from every run and held for three years.

04 Standards

The excluded
forty-one

A short list of what we use is only meaningful next to a long list of what we do not. These are the names from Ida’s notebook, kept current by whatever customers report.

Fragrance and essential oils head the list because they are the two most frequent causes of allergic contact dermatitis in leave-on cosmetics, and because they deliver nothing your skin can use. The rest are materials with either a thin safety record at the doses the industry likes, or no record at all.

  • Anhydrous lanolin
Note — margin, p. 27 “Clean” is not a claim we make. Several excluded here are perfectly safe; we simply cannot find a reason to need them.

05 Packaging

Glass once,
pouch forever

The bottle is the only part of a skincare product most people throw away. We designed around that single decision.

71%

Less material per refill

A 200 ml pouch uses 22 g of laminate against the 340 g of a glass tube and cap.

20%

Lower price on refills

The whole saving is passed on. We are not subsidised by it — the glass was the margin.

4,200

Bottles returned in 2025

Washed on site and refilled for the studio. Anything chipped becomes a lab beaker.

0

Plastic in the outer box

Paper tape, moulded pulp trays, no shrink wrap. Our courier picks it up by bicycle in the city.

Why the colour is dull

Matcha leaf water goes a green-grey that no one has ever called beautiful in a focus group. We stopped adding colour in 2021, which means the shade you see in the jar is the shade the ingredients make on their own.

It also means a batch that turns slightly darker in winter is telling you something true about the harvest, and we would rather explain that than hide it.
