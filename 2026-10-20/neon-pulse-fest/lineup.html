Spotlight

The Four Closing Names

Each of these acts has a full production budget, a longer slot than anyone else on the bill, and a reason to fly in early.

The four rooms

Know Where You’re Standing

Pulse Mainstage — Hangar 9

6,000 capacity under a 14-metre steel roof. Line array left, right and centre, plus a 24-metre LED truss that gets used sparingly. Heated through the December nights, so this is where you recover.

The Void — Warehouse 4B

2,200 standing, blacked-out, no house lights between sets and no phone photography on the stage deck. Delay towers mean the back wall hits as hard as the rail. Doors lock at 2 a.m.

Bassment — Loading Dock Tunnel

1,400 capacity in a concrete service tunnel with a full sub array laid into the floor. It is warm, it is close, and the low end is physically noticeable above 100 dB. Earplugs at the top of the ramp.

Solaris — The Grass Bank

3,000 outdoors on a south-facing slope above the bay. Runs 2 a.m. to sunrise on Saturday with blankets, hot drinks and a fog machine that competes with the actual weather.

Fine print

About Set Times

Timetables are final from 1 December 2026. If an act cannot travel we replace them the same day and email every pass-holder for that night — the slot length stays the same. Stage changeovers run eight minutes; The Void and Bassment run back-to-back mixes, so you will never hear silence in those rooms.

Two sets overlap on Saturday between 1:40 and 2:00 a.m. That is intentional. You cannot see everything, and the festival is better for the walk you miss.
