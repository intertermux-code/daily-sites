What Neon Pulse is

A Festival Built Around the Drop

Neon Pulse is a three-night electronic music festival staged inside a decommissioned shipping yard on the Oakland waterfront. No fields, no mud, no 40-minute walk between stages — just warehouses, cranes, concrete and a sound system tuned by people who stand in front of it.

Every year we book 24 acts across four rooms: a 6,000-capacity mainstage in Hangar 9, a blacked-out warehouse called The Void, a subterranean bass cave, and Solaris — an open-air garden stage that plays from 2 a.m. until the sun comes up over the bay. Sets run long. Transitions are deliberate. Nobody plays a 25-minute slot.

The weekend

Three Nights, Three Temperatures

Each night has its own shape. Friday opens heavy, Saturday goes wide, Sunday lands soft. Buy all three and you get the whole arc.

Friday · 18 December · Doors 8:00 p.m.

Ignition

The yard cracks open with techno. Lumenkraft brings a 90-minute live audio-visual rig to the Pulse Mainstage, Kern Depth takes The Void apart for two hours, and the bass cave doesn’t exhale until 4 a.m.

Saturday · 19 December · Doors 6:00 p.m.

Peak

The longest night. Six hours of headliner-tier programming across all four rooms, a coordinated laser cue at midnight, and the Solaris garden running on a single continuous set from 2 a.m. to dawn.

Sunday · 20 December · Doors 4:00 p.m.

Afterglow

Earlier start, slower BPM, everything outdoors. Ambient, dub, live modular and a brass-led closing ceremony on the mainstage at 11 p.m. Sunday passes are the ones locals buy.

The system

Tuned by People Who Stand in Front of It

We run a 96-box line array on the mainstage, delay towers in The Void so the back wall hits as hard as the rail, and a full sub array under the Solaris grass bank.

Every stage is time-aligned and measured on the night before doors. If you’ve ever stood at a festival and heard the kick arrive a half-second after the flash — that’s not going to happen here. Bring earplugs anyway. We give them out free at all four gates.

Where

Meridian Yards, Oakland Waterfront

Nine acres of concrete, steel and bay wind, two kilometres from the Lake Bay Terminal BART station. The yard has been hosting electronic music since 2014 and it still sounds like nothing else in Northern California.

  • Hangar 9 / Pulse Mainstage — 6,000 capacity, covered, heated in December.
  • The Void — Warehouse 4B. Blacked-out room, 2,200 capacity, no phones on stage.
  • Bassment — Loading dock tunnel. 1,400 capacity, sub-heavy, all-ages until 10 p.m.
  • Solaris — Open garden on the grass bank. 3,000 capacity, blankets encouraged.
