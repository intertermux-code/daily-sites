The Craft

Milled, Proofed, and Baked Within These Walls

Our stone mill sits by the window on purpose. Grain arrives in sacks from farms in the Alentejo and Beira Baixa, and it leaves as flour the same week — nothing waits long enough to lose its smell.

Long, cold fermentation does the rest: twelve to thirty-six hours of patience give our loaves their open crumb, their keep, and that faint sourness children politely ignore.

“Bread is the clock the neighborhood keeps its time by.”

Come Up the Hill

A Corner Shop with a Long Queue and Good Reason

We open at 7:30 on the Bica steps, twelve minutes after the tram clears the corner. Grab a nata while it is still too hot to name, then find the two marble tables by the window — they are usually free if you are early.
