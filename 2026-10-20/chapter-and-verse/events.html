Four nights a week, the tables are spoken for.

Everything below happens in the shop, in the cellar, or in the yard behind the bindery. Most nights are free; the ones that cost money include something you can drink.

The Calendar

Open a night to see the room.

Tap any line to expand it. Doors open thirty minutes early, and we keep two unreserved chairs at the front for whoever turns up last.

Two poets and a translator take turns with exile, landscape, and the sentence that refuses to stay in one language. Iona reads from a sequence written in a Fife bothy through one entire winter; Samir answers with his new translations from Catalan, which is how the argument started in the first place.

Fourteen steps down, standing room for forty, and the acoustics are better than anyone has a right to expect from a former ribbon store.

Bring a paperback whose spine has given up. You will learn to sew a text block on tape, line the spine with Japanese tissue, and re-case it so it shuts like it did on the day you bought it. Three hours, hands-on, eight people maximum.

All tools and materials are provided, along with tea, a biscuit you will not notice the crumbs of, and your repaired book at the end.

We hand four translators the same sentence — this month it is the opening of Pedro Páramo — and they read what they made, then defend it against the room. It is the most hostile evening we run and the best attended.

Voting is by applause, the winner gets a £20 shop credit and the lasting enmity of the others. Last year’s margin was eleven decibels.

Where It Happens

Three rooms, each with a different echo.

The Poetry Cellar

Fourteen steps down, brick on four walls, holds forty standing. Readings are unamplified, which is why the room goes quiet on its own.

Capacity 40Step-free? No

The Long Table

First floor, twenty-two seats around one Sussex oak slab. Book clubs, launches, and the arguments that go on after everyone else has gone home.

Capacity 22Lift access

The Bindery

In the yard behind the shop, under a skylight. Eight people at the benches, six at the press, and nobody allowed near the guillotine unaccompanied.

Capacity 8Step-free

Hire the Shop

Borrow the room, keep the books.

Outside opening hours the shop is yours: shelves, tables, twenty-two chairs, the espresso machine, and a person who will hand you a glass and then leave you alone.
