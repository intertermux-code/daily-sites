Book Club

Fourteen clubs. Not one has ever died out.

No auditions, no pace-shaming, and nobody who has not finished the book gets tucked into the corner. Answer a few questions and we will seat you at the table that suits how you read.

How It Works

Four steps, then wine.

01

Tell Us How You Read

Finish the form at the bottom of this page. The important question is the honest one: how much reading is too much for you in a month.

02

We Seat You

Nia matches you against fourteen live clubs and emails within two days with a table, a night, and the name of whoever brings the biscuits.

03

Buy the Book Here

Club copies are ten per cent off and held until the meeting starts. Read it, loan it, dog-ear it; we will still take it back.

04

Come Back or Leave

No rolling attendance guilt. Miss six months, turn up on a Tuesday, and somebody will already have saved you the good chair.

Before You Ask

The questions we get every September.

No, and roughly a third of any given table has not. Coming anyway is the tradition; the reading is the homework nobody marks. The one rule is that you do not summarise the ending for people who have not got there, which we enforce with a look.

Nothing for the club itself, ever. You buy the book if you want it (members get ten per cent off), and the Sunday table asks for £2 towards wine. The cellar nights are free because we already have the glasses upstairs.

The First-Timers exists precisely for you, and it runs with a printed script on the table: three opening questions, then it takes care of itself. Every permanent club in this shop has at least one member who started there and would rather not be reminded.

Partners and friends: yes, tell us in the form and we will count the chairs. Children: the Sunday table is the family one, with two volunteers on door duty. Any other night, bring them to a cellar reading instead — nobody has ever complained about a child listening.

We like that about three weeks in, when the novelty has worn off and you still turn up. Bring four names and a night you can hold; we provide the room, the ten per cent, and a shelf label with your club’s title on it. Nine of the fourteen started exactly that way.

Ground floor and the bindery are step-free; the first floor has a lift big enough for a pushchair and a stack of Moby-Dick. The cellar is fourteen steps and we are honest about it. Say the word in the form and we will move your club upstairs without making a thing of it.
