How We Shelve

No decimals. Nine rooms of argument.

We do not use a classification scheme. Fiction sits with criticism when they are having a conversation, and the poetry cellar is technically a fire hazard, which we are working on. New arrivals go on the front tables for a fortnight, then move to whichever wall they argue best against.

If you cannot find something, do not browse. Ask. Somebody here has read it, disowned it, or is quietly furious that we do not stock it yet.

Ordering & Holds

Questions the counter hears daily.

Yes, and it costs you nothing extra. Give us a title, an author, and roughly how much you can wait. Trade orders clear overnight; the harder ones — out-of-print, foreign editions, anything with “private press” in the description — take a week or two and we will email you the moment it lands.

Ten days from the moment it arrives, and we will text you twice. After that it goes back on the shelf, because someone else has usually been asking about it. Book-club copies are held until the night of the meeting, no exceptions, even when you are late.

Tom takes them on Wednesdays and Saturdays between noon and five. Bring the books, not the photographs. We pay in shop credit at 120 per cent of our cash offer, and we turn down more than we buy — mostly Reader’s Digest sets, and anything with a water stain shaped like a map.

If the author is here, yes — buy the copy from the trestle table at the event and queue with the rest of us. We do not do pre-signed stock. A book signed before you have read it is a decoration; afterwards it is evidence.
