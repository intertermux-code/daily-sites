Nothing Here Is Charity Theatre

We run the pantry like a grocery store, because that is what hungry people need: shelves, choice, and a clerk who treats them like a customer. Every item that leaves this building is scanned, weighed and traced back to the farm, bakery or family that gave it.

The chain of custody

Four Moves From Field To Front Step

Food only stays good when nobody is guessing. Here is exactly what happens between a surplus flat of tomatoes at Mill River and the bag that lands on your kitchen counter.

  1. Grow & Gather

    Eleven grocery partners set aside short-date product at 7 p.m. Cane Hill and six other farms field-call us for surplus that is too good to plow under.

    63% grown within 40 miles

  2. Sort & Store

    Everything passes through Dock 2 on Mill Race Road. Volunteers grade, wash where needed, weigh and scan each lot into cold or dry storage within ninety minutes.

    Two walk-ins · 38°F and 55°F

  3. Cook & Pack

    The Bright Kitchen turns near-expiry produce into ready-chilled meals. Households that would rather cook take a family box built for five days.

    1,284 meals plated daily

  4. Deliver & Choose

    Six refrigerated vans run fourteen fixed stops, and our home-delivery crew reaches 860 neighbors who cannot drive. At every stop you pick what your family actually eats.

    Choice pantry, always

The Only Part We Cannot Scan

A tonnage report tells you how much food moved. It cannot tell you what happened at the kitchen table. So we ask, and neighbors answer, and we print what they say without tidying it up.

  • Every story is published with written consent and a chosen name if the neighbor wants one.
  • Nobody is photographed at a distribution line, ever.
  • Storytellers are paid $150 for their time by our partner newsroom.
