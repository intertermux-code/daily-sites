Six Ways Food Reaches A NeighborNeighbor

North River County is 740 square miles with one full grocery store for every 14,000 people and four towns that have none at all. So we do not run one pantry and hope. We run six programs that meet people where they already are: at a bus stop, a school door, a porch, a church lot.

The six programs

Program 01 · 14 stops

Mobile Pantry Route

Two refrigerated 16-foot trucks converted into shelves, parking in church lots and fire stations from Two Rivers to Odell Bend. You walk the aisles and pick your own groceries, exactly as you would in a store, then pay nothing at the door.

  • Fourteen stops a week, each open four hours with no appointment.
  • Two crews of three: one driver, one stocker, one greeter who speaks Spanish or ASL.
  • Diapers, infant formula, insulin-safe coolers and pet food at every stop.
Route schedule Ride a van

Program 02 · 12 schools

School Backpack Program

Friday at 2:40 p.m., a counselor sets a backpack in a cubby and a child takes it home without a line, a form, or a single question. It refills Monday at school. 2,100 kids across Ashford Falls, Wexford and Fairview Cross.

  • Nine no-cook items a child can open and eat alone: turkey and oats, fruit cups, whole-grain crackers, milk.
  • Referrals come only from school staff, never from the family.
  • Backpacks are plain navy canvas so nobody reads a logo across a cafeteria.

Program 03 · 860 homes

Senior Food Box

Fixed income does not stretch over a pension cut and a knee replacement. Neighbors 60 and older on this route get a five-day box every fortnight, carried inside and onto the counter by the same volunteer each time.

  • Low-sodium, soft-to-chew and diabetes-friendly swaps on request, noted on the route card.
  • A two-minute wellness check is part of the delivery, and a missed knock goes to a named coordinator.
  • No grocery benefit paperwork — age and ZIP are the only two things we ask.
Call (555) 014-2200 Fund a year of boxes

Program 04 · 48 plots

Community Garden & Gleaning

Forty-eight half-plots behind the Mill Race building are free to any household that eats what it grows. Whatever our gardeners cannot use, and whatever Cane Hill Farm leaves in the field, our gleaning crew picks up inside a day.

  • Seedlings, water, tools and a shared hoop house, at no cost.
  • 2.1 tons gleaned last season — 71% of it went straight to the senior route.
  • Plot holders keep half of what they grow and donate what they can not eat.
Gleaning days

Program 05 · 1,284 meals a day

The Bright Kitchen

Produce that will brown by Friday becomes a ready-chilled meal by Wednesday. Our two cooks build one hot plate and one grab-and-go tray daily from whatever the warehouse has too much of, so nothing good gets thrown and nobody eats only carbs.

  • Halal, vegetarian and low-sodium versions of every menu, always in stock.
  • Trays finish at 2:30 p.m. and are on the Wednesday evening van by five.
  • St. Brendan’s Kitchen and four congregations retherm and serve on Sundays.
Line shift Buy a week of trays

Program 06 · 48 classes

Cook What’s In The Box

A ninety-minute class at the library, the senior center or the church hall, taught by a neighbor who cooks on $34 a week. Every attendee leaves with a kit of the exact ingredients and a printed card with no fancy words on it.

  • Classes in English and Spanish, with an interpreter on request at no charge.
  • Childcare at the back table, staffed by volunteers who already passed our screening.
  • 1,340 people cooked with us last year; 71% signed up for a second class.

First time here

Four Steps, Zero Paperwork

We ask one question: can you carry this home? Everything else — income, address, how many people count — is none of our business and none of it stands between you and dinner.

  1. Call Or Walk Up

    Ring (555) 014-3377 and a coordinator answers, or come to any stop on the schedule during its window.

  2. Tell Us Household Size

    A number is enough — “four of us, two little.” That decides box size and nothing else.

  3. Pick Your Groceries

    Walk the shelves or the van. Swap anything you will not eat for something you will; the stocker will help.

  4. Come Back When You Need To

    Every household may use a stop once a week and the walk-in pantry twice. There is no annual cap.

Bring This, If You Have It

  • Nothing is required. Not ID, not a bill, not a referral.
  • A reusable bag or wagon helps — a full family box weighs about 32 pounds.
  • Tell us about allergies, infant formula, or a soft-food diet at the counter and we will pack around it.
  • Bring the kids. There is a coloring table, a water fountain and nobody will shush them.

Can not get to a stop? Home delivery covers 860 households — call the same number and say “delivery.”

Refer A Neighbor

Teachers, nurses, clergy, case workers and apartment managers send us 41% of new households. Send a name and a phone number through your agency’s secure line, or call the pantry desk and leave it with a coordinator — we never tell your neighbor who mentioned them.
