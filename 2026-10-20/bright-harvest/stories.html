Story 01 · featured this month

“I Stopped Counting Days By Paycheck”

Home health aide, thirty-four, two kids, a car with a bad transmission and a route that never paid for the transmission.

“I started at the Two Rivers stop in February because a neighbor I did not know told me the van was there Tuesdays. I stood in the car for eleven minutes with the engine off before I walked in. I had a job. I did not think I was allowed.”

“The woman at the door asked my kids’ ages and what they would actually eat. That was the whole interview. My son will not touch anything with a green in it, so she put in the apples and the peanut butter and said, ‘you are the expert on your kid, not me.’ I cried in the parking lot of the Shell station and then I went to work.”

“The transmission got fixed in June. In April I did my first van shift, because I wanted to be the person who makes somebody else sit in a car for eleven minutes and then not have to. Now I do the second Tuesday and the last Saturday. My daughter thinks it is her club.”

Nobody has ever asked me for a single piece of paper. Not one.

Alma Requena

Marsh Landing · pantry member since February, van volunteer since April

Tell Us Yours

If you have used a stop, a box, a route, a class or a plot, we would like to print it. You choose your name, we never photograph you at a distribution, and you read every word before it goes up. Storytellers receive $150 and a copy of the printed volume.

  • Thirty minutes, at the pantry or on the phone.
  • You can withdraw the story at any time, forever.
  • Nothing you say affects the food you receive.

Twenty-eight years

How This Got Built

  1. 1998

    A Church Basement And A Station Wagon

    Six families in Ashford Falls start trading a Tuesday crate of seconds from the old A&P. First year total: 4,100 meals.

  2. 2003

    The First Van

    A donated step-van with a broken heater runs three stops to Two Rivers and Marsh Landing. It still runs two of them, Saturdays, driven by whoever is free.

  3. 2009

    Mill Race Road

    The county leases us the old textile warehouse for a dollar a year for twenty-five. We spend the first winter pouring floor drains.

  4. 2014

    We Take The Bags Away

    Pre-packed commodity bags end; shelves and choice begin. Reported food waste falls 31% in one quarter, and neighbors start coming more often because the food is finally theirs to pick.

  5. 2019

    School District Contract

    Twelve schools, 2,100 backpacks a week, and the consent policy that still governs every story on this page.

  6. 2020

    3.1 Million Meals In Curbside Trunks

    One hundred days of parking-lot distributions, two vans converted to contactless runs, and 94 new volunteers who are still here.

  7. 2023

    Forty-Eight Plots

    The gravel lot behind Dock 2 becomes garden ground. First harvest: 1.4 tons, most of it tomatoes we had to teach people to can.

  8. 2026

    The Second Walk-In

    Cold storage opens in August, which is why we can finally accept the dairy gift Riverbank has offered since 2021.
