§ 01

How a Term Works

Back room · p. 02

Four meetings, one shared book per month, and no obligation to have finished it.

  1. Pick a circle, not a book

    Each circle keeps its own taste and its own argument. Choose on the form below; if it is full we will put you on the waiting list and tell you honestly how long that tends to take — usually one meeting.

  2. The extract arrives by post

    Two weeks before the first meeting you get the opening pages printed on one side, plus a note from the convenor with the two questions they will ask. Nothing to buy on day one.

  3. Come down on the night

    Fourteen chairs, one long table, kettle already boiled. The convenor reads four minutes, then the table talks for ninety. If you have not finished the book, say so at the start; nobody will make it awkward.

  4. Keep or return the copy

    We hold six shop copies at half price for members and lend the rest. Whatever you decide, the circle is still yours next month, and you can switch circles at any term break.

Terms run September to December, January to March, and April to June. July and August is one outdoor meeting each — the cemetery read in 2024 is still the thing people mention to us most. Shop diary, September 2025

§ 05

Questions We Get at the Till

Notes · p. 27

Straight answers, in the order they arrive.

Q 01 What if I have not finished the book? +
Come anyway and say so in the first two minutes. Roughly a third of the table has not finished, every single week, and the conversation is better for the people who are reading it live rather than remembering it.
Q 02 Do I have to buy the book from you? +
No. Borrow one of the six shop copies, buy it secondhand, use the library, read it on a screen. We make our margin on the standing order and none of it depends on you at the meeting.
Q 03 I am shy. Will I be made to read aloud? +
Never without asking, and asking comes with a real option to say no. The convenor reads the opening four minutes. After that, listening is a completely normal way to spend ninety minutes, and several of our best contributors do it for two terms before they say anything.
Q 04 Can I join two circles? +
Yes, if you can face two evenings a fortnight. Nine people do. The Gothic Table and Verse Only overlap in taste more than in schedule, so that is the common pair.
Q 05 How do I get out of the basement? +
Whenever you like, without explaining. Say you are leaving, put your chair in, and go. Nobody remembers that you left; everybody remembers that you came.
Q 06 Is there an age limit? +
Sixteen for the evening circles — the youngest regular we have had was seventeen and out-argued Tomás about Le Guin. Under-twos go to Little Hands on Sunday mornings, and we would love a Saturday afternoon youth circle; ask us and help us start one.
