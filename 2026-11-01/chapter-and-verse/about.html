Chapter V · Colophon · Fourteen years, one lease

Twelve,Cormorant

We are a shop of two rooms, a basement and a window, run by four people who read too much and argue about it in public. This is the part we usually leave out.

§ 01

The First Lease

Prologue · p. 02

Nadia Ferreira on why the shop exists, in her own hand.

In 2011 I had eleven months of savings, a shelf in a bedroom, and a strong opinion that a bookshop should be able to tell you what it thinks. The unit at number twelve was a dry cleaner’s when I looked at it, and it still smelled of the pressing machine for the first winter, which the customers eventually said they liked.

The rule I wrote on the stockroom door in the first week is still the rule: we do not stock a book none of us has finished. It is a slow way to build a shop. It means our fiction table has eighteen titles where a chain store has four hundred, and it means that when we hand something across the counter we are not reciting a blurb — we are telling you what happened to us at chapter nine.

We nearly did not make it to the third year. In the spring of 2014 a clearance lot of sixty boxes of Gothic paperbacks came up for £340, and I bought them with the takings from Christmas rather than pay the rent early. That is how the back room became the reason people come from two counties away, and how we learned that the shop’s taste and the market’s taste are not the same sentence.

Fourteen years later we send mail within two and a half miles, we run six clubs, and we still close on Mondays to shelve. If you want to know what we think of a book, come in on a weekday morning. We will stop what we are doing.

The original stockroom door, with the rule written on it in pencil, is behind the till. It has been repainted twice and the pencil has been copied, not erased. That is the only artefact we have of the first year. Shown to anyone who asks, which is rarely
§ 02

A Short History of the Premises

Index of years · p. 11

Listed as an index, because that is how we keep it — in the ledger, in pencil.

  • 2011

    Doors open, 4 April

    One room, 2,400 titles, a secondhand trolley outside, and a signing by Tomás that we cannot read from the street. First customer: a woman looking for a book on beekeeping who left with a novel about beekeeping instead.

  • 2014

    The Gothic clearance and the back room

    Sixty boxes for £340. We knock through to the storage space behind, build nine shelves out of the old pressing benches, and never again run out of reasons for people to walk to the end of the street.

  • 2016

    Thursday Poetry begins

    Five people in a back room on a wet evening. It moves to the front room in its second year, when eleven of us could not fit, and has been held every Thursday except two since.

  • 2019

    The dawn reading, by accident

    A power cut at 06.40 in November meant Nadia and two customers stood in the half-light finishing Book 4 of Meditations. It is now the last Sunday of November, and people come in coats.

  • 2020

    Parcels, and the two-and-a-half-mile rule

    The shop closes for eleven weeks. We deliver by bicycle and on foot within two and a half miles, 1,806 parcels, and meet half of our current club members on the doorstep. The mail order stays after the doors reopen.

  • 2023

    The basement, and the six clubs

    Leila Marsh helps clear the cellar, we put in fourteen chairs, a loop, and a kettle that is louder than the radiator. The standing order becomes five circles, then six when the futures crowd asked nicely.

  • 2026

    Now

    Nine thousand, one hundred and forty titles, four booksellers, one apprentice on Tuesdays, 214 letter subscribers, and a window seat that has caused three arguments this year. The lease runs to 2031.

§ 03

Who Is in the Shop Today

Staff · p. 19

Ask for any of us by name. If we are not behind the counter we are shelving, and you are welcome to find us.

NF

Nadia Ferreira

Founder · Head of shelves · Gothic, Portuguese

Opened the shop with eleven months of savings and has been quietly furious about book covers ever since. Reads everything twice, sells everything once, and keeps the pencil rule on the stockroom door.

Ask her for: anything with a house in it that is worse than it seems.

TW

Tomás Whelan

Fiction, poetry, and the long shelf

Convenes Verse Only and has never let an ambiguous pronoun pass in fourteen years of Thursday nights. Keeps a notebook of first sentences, which he will show you if you ask and you have asked sincerely.

Ask him for: a novel that starts badly and ends unavoidably.

BO

Beatrice Osei

Children’s, world writing, translation

Runs Little Hands on Sunday mornings and the World Writing circle on Thursdays, and will tell you plainly that picture books are a harder literature than most prizes admit. Reads in four languages, argues in one.

Ask her for: a book in translation you would not have picked up.

WA

Will Adeyemi

Nonfiction, science, the ledger

Keeps the accounts, convenes Longform and The Long Way Round, and is the reason we know exactly which eighteen books lost money last term and why we are stocking them again.

Ask him for: reportage that reads like a thriller, and one very long book.

§ 05

What We Hold To

Colophon · p. 31

Seven lines, pinned inside the stockroom door since 2011.

  • A recommendation must be earned by having read the book, not by having received the proof copy.
  • Returns are a service, not an argument. If it did not work, bring it back and we will find the one that does.
  • The translator, the editor and the binder get named at the counter whenever we can.
  • No table in this shop is rented to a publisher. Nothing faces outward because somebody paid for it to.
  • A secondhand copy with somebody’s notes in it is worth more than a clean one, and we price it that way.
  • Independence means being able to say no to a title everybody is talking about, and yes to one nobody is.
  • The shop closes on Mondays so that the shelves get done properly. Nobody has ever minded.
Number four has cost us two large orders and one very polite threat. We have kept it for fourteen years and we intend to keep it for fourteen more. Repinning note, stockroom door, 2023

Access, and one honest caveat

The front room is level from the street through a 940mm doorway, with a wide aisle between the central table and the fiction wall — a wheelchair fits, and we will move the table if you call ahead. There is a hearing loop in the front room and the basement, and an accessible WC on the ground floor behind the counter; the key hangs by it.

The back room has one step of 120mm and the basement is fourteen stairs with a handrail on the right. If either of those is a problem, say so when you reserve: we move club nights upstairs whenever we can fit the chairs, and we have done it eleven times.

Quiet hours are 09.30 to 11.00 on Tuesdays and Thursdays — no events, no music, the lights at half. It was requested by three readers and it has become the calmest part of our week.

If you would like a book read to you, ask. We have done it four times, always in the window seat, and it takes about as long as you would expect. Shop rule no. 8, added 2022

“A shop is a sentence you can walk into. Come in, and we will finish it together.”
