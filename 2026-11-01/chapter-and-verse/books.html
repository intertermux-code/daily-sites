§ 03

How the Shop Is Bayed

Floor plan · p. 18

Nine bays, one rule: a title earns its place by being finished, not by being popular.

  1. Bay 1–2: Fiction, current and permanent

    New fiction faces outward on the central table; the permanent shelf behind the till holds the twelve books we will always carry, whatever the publisher does.

  2. Bay 3: Gothic & the uncanny

    Our founding collection, and the reason the shop survived its first winter. Shelved by century rather than author, because the atmosphere travels.

  3. Bay 4–5: Mystery, history, and the long nonfiction shelf

    Crime gets the end bay next to the till on purpose. Nonfiction is grouped by argument, not subject, so the beekeeping book sits beside the one on attention.

  4. Bay 6: World writing & translation

    Shelved by language of origin. We give the translator’s name on the card, and we mean it as the second recommendation.

  5. Bay 7–9: Science fiction, poetry, philosophy

    Poetry shares a wall with philosophy because both are read standing up, and both sell in the rain. Science fiction keeps its own bay and its own regulars.

A book comes off the shelf if it has not sold in eighteen months and none of us would fight for it. It goes back on if somebody asks. That has happened nine times, and we have never once regretted the second decision. Shop rule no. 4, pinned inside the stockroom door
