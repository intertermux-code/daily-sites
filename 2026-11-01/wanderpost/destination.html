    Where you sleep

    Two addresses, nineteen nights.

    Machiya townhouse, Higashiyama

    Built 1901, rebuilt 2016 by the family who run it. Six rooms around a courtyard with one zelkova tree, a deep tub, and a kitchen that opens at seven for rice and pickles. Four minutes on foot from Nanzen-ji's gate, eleven from the covered market.

    Nights 9Bath Cedar tubBreakfast Included

    Island guesthouse, Ashijima-cho

    An old sailmaker's house above the harbour, five rooms, run by Yoshie-san who cooks what the boats bring in. No television, one shared veranda, a boathouse you may borrow. The supermarket closed in 2019; the shop that replaced it sells miso, batteries and ice cream.

    Nights 10Dinner With hostsFerry 90 sec

    The shape of a day

    Six o'clock rice, one long walk, one evening with nothing in it.

    Slow travel is mostly a decision about when to stop adding things. Here is the template we build from, and the two half-days a week that belong entirely to you.

    On the island the rhythm changes only in one respect: the sea decides whether the boats go out, and we get up early regardless because the light is better.

    1. 06:30 — Rice

      Kitchen opens. Pickles, kombu, a boiled egg, and the newspaper no one has cleared away.

    2. 08:00 — One street

      A single lane, walked properly: the temple that opens at eight, the knife grinder, the second-hand bookshop with the cat.

    3. 12:00 — Lunch alone

      Unstructured. You get a shortlist of four places and no guide. This is the hour that makes a place yours.

    4. 15:00 — Craft

      Paper, indigo, joinery or lacquer — two hours at a working bench with someone who will not rush you.

    5. 18:30 — Table for six

      One long dinner, cooked by whoever is cooking that week. Wine is an afterthought; the pickles are the point.

    Language

    Six phrases, then silence

    The folio carries phrase cards written for this route, not for Japan in general. Haruko teaches the four that matter over breakfast on day two.

    Money

    Cash, and one 7-Eleven

    On the island there is a card reader in the shop and nowhere else. We carry a float for you; you will need about ¥6,000 of your own.

    Baggage

    One bag, two sizes

    A large case stays in Kyoto for the island nights. Everything you carry on the ferry fits under a seat — the reason we plan a fortnight in two places.

    How to behave hereEty

    Nine small things that make you a good guest

    1. Shoes come off at the genkan and stay off; step down onto the floor, not up.
    2. Photograph the garden from the veranda, never onto it.
    3. Inside a temple, the shutter noise is the rude part — turn the sound off before you lift the camera.
    4. Eat the thing you are handed before it cools; a cold sweet potato means you were expected.
    5. Say itadakimasu even when no one else does.
    6. On the train, the phone goes in your bag, not on silent.
    7. Baths are washed in, not washed with. Rinse first, then soak.
    8. Do not tip. It arrives back in your pocket within a week, with an apology.
    9. At the island table, ask Yoshie-san about the boat before you ask about the fish.

    Before you ask us

    Questions the Kyoto guide answers badly.

    Do I need to speak Japanese?

    No. Haruko and Yoshie-san cover the route; the folio carries the six phrases that get you a better seat, a later closing time, or a second helping. Twice a day you will be alone and mute, which is the point.

    How fit does this trip require?

    Up to fourteen kilometres on flat stone, three days of temple stairs, and one long harbour path with steps. No altitude. If you can manage a museum at your own pace for four hours you will manage this.

    Is cherry blossom season worth it?

    Not at the fare we can get you. Early April pushes crowd pressure to 94 out of 100 and rooms to nearly double. We send people in late March for the plum blossoms instead, which are prettier and quieter — see the dial above.

    What about the fish market? It has moved.

    Yes — Inner Kyobashi since 2017, and the famous morning auction is now closed to visitors. We go to the outer wholesale streets at 06:40 instead, where the tuna still comes in by hand.

    Can we extend on the island?

    Add up to five nights; Yoshie-san has two spare rooms and no other bookings before December. Tell us at folio stage, not after — the ferry slots are held with the rooms.

    Ready to read the day-by-day plan?

    Nineteen nights, two bases, and every pencilled transfer on one page.
