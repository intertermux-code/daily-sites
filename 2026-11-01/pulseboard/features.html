Features

Four surfaces. One honest number.

Pulseboard is built so that a founder, an analyst, and an executive open the same metric and reach the same conclusion. Each surface below exists to remove a step between the question and the answer.

01 — Live dashboards

Boards that update while you argue about them.

Every tile is a live subscription, not a snapshot. Open a board in a review and the numbers keep moving; share a link and your colleague lands on the same view, the same filters, the same minute of data.

  • Tiles stream at one-second granularity. Lag is shown on the tile itself, so nobody quotes stale data.
  • Composable layouts. Six tile types — trend, funnel, cohort grid, distribution, table, narrative — on a 12-column canvas.
  • Pinned comparisons. Compare against any prior window, segment, or experiment arm and keep that comparison as the default view.
  • Readable on a phone. Boards reflow into a single column with the narrative tile first, because that is the part people need on the move.
02 — Anomaly signals

The regression you find before your customers do.

Pulseboard learns the shape of each metric — weekday rhythm, launch spikes, the quarterly dip — and alerts only when the deviation survives that context. Each alert arrives with the evidence you would have dug for yourself.

  • Automatic decomposition. The alert names the segment responsible: plan, country, browser, referral, or release cohort.
  • Severity you set. Choose the business threshold — “warn me at a two-point drop in trial conversion” — not a sigma you have to interpret.
  • Delivery where work happens. Slack channels, PagerDuty, email digest, or a webhook into your own runbook tooling.
  • Feedback loop. Mark an alert useful or noisy, and detection recalibrates for that metric within a day.
03 — Cohort notebooks

Analyses that survive the analyst leaving.

A notebook is a document with executable cells: prose, the query, the chart, and the conclusion in one place. Re-run it a year later and it recomputes against today’s data, or freezes at the date you published it.

  • SQL and blocks side by side. Write the query when the visual builder cannot express the question.
  • Assumptions are first-class. Every notebook records filters, exclusions, and caveats in a block reviewers can check off.
  • Publish to a board tile. Turn the finding into a standing metric with one click from the notebook cell.
  • Comments with context. Threads anchor to a cell, so the debate stays attached to the evidence.
04 — Governed metrics

One definition, owned by someone with a name.

The metric layer is the product. Every definition has an owner, a version history, and a dependency graph, so “active user” means one thing across every board, alert, notebook, and export.

  • Change review. Edits to a governed metric open a diff with the downstream impact list before they can be merged.
  • Backfill aware. Republishing a definition backfills history and marks the restated range on affected charts.
  • Row and column controls. Restrict revenue or health data by team, region, or role without duplicating the pipeline.
  • Audit trail, exportable. Who viewed, changed, or published what, retained for as long as your compliance team asks.
Under the hood

Details operators care about, kept out of the way.

Six things that decide whether an analytics tool survives its second quarter.

Query engine

Columnar scans over your warehouse with a 40 ms hot path for cached series. Ten years of monthly retention renders in under two seconds on a billion-row table.

Identity resolution

Deterministic joins on your user key, with an optional probabilistic layer for anonymous web traffic. Merge and split events are replayable, never silent.

Warehouse-native mode

Run compute in Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, or Databricks. Raw events never leave your storage; Pulseboard stores definitions and result caches only.

Collection without chaos

A typed event SDK with a schema registry. Rename a property and the migration plan shows which boards break before you deploy.

Everywhere delivery

Embed boards in your own product with signed tokens, export series to CSV or API, and schedule PDF briefs to execs who never log in.

Security posture

SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, SSO and SCIM provisioning, EU or US data residency, and per-workspace encryption keys on the Enterprise plan.

Integrations

It joins the stack you already justified.

Two-way connectors keep identity, experimentation, and incident context in sync, so Pulseboard is the reading room rather than another silo.

A week on Pulseboard

From Monday’s alert to Friday’s decision.

Mon

A signal fires at 07:12: trial conversion down 2.4 points, attributed to the EU on the new checkout release. The alert links straight to the affected tile.

Tue

The team opens a notebook from the alert. Two cells confirm the drop is payment-method specific, not geography, once the cohort is split.

Wed

Engineering ships the fix. The notebook’s conclusion becomes a standing tile so payment-method conversion is watched permanently.

Fri

The weekly brief writes itself from the board, the notebook, and the metric changelog. Nobody reconciles spreadsheets.
