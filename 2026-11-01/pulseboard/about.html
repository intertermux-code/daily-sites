Our story

We built the tool we needed at 3 a.m.

Pulseboard began as a shared spreadsheet kept by three on-call engineers, listing which metric meant what. By the time we deleted it, we knew what the product had to be: a place where a number carries its own explanation.

Two years of dashboards, one honest sentence.

Our founding team came from measurement-heavy products: a payments company where conversion meant fraud rules, a streaming service where retention meant buffering, and a data platform where nobody could say how many active workspaces there were. That last one was the moment.

The problem was never computing the number. It was agreeing on it, then remembering the agreement. Every quarter the definition drifted, a new hire rebuilt the same chart with different exclusions, and two teams presented different truths to the same board.

So Pulseboard puts the definition first. The board, the alert, and the notebook are all readings of one governed layer — which is why the site is quiet. Calm is a feature when the alternative is an argument in a meeting room.

How it grew

Seven years, in the order it happened.

2019

A spreadsheet with three owners

Dana Okafor and Ilian Petrov start keeping a shared list of metric definitions during an on-call rotation at a data platform company. It reaches four hundred rows.

2021

The first governed layer

A prototype runs queries against a customer warehouse and refuses to render a chart whose definition has no owner. That refusal becomes the product’s core rule.

2022

Signals replace the daily scrub

Seasonality-aware detection ships after six months of false-positive tuning with eleven design partners. Alert precision settles at 96 percent.

2024

Notebooks, and the first enterprise workspace

Analysts ask for prose next to queries. Nine months later a health-tech customer passes a security review and Pulseboard gains EU residency.

2026

Four hundred product organisations

The company stays deliberately small: thirty-one people, profitable since 2023, with a roadmap driven by the support queue rather than a forecast.

Principles

Four rules we have not broken yet.

They are on the wall of every planning session, and they cost us deals regularly.

A number without an owner is a rumour

Pulseboard will not render a metric that nobody is accountable for. We have held this line since the first prototype, including against customers who wanted it off.

Explain before you decorate

Every visual earns its place by answering a question. If a chart cannot state the question in its title, it does not ship — which is why our boards look quiet.

The customer’s warehouse is the source of truth

We would rather be slow than copy your raw events into our storage. Warehouse-native mode exists because the trust argument always ends there.

Answer in the support queue, twice a day

Engineers rotate on tickets. No question waits more than four business hours, and the queue is read as a team every Friday before the roadmap changes.
