General dentistry is a wide field, and the honest version of it is that no single person is excellent at everything. Dr Ferreira does not place implants and Dr Osei does not line up aligners. What you get here is a small practice that behaves like a clinic: each clinician stays close to the work they do most, and refers the rest to the colleague three doors away.

That structure has a practical benefit for you. The person treating your gums has done that exact procedure several times a week for years, and the person reviewing your implant has audited their own ten-year survival rate — it is 96.4%, published above, because measuring it is how you know whether to keep doing it.

Every clinician here holds full General Dental Council registration, carries indemnity cover, and completes a minimum of forty hours of continuing professional development a year, fifteen of them face-to-face. Two of us also teach: Dr Osei supervises implant postgraduates at the University of Bristol dental school on Fridays, and Dr Almeida runs the safeguarding training for the whole practice.

03

Four rules the practice runs on

Not values on a poster — the specific commitments we are audited against, and you are entitled to hold us to.

01

Consent is a conversation

Nothing begins in the same appointment it was first discussed, unless you ask for it to and the situation is urgent. You get alternatives, including the option of doing nothing, and the cost of each.

02

No treatment targets

No clinician here has a sales target, a bonus tied to spend, or a plan they must fill. Pay is salaried and reviewed annually against clinical audit, not against turnover.

03

We publish what fails

Implant survival, restoration longevity and our own complaint numbers appear on this site each January, including the years that were worse than average.

04

Second opinions are free

We will send your scans, photographs and plan to any practice you choose, at our own cost, and we will not ask why. Referrals are made to people, not through brokers.

Governance

Monthly clinical audit

Radiograph quality, endodontic case selection, restoration failures, and infection-prevention swabs — minuted, and the minutes are available on request.

Training

40 hours CPD a year

Fifteen of them face-to-face, as required by the GDC scheme. Two of us also teach at Bristol dental school.

Safeguarding

Level 3 lead

Whole-team training refreshed annually, with named local authority contacts for both children and adults at risk.

04

Work with us

We advertise once a year, hire slowly, and keep a standing register for hygienists and coordinators.

Currently recruiting: one part-time hygienist (two or three days, Wednesdays essential), and a second treatment coordinator to share the recall list. Both roles are salaried with a protected CPD day each month and a £1,200 training budget.

We do not use commission, and we do not ask clinicians to hit an uplift target. Dentists joining the practice are expected to declare one special interest within their first year and to give two in-practice teaching sessions annually — one to the clinical team, one to the reception team, because the second one matters more than people think.
