    Go to A&E or call 999 instead if

    • Swelling is closing your eye or throatDifficulty swallowing, breathing, or opening your mouth is a medical emergency, not a dental one.
    • Bleeding will not stop after ten minutes Firm pressure with a clean folded towel, ten minutes without peeking, then 999 if it continues.
    • Face or jaw after significant trauma A fall from height, a collision, or a jaw that will not close properly needs imaging tonight.
    • Fever with a dental infection Temperature above 38.5°C with facial swelling needs same-day medical assessment.
    Knocked-out adult tooth

    An hour makes the difference

    Handle it by the crown, never the root. Rinse in milk for ten seconds, try to put it back in the socket, bite on a clean handkerchief, and get here. If it will not go back, carry it in milk — not wrapped in tissue, and not in water.

    Broken tooth

    Save the fragment

    A chip kept in milk or saliva can often be bonded back on the same day, which is faster, cheaper and better-looking than rebuilding it from scratch.

    Lost filling or crown

    Bring it with you

    Do not use household glue. Keep the crown in a bag, avoid chewing on that side, and we re-cement it in about twenty minutes in most cases (£78–£110).

    Toothache at night

    Pain that wakes you

    Usually means the nerve is dying rather than infected. Ibuprofen if you can take it, nothing harder than a cold flannel on the cheek, and we can usually make it comfortable in a single thirty-minute visit (£85 then £145).

    Access & inclusion

    What is actually in the building

    Tell us what you need when you book, and it will be there. We would rather ask twice than have you white-knuckle an appointment.

    • Step-free all the wayPavement to the second floor by lift; the door openings are 950mm and the hygiene suite has a 900mm chair-side gap for a wheelchair transfer.
    • Hearing loopAt the reception desk and in both consultation rooms. Ask for the “T” setting on your hearing aid and a coordinator will check it with you.
    • BSL and interpretersBooked through our video-interpreting service with 48 hours’ notice, at no cost to you. Ask reception and they will arrange it on the call.
    • Large print and brailleConsent forms, price lists and home-care sheets are available in 18pt large print and on request in braille or audio.
    • Support animals welcomeGuide dogs and assistance dogs are welcome in every room. Water bowls by the door; two of our clinicians have their own dogs in the waiting area most Thursdays.
    • Quiet and sensory-considerateA low-stimulation waiting room with no music, a separate entrance available on request, and appointments scheduled outside school-run and lunchtime noise.
    • Gender and nameYour record uses the name and pronouns you give us, not the ones on a document. We ask at registration rather than assume.
    • Chaperones alwaysEvery clinician will treat you with a chaperone present if you ask, at any appointment, with no explanation needed and no charge.

    04

    Complaints, records and your information

    Three things people most often need from us in writing, and exactly how to get each one.

    Complaints

    Nine last year, eight resolved

    Send it to complaints@lumident.co.uk or ask reception for the paper form. You get an acknowledgement within two working days, a named person investigating, and a written answer within fifteen working days. If you are not satisfied, the Practice Standards Scheme will review it independently, free of charge.

    Clinical records

    Yours, within five working days

    Under UK GDPR you can have copies of your notes, charts, photographs and radiographs. Email records@lumident.co.uk; there is no fee for your own copy, and we will send them straight to another practice if you prefer.

    Privacy

    No marketing you did not ask for

    We hold treatment records for eleven years after your last visit, as dental indemnity requires. Contact details are kept for two years after that unless you ask us to delete them sooner. We never sell data, never use it for advertising audiences, and appointment reminders are the only texts we send.

    If you are unhappy with something that happened here, say it at the desk while you are still in the building. Most of our nine complaints last year were things a clinician could have fixed with a phone call the same afternoon, and one of them genuinely was. Writing to us takes longer; ring 0117 496 0284 and ask for the practice manager if you would rather talk it through first.
