Seven Neighbours, Told in Their Own Words

Every story here ran past the person in it twice: once to agree to be asked, once to approve the final wording. Some changed their names, one kept theirs, and none of them read like a grant report — because they aren’t.

  • Written with consent, reviewed by the speaker
  • No children’s faces, no addresses
  • Updated October 2026

Showing 7 of 7 stories

  1. 01

    Pantry shopper · Riverton

    “I Stopped Doing the Arithmetic at the Checkout”

    Marisol took work-related redundancy in 2024 and spent four months subtracting vegetables from her grocery list. She started at the Riverton pantry on a Monday because the sign said open and meant it — no referral, no form. Her household now takes a full crate each week and she has moved to the twice-monthly slot.

    “Nobody has asked me why I’m here, not once. That question is the part that used to keep me home.”

    Told to us in July 2026 · approved as written

  2. 02

    Dock volunteer · three years

    “Thursday Is the Hour My Week Turns Around”

    Dez came along because a neighbour needed a lift, signed up for one shift to be polite, and is now the person who trains new volunteers on the scanner log. He has logged 612 hours and refuses to miss the 6pm wrap, when the crew splits the day’s rejects into staff-and-volunteer bags.

    “I can tell you which farm’s sweet corn goes soft fastest. That is the most useful trivia I own.”

    Logged hours through September 2026

  3. 03

    Partner grower · Riverbend Farms

    “We Stopped Ploughing Under the Ugly Ones”

    Riverbend had always tilled in produce that failed supermarket sizing. In 2019 Bright Harvest started collecting it at cost-plus-handling; last season the farm sent 31 tonnes that would otherwise have become field matter, and paid nothing for the pickup.

    “A two-tonne loss used to be a Tuesday. Now it’s somebody’s month of vegetables.”

    Grower account, audited figures for 2025

  4. 04

    Grow plot household · Kestrel Row

    “My Kids Thought Tomatoes Came From a Box”

    The Kwan family took bed 41 in April with no gardening experience between four people. By August they were harvesting twice a week, eating the runner beans their daughter refused in March, and giving two crates of cucumbers back to the pantry shelf.

    “The bed is ten by four. What grew in it is bigger than that.”

    Names used with the family’s written permission

  5. 05

    Van runner · North loop

    “I Drive to Three Towns and Learn Everyone’s Order”

    Tomás Liggett took the van licence check as a volunteer and now runs Thursday’s north loop eight months a year. He knows Ash Barn wants leafy greens before squash, and that Hollow Creek’s stop has to finish by 5:15 or the shift ends in the dark.

    “Nine hours in a cold truck, and I have never once felt like I was wasting the day.”

    Route log, 44 Thursdays in 2026

  6. 06

    Cookshop graduate · Fairview

    “I Came for the Free Groceries. I Stayed to Teach.”

    Nadia Belov attended six Saturday cookshops as a shopper, then asked to help with prep. She now runs the first thirty minutes of the kids’ session, using the celeriac recipe that finally made the vegetable work at home for her family.

    “Everybody in that kitchen has been hungry. It changes how you explain a knife.”

    Interviewed after the September class

  7. 07

    Donor manager · Riverton office

    “My Job Is Turning Dollars Into a Wednesday Plan”

    Priya Raman reconciles gifts against the shortage board each Monday, which is why our buy orders track real gaps rather than whatever a warehouse happens to have too much of. She also writes the monthly letter that shows the misses as well as the wins.

    “If a donor can’t see what changed hands, they shouldn’t have to guess. So we print it.”

    Staff profile, published with approval

Our story rules

How a Story Gets Published Here

Ask for the consent form→

  • Asked Twice, Never Assumed

    First we ask whether someone would talk; then we ask again after they have read the finished draft. A no at either stage ends it, and the shopper’s next visit is unaffected either way.

  • They Choose the Words

    Anyone can strike a line, change a name, or refuse a photo. Two of the seven profiles above use a chosen name, and we list that fact under the story rather than hiding it.

  • Hard Limits on Privacy

    No children’s faces, no home addresses, no immigration status, no health detail. Nothing that could follow a person into a job interview or a landlord’s office.

Your turn, if you want it

Tell Us What Changed on Your Table

Some of the best stories here started as a note on the comment card by the soup urn. You do not need to be eloquent, and you do not need to use your real name.

  • Email stories@brightharvest.org — a few sentences is plenty.
  • Ask a host for the comment card and write it at the table.
  • Prefer to speak? We record by phone in English, Spanish, or Portuguese.
