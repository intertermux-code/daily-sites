What we run

Four Programs, One Simple Rule: Take What You’ll Actually Eat

Everything below is free, open to anyone in Riverton, Fairview, or Old Mill county, and run without a means test. We started with one folding table in 2011; this is the shape the work settled into.

  • No ID, no forms, no income proof
  • Spoken: English, Spanish, Portuguese, ASL
  • Step-free at every site

Program 01

Market-Style Pantries

Three permanent pantries laid out like a small grocery: produce case, dry aisles, frozen, dairy, baby, and a household shelf with soap, toothpaste, and pet food. You walk in with a bag and choose.

Shoppers take a median 19 pounds per visit, of which 11 pounds are fresh produce — roughly double what a pre-packed hamper used to move through this building.

  • Riverton — 118 Dockside Lane, Mon/Tue/Thu/Sat
  • Fairview — St. Anne’s hall car park, Wed/Fri
  • Old Mill — Library back lot, Tue/Sat

Program 02

Mobile Harvest Vans

Two refrigerated 14-foot vans carry the pantry to places where the nearest grocer is more than fourteen miles away. They park for ninety minutes, unload a produce table, and stay long enough that people actually chat.

Every route stops at a benefit-enrolment desk and a hot-drinks urn. Last season 61% of van shoppers had never visited a fixed pantry.

  • North loop — Hollow Creek, Ash Barn, Two Rivers (Thu)
  • South loop — Old Mill Fields, Kestrel Row, Fairview Gardens (Sun)
  • Campus stops — six apartment towers with no corner store (Wed evenings)

Program 03

Kitchen Cookshops

Ninety minutes, four burners, whatever came off the van that morning. You cook five weeknight meals from one basket, eat them together, and take the printed recipe card home in your own language.

Childcare sits in the next room and no one needs to know how to use a knife. Two graduates now run the kids’ session themselves.

  • Free, with a take-home ingredient bag for each recipe
  • Wheelchair-height workstations at both teaching kitchens
  • Low-alert sessions the first Saturday of each month, no music, dim pots, ten seats

Program 04

Grow-Your-Own Plots

Eighty raised beds on two donated lots, seeded and fed by us, harvested by you. Each household gets a ten-by-four bed, a tool locker, and a mentor who has grown tomatoes in this soil for nine seasons.

Plots stay open through November with row cover, and the surplus goes straight back to the pantry instead of composting.

  • Kestrel Row lot — 54 beds, water on site, bus 12 stops at the gate
  • Dockside roof — 26 beds, lift access, seedlings delivered weekly
  • Waitlist — currently 2 weeks; we never charge for a bed

How these four came to be

Fifteen Years of Adding Doors, Not Rules

Each program exists because someone knocked and asked for something we were not offering. Here is that line, in order.

  1. 2011

    One Folding Table

    Founder Alma Reyes starts a Saturday produce table outside the Riverton laundromat with a pickup truck and eleven crates from Green Valley Co-op.

    38 neighbours, first Saturday

  2. 2014

    The Cooler We Didn’t Plan For

    A donated 40-foot refrigerated container lets us hold leafy greens for the first time. Produce volume triples in one quarter because we can finally say yes to a farm’s Tuesday.

    First paid warehouse lead

  3. 2017

    Pantries Become Markets

    After shoppers told us hampers wasted half their contents, we rebuilt distribution as grocery aisles. Food waste inside our system drops 31% and dignity complaints go to zero.

    Aisle layout still in use

  4. 2020

    Vans Replace Queues

    Indoor gathering stops; school kitchens close. We lease two refrigerated vans, drive curbside, and end up keeping the routes — the rural demand was real and permanent.

    18 stops, still running

  5. 2023

    Cookshops and the Plot Program

    Shoppers asked what to do with celeriac; neighbours asked for ground of their own. Both answers now run weekly, taught by people who were on the receiving end two years earlier.

    80 beds · 2 teaching kitchens

  6. 2026

    Building the Fourth Wall

    Ground-breaking in March on a combined Riverton hub: cooler, pantry, teaching kitchen, and covered van bay under one roof, with a bus shelter at the door.

    Opens spring 2028

Getting help

Eligibility Is One Sentence Long

Ask about a site near you→

  • Walk in

    No ID, no proof of income, no reason given. If you live in Riverton, Fairview, or Old Mill county and could use food, you qualify — and you can come every time the doors are open.

  • Take what you’ll eat

    Shop the aisles and pick your own. A four-person household typically leaves with 19 pounds. Dietary lines, baby food, and pet food sit in the same room, not a back office.

  • Stay as long as you like

    Most neighbours visit twice a month; some visit weekly through winter. Nobody is time-limited off the program, and nobody is asked to prove they still need it.
