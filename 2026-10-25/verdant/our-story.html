Chapter 01 — Fernleigh Farm

It started with a bench, a thermometer, and twelve trays of clover.

Verdant is not a lab that buys botanicals. It is a four-acre garden in the Columbia River floodplain that happens to run a lab, and the difference shows up in the harvest date printed on the base of every bottle.

The origin

Why a garden, and not a warehouse

Naomi Ashe spent nine years writing formulas for houses that bought their actives by the drum. The ingredient lists were long, the story was purchased from a broker, and nothing in the bottle could be traced further than the co-packer. In 2019 she took a leased bench at Fernleigh Farm, a produce market garden outside St. Helens, Oregon, with one question: could a skincare line be planned the way a vegetable bed is planned — variety, season, yield, and a reason for being there.

The first season was eleven failures. Calendula extracted at the wrong hour turned brown and smelled of hay. A clay mask that felt luxurious in March cracked like a sidewalk in August. But two formulas survived the winter unchanged — the clover cleanser and the oat essence — and those two are still the highest repurchase rate in the line seven years later.

Today the farm grows eight of the eleven botanicals we use. The other three come from growers we visit once a year and name on the label, because a plant that travels two thousand miles should at least arrive with an address.

02 — The ingredient index

Eight plants, and what each one earns its place for

A plant belongs in a formula for one of two reasons: it measurably does something, or it makes the thing that does something tolerable. Everything below clears one of those bars, with the concentration we publish on the carton.

Clover

Trifolium pratense

2.5% isoflavone extract

Our cleanser’s quiet workhorse. Isoflavones keep the gel soft on the skin while the surfactants lift sunscreen — the reason nothing here leaves you reaching for moisturiser within the minute.

Calendula

Calendula officinalis

4% flower water

Picked at first light when the resin content peaks. In the Cushion Cream it calms the flush that reactive skin shows after actives; the same flower water forms the base of three other formulas.

Oxalis / Sorrel

Oxalis triangularis

1.8% leaf ferment

A gentle natural source of oxalate-adjacent acids at a concentration low enough to polish without the sting of a laboratory peel. It is the reason the Night Balm smooths rather than strips.

Vervain

Verbena officinalis

12% vitamin C complex

Verbena extract carries the brightening work alongside a stabilised ascorbyl glucoside, so the drops stay clear for twelve weeks on the shelf instead of turning rust-coloured in six.

Avenue Oat

Avena sativa

4% ferment, 3% beta-glucan

Fermented for nine days, then cold-filtered. This is the essence — the layer that makes a routine absorb rather than sit on the surface, and the most-repurchased bottle in the line.

Wood Fern

Dryopteris filix-mas

6% kaolin complex

Lentil-sized clay held at riverbank softness with glycerin. Fern extract supplies the antioxidant layer so the mask doesn’t oxidise on your face between the eighth and twelfth minute.

Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare

3% seed extract, 92% squalane

Sugarcane-derived squalane, not shark or olive, because it lands lighter and never comedogenic. Fennel seed extract gives the Seal its faint warm scent without an essential-oil load.

Sheet Moss

Hypnum cupressiforme

8% moss ferment

A bryophyte that holds twenty times its weight in water — which is exactly what it does on your cheeks. Harvested from the hedgerow margins, never the beds, and re-tested each season for metals.

03 — Season notes

Ninety minutes from bed to beaker

Fig. 1 — four seasons of one garden

Extraction happens within ninety minutes of cutting because the volatile fraction drops off faster than any preservative can compensate. It is an inefficient, stubborn rule and it is the single largest reason our formulas smell faintly green in April and faintly honeyed in September — the same batch notes, different weather.

04 — How we got here

Seven harvests, unchanged rules

2019

One bench and eleven failures

Naomi leases a market-garden bench, grows twelve trays of clover, and writes a cleanser that survives its first winter.

2020

Four formulas, shipped by hand

Orders go out from the farm shed. The first refill request — from a customer who mailed back three bottles unasked — becomes a program.

2021

The lab moves on-site

A converted dairy room becomes the extraction lab, cutting field-to-beaker time from six hours to ninety minutes.

2022

Concentrations go on the label

Every active percentage is printed on the carton. Two distributors drop us that quarter; repurchases rise 34% by year end.

2023

Third-party batch testing

Purity, heavy metals and preservative efficacy are tested independently. Certificates become searchable by batch code.

2024

The ten-formula rule

We cap the line at ten and retire two toners to make room for the SPF. Nothing has been added since without a retirement.

2026

Glass back home

63% of customers now order pouches. Four returned vessels earns a free pour, and the fleet of empties in the shed finally has a purpose.

05 — Exclusions

What never goes into a bottle

This list is longer than most brands’ highlight reels on purpose. If a supplier offers us a cheaper version of something here, the answer doesn’t change.

  • Denatured alcohol above 2% in any leave-on formula
  • Synthetic fragrance and any “parfum” line item
  • Dyes — the green in the glass is the plant, not a colourant
  • Microplastic beads or silicone elastomer blends
  • Essential oils over 0.4% in the two scented formulas
  • Retinyl palmitate and any photo-unstable vitamin A ester in morning steps
  • Bamboo or coconut-derived powders sold as “natural exfoliants” with sharp fracture edges
  • Palm-derived surfactants, and the certificates that pretend otherwise

06 — The bench

Nine people, and the same three questions

Verdant employs four growers, three formulators, one packer and one person who answers your email. Before anything reaches the shop, it has to survive the same three questions asked out loud in the dairy room.

Can we grow or name it?

If we cannot point to the bed, the hedgerow, or the grower we visited this year, the ingredient doesn’t go in.

Would we notice its absence?

A formula is cut back until removing one more thing would be visible on the skin. That is where we stop.

Can we defend the number?

Every published percentage has a stability study and an external certificate attached to the exact batch you receive.
