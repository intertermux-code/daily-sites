The Long Story, Short

Five pieces our editors were arguing about this week. Open one at a time — the panel expands with the lead.

We spent six weeks asking working engineers one narrow question: where, exactly, does a model earn its keep in your day? Forty answered. Almost none of them described the revolution the keynote promises — and almost all of them described something real. The pattern is unglamorous, expensive, and quietly reshaping who gets hired.

A wafer fab takes four years to permit and three to fill. This one has done half of that in eighteen months, funded by a state that has never built a fab before. We looked at the water rights, the tax abatements and the two thousand jobs already promised to people who have not yet been hired.

We captured ninety days of telemetry from eleven consumer routers sold in the European Union. Six shipped diagnostic payloads containing timestamps, device lists and signal maps — all of it permitted by the terms nobody opens. Two of the vendors told us they had never considered the data sensitive.
