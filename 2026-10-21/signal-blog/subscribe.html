What Arrives in the Inbox

Five fixed sections, in the same order, every week since 2016.

Section One

The Lead

This week’s long read, plus what we cut and why. Always written by the reporter on the story, never summarised by a desk editor who was not in the room.

Section Two

The Working Notes

Filings, datasets and the specific documents that moved a story forward — or did not. Subscribers get the notebook templates on request.

Section Three

Killed On The Desk

The pitches we could not prove, named honestly. Roughly one in four of these turns into a published story later.

Section Four

Corrections & Follow-Ups

Every correction from the previous week, at the top of the letter rather than buried at the bottom of a page.

Section Five

Your Replies

Three or four answers from readers, printed with permission. The desk is small enough that this is genuinely a conversation.

Sample · Issue 470

Thursday 15 October: What a Datasheet Does Not Say

Hello from a warehouse outside Columbus, where five megawatts of sodium-ion cells have been running for nineteen months and nobody in the industry will tell you what “running” means.

In this letter: why we spent two weeks reading thirty-four degradation curves instead of one, the audit number that finally made a cooperative change its contract language, and the flow battery in Chile that is not an arbitrage story at all.

Killed on the desk: a piece on a solid-state startup whose production date we could not pin to a document. Corrections: one, minor, and it moved the comparison table in Tuesday’s explainer.
