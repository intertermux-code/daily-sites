The house

Fourteen acres of history, two of them genuinely flat

Harbor Light was built in 1911 for a ship chandler who wanted to see every mast in his harbour from his breakfast table. A century later that single instinct still organises the whole house: nothing important faces the wrong way.

The 2016 renovation kept the central hall, the original pine floors and both marble fireplaces, then rebuilt everything behind them — new roof slate, geothermal loops under the south lawn, a wired cellar, and a kitchen wing that can feed twenty people without anyone walking through a living room.

What remains rare is the ground. Two flat, buildable acres run down to a bulkhead with 180 feet of frontage and a dock rated for a 55-footer, and the boathouse has its own well and a heated floor. On Old Harbor Point, that combination has not come to market since 2009.

The guest wing

Room One

The guest wing runs the whole east side

Four suites, each with its own bath and a shared sitting room at the top of the stair. Independent HVAC, its own entrance from the courtyard, and the kind of quiet that makes people stay a week longer than they meant to.

Room Two

The library was the chandler's chart room

Ceiling-height walnut shelving survives from 1911, along with the brass rail that held his harbour maps. It reads as the oldest room in the house because it is — and the only one whose joiner is buried in the churchyard up the lane.

Room Three

A kitchen built by people who actually cook

Twelve feet of honed stone bench, a gas range facing the windows, a scullery behind with its own dishwasher and cold storage, and a pantry that holds a month of provisioning. The service door lands at the courtyard, not the front hall.

Room Four

Ten bathrooms, and the marble is real marble

Six full baths on the principal level, four more above, plus two powder rooms. The primary bath takes the whole corner of the 1911 addition: freestanding tub under a west window, heated stone, and a door that locks properly.

Old Harbor Point

Nine minutes from the boatyard, forty from the city

The Point is a single road in, with a gate the neighbours maintain themselves. There is no through traffic and no storefront; the post office closed in 1998 and everyone still collects at the box cluster by the chapel.

Low tide at the bulkhead is 6.1 feet, which means a deep-draft boat leaves when it likes. The channel marker off the point is the one the harbourmaster watches, and the fog here arrives about twenty minutes after it reaches the town.
