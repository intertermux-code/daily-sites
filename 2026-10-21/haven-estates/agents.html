Inside the office

Why we stay six

01

One advisor, one client

House Rule One

Nobody works on more than fourteen live relationships

When an advisor hits thirteen, the office stops giving them new ones. That cap is the whole difference between us and the franchise down the road: it is why your advisor answers, remembers, and has actually been inside the houses they recommend.

House Rule Two

Thursdays, 8 a.m., over coffee and the week's failures

Every deal that slipped gets taken apart in front of everyone, including the junior. Delanor has stood in that room and explained a valuation she got wrong. It is the fastest way we know of making six people into one office.

House Rule Three

We do not pay each other for introductions

If your advisor sends you to Theo for a Kingsmere co-op, nobody takes a cut and nobody gets a say in your offer. Referrals inside the firm are free, which means the only reason to make one is that it is right for you.

What an advisor actually does

Four jobs, done by the same person

Valuation

A number, with receipts

Three comparables we can name, the adjustments we made, and the reason your house is not the one on the corner. Free, and yours to keep.

Preparation

Six weeks of tidying

Photographer at the right hour, drainage fixed before anyone asks, permits found and closed out. We pay for it up front and take it back at completion.

Negotiation

One voice on the phone

Your advisor sits in every conversation from first offer to final walk-through. No transaction coordinator reading your terms off a screen for the first time.

Aftercare

The number you keep

Caretaker, roofer, arborist, and a call in November when the ice comes out. Amina owns all of it, and her door is unlocked on Fridays.

Careers

We are not hiring. Ask again in January.

A seat opens when someone leaves, and it has taken two years on average. If you sell houses on this bay and want a smaller desk with better conversations on it, send us one page about your last three closings.
