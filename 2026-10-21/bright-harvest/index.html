How it works

Three moves, from a field that over-yielded to a kitchen that needs it.

A grower calls us with a load that will not sell — too ripe, too odd-shaped, or simply more than the market ordered. We pick it up the same afternoon, bring it to the Orchard Lane depot, and sort it into family boxes before the next morning.

  1. Rescue the surplus

    Two refrigerated vans run fixed routes to 212 partner farms, grocers, and bakeries. Anything edible that would be composted comes home with us instead.

  2. Sort it with care

    Volunteers check every item against a date and quality standard a grocery store would be embarrassed to serve below. Roughly 6% still goes to our compost partners.

  3. Hand it over, no questions

    Neighbours choose their own groceries at one of seven distribution points, or have a box delivered when leaving the house is not possible.

Our promise

We would not hand a neighbour anything we would not serve our own family.

“Hunger is not a character flaw and it is not permanent. Most of the people here are one car repair away from being fine — and every single one of them deserves to pick their own vegetables.”

That standard is written into our sourcing policy, our volunteer training, and the way the shelves are laid out. Read the full account of where food comes from and where it goes in our community stories.
