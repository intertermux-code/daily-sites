Four hours on a Tuesday packs 620 meals.

Six thousand nine hundred neighbours volunteer with Bright Harvest — students, retirees, two fire crews, and a choir that comes on the third Sunday of every month. Pick the shift that fits your week. Training is free, coffee is on, and nobody will ask you to lift anything you do not want to.

Five ways to help

Every role here is real, recurring, and needs someone on it this week.

Warehouse

Sorting & box lines

Check dates, trim a bruise, build a family box. Indoor, standing work, always at least two people per table. The most flexible role we have — arrive at 7, leave at 11, done.

Mon–Sat · 4-hour shifts

On the road

Delivery & senior boxes

Ride along with a driver on a Wednesday loop, or take a van solo after your third shift. Two people per stop list, 210 households, and a lot of short conversations at doorsteps.

Wednesdays · 8–11 a.m.

Outdoors

Gleaning crews & the garden

Sunrise calls at farms within forty miles: pick, crate, load, and be back at the depot before ten. In the off-season, the same crew weeds, pots, and repairs beds at Orchard Lane.

May–Nov · 6–10 a.m.

Evenings

Community kitchen hosts

Chop, stir, set tables, wash up — and sit down to eat with the neighbours who came. You do not need to cook; you need someone to keep the childcare room running while they do.

Thursdays · 6–9 p.m.

On the bus

Mobile Market crew

Unload, shelve, weigh, and chat on a route that parks in the same four blocks every week. Regulars know their customers by name and by what their children will actually eat.

Wed & Fri · 3-hour stops

Your first shift

What actually happens when you show up.

Nothing mysterious, and nothing you have to prepare for. Here is the whole sequence, from the door to the drive home.

  1. Sign in at the front window

    Give the name you used on this form. You will get a lanyard, a pair of gloves, and an apron if you want one — closed-toe shoes are the only requirement.

  2. Meet your shift lead

    One person walks you through your station in about six minutes. Every station has a seated option and a lifting-free option; say which you prefer and they will not ask twice.

  3. Work, break, work

    Forty-five minutes on, fifteen off. The break room has coffee, someone’s leftovers, and a window that looks at the garden. Most shifts end with the crew eating lunch together.

  4. Take a box home

    Every volunteer leaves with a grocery box, no questions asked. It is not a perk we are grateful for — it is how we keep the people who feed the valley fed.

Training & access

Free, on-site, and twice a month.

New volunteers start with a ninety-minute orientation on the first shift — no booking needed. After that, everything below is optional, paid for by the county workforce grant, and open to neighbours who are not volunteers at all.

  • Access we already solved

    Step-free entry at every station, a quiet room off the break area, Spanish and Vietnamese spoken on most shifts, and ASL interpretation booked with three days’ notice. Say what you need on the form below.

  • Groups, courts, and schools

    We host groups of up to thirty, sign court-ordered community service, and issue service-hour letters for students. Email groups@brightharvest.org at least two weeks ahead.

    Fair questions

    Before you commit a Saturday.

    Do I need to be strong, fast, or experienced?

    No. The heaviest regular lift is a 22-pound crate of tomatoes, and crates are always passed down a line of people rather than carried. Every station has a seated version, and about a third of our volunteers work one exclusively.

    Can I bring my kids?

    Children twelve and over work a shift alongside an adult on the box line and in the garden. Under twelve, they are welcome in the break room with a book, and on the last Saturday of the month we run a dedicated kids’ sorting table with its own aprons.

    What is the minimum commitment?

    One shift. Roughly four in ten volunteers never come back, and we would rather have the food they packed than guilt them into a second Tuesday. Regulars tend to sign up for a standing slot after their third visit, which is entirely optional.

    Will I be around people who use the food bank?

    At the depot, mostly no — volunteers work behind the sorting wall and neighbours shop the shelves on the other side. Kitchen nights, Mobile Market stops, and delivery routes are different, and that is deliberate. If you would rather not, say so on the form and we will keep you warehouse-side.

    Can my company or congregation book a group?

    Yes, up to thirty at a time, Tuesday through Thursday. We ask for two weeks’ notice, a contact person, and no presentations — the crew will be busy. Email groups@brightharvest.org and we will send a fifteen-minute plan.

    Cannot give a Saturday?

    Fifty dollars a month does the same work as four shifts.

    It pays a driver, keeps a refrigerated van on the road, and buys the onions and shelf-stable milk that no partner donates. That is a real option, not a guilt trip — some of our biggest contributors have never set foot in the depot.
