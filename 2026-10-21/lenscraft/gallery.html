Three Series From the Archive

A wedding is not one set of pictures. It is the waiting, the vows, and then everything left standing when the room empties. These three series are how I file a day.

Series I

The Waiting

Two hours before anyone says anything official. This is where the photographs live that nobody commissioned: a mother fastening a button from the wrong side, a best man reading his speech again in a mirror he does not like.

I do not photograph getting-ready as a product shot. The dress matters less than the hands that had to zip it. If a room is dark I let it be dark, and I push a roll of Delta 3200 rather than reach for a light.

How I work

Series II

The Long Table

Receptions are the hardest room to photograph and the easiest to ruin. My rule is one pass per course, no interruptions, and never asking a table to look up.

Glasses, candles, and the particular exhaustion of a good speech are what carry these frames. I shoot the table from the ends rather than the side, so the faces stay in the same order your guests will remember them in.

What coverage includes

Series III

The Leaving

The last ninety minutes are the most photographed part of any wedding and the least observed. Everyone is tired, the ties are off, and the goodbyes are real.

I stay until the final guest has gone, and I photograph whoever is left holding the box of presents. Those frames are the ones couples tell me they come back to after ten years.
