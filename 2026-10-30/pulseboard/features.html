01 Raw event queries

The long tail is where your worst problem lives

Sampling engines resolve a segment by scanning a pre-built slice of your data. That is fast, and it is also why the enterprise churn you can see in the CRM never shows up in the funnel. Pulseboard compiles your question to SQL against the raw event table and lets the warehouse do what warehouses are good at.

A segment of 214 users returns 214 users. A property with 3.1 million distinct values is grouped, not dropped. When a query would scan more than your compute guard allows, the workbench tells you the byte estimate up front and offers a narrower window instead of quietly substituting an approximation.

What you get

  • Unlimited property cardinality, including per-account custom properties
  • Cross-event segmentation: users who did A in the last 7 days and never did B
  • Sessionization at query time, with a configurable inactivity window
  • Computed properties defined in the metrics layer and reused everywhere
  • Full SQL escape hatch with the compiled query shown line by line

The honest cost

ComputeYou pay warehouse credits per query, so we ship per-workspace budgets, a 40-second hard cancel, and a cache that keys on the compiled SQL plus the table version.
FreshnessAnswers are as current as your pipeline. Most teams land events in 1–3 minutes; batch loaders inherit their own cadence and we surface it as a lag badge on every board.
TuningClustering the event table on actor_id and occurred_at typically cuts funnel cost by 40–70%. The onboarding audit prints the exact DDL for your engine.

02 Funnels, retention, paths

Behaviour analysis your analysts can audit

Build a funnel by dragging steps onto the canvas, then open the compiled SQL and read the join it chose. Two people looking at the same chart get the same definition, the same window, and the same exclusion rules — even when one of them is a product manager and the other is a data engineer checking the work.

Retention grids compare cohorts side by side, with a baseline cohort always visible. Paths collapse nested events so a click storm inside one modal reads as a single step instead of forty noise branches.

Analysis primitives

  • Funnels with ordered, unordered, or any-step matching and a configurable window
  • Unbounded funnels for multi-quarter enterprise sales motions
  • Retention by cohort, with rolling, unbounded and bracket modes
  • Path discovery with a collapse rule per event family
  • Distribution and percentile views for latency-style properties
  • Cohort overlap matrix to see what “activated” actually means

Saved as a definition, not a screenshot

Any chart can be promoted to a metric. Promotion writes the definition into the metrics layer, opens a review request, and gives every dependent board an upgrade flag when the definition changes — so a redefinition never silently rewrites last quarter’s narrative.

ExperimentsRead a Flagship, LaunchDarkly or Statsig split against any of the above with a confidence interval computed on the warehouse, not in the browser.
ExportsBoard data exports to CSV or straight back into a warehouse table for downstream joins.

03 Anomaly detection

Find out from a message, not from Monday’s review

Pick the metrics your team actually reacts to, set a cadence, and Pulseboard learns the shape of each one: weekly seasonality, month-end spikes, the November dip that everyone stopped explaining three years ago. When a metric leaves its own band, an alert fires with the query attached.

Alerts decompose automatically. The message above did not stop at “conversion is down”; it segmented by region, plan and device, ranked the contributors, and matched the drop to a provider latency change it could see in your own event stream. You still decide whether it matters — you just start three steps further along.

Watch configuration

  • Seasonality-aware bands with per-metric sensitivity from “quiet” to “hair trigger”
  • Threshold, period-over-period, and modelled baselines side by side
  • Automatic dimension drill-down, capped at the top five contributors
  • Routing by severity to Slack, PagerDuty, email, or a webhook into your own on-call
  • Acknowledgement, snooze, and incident notes written back onto the board
  • Weekly false-positive review that suggests retuning or retiring a watch
Guard railWatches only run on metrics defined in the metrics layer. An alert on an ad-hoc chart is an alert nobody maintains, so the product refuses to create one.
NoiseMedian customer runs 11 watches and receives 3 messages a week. Every message carries a “not useful” button that pauses the watch and opens the tuning form.

04 Metrics layer & governance

One definition of “active”, enforced everywhere

Most analytics estates do not have a data problem; they have a vocabulary problem. Growth says active, finance says billed, support says ticketed, and the weekly review spends twenty minutes reconciling three dashboards that are each correct in isolation.

Pulseboard promotes a question into a named metric with an owner, a version history, and a dependency graph. Funnels, boards, alerts, exports and the read API all resolve through it. Change the definition and every consumer gets a flag plus a diff, so a redefinition is a decision the team makes rather than something that quietly happens.

Governance you can hand to your security reviewer

  • Workspaces, projects and roles mapped to your existing warehouse grants
  • Row-level policies and column masking applied before the query compiles
  • Approval workflow for metric changes, with a signed review trail
  • Per-person audit log exportable to your SIEM
  • SSO with SCIM provisioning, and service accounts with scoped tokens
  • PII detection on property names, with one-click masking templates
Read APIEvery governed metric is queryable through a REST and a GraphQL endpoint, rate-limited per token, so the number in your product is the number in the board.
dbtImport a dbt manifest to inherit models, tests and descriptions. Pulseboard never re-implements a transformation you already own.

07 Questions data engineers ask

The awkward ones, answered

Is this just a query tool for analysts?

No — and this is the design tension we manage hardest. The workbench and SQL editor exist because analysts will not trust a tool they cannot audit. But the product manager surface is the board: drag steps, pick a saved metric, and the compiled query stays hidden behind an “explain this” disclosure. Roughly 71% of Pulseboard views in a healthy workspace are opened by non-analysts.

What happens to cost when a board refreshes every five minutes?

You pay for the scan, so we make refresh an explicit decision. Boards default to warehouse wake-up cadence, with per-board schedules, result caching keyed on table version, and a compute meter on every board footer. Teams on Scale set per-workspace budgets that pause non-critical refreshes instead of failing the quarter’s warehouse bill.

Can we use Pulseboard if our tracking plan is a mess?

Almost always yes. The modelling step is where you say which of your event names mean what, and computed properties let you normalise three spelling variants of plan_type without a pipeline rewrite. The migration audit lists the events you should stop emitting; it does not require a tracking-plan freeze before you get value.

Do you have a native SDK?

We do, for browser and the four mobile platforms, and it writes into your warehouse rather than into us. Most customers keep their existing collector because they already route through Segment, RudderStack or a Kafka topic. Choosing Pulseboard does not require replacing an instrumentation stack that works.

What is the smallest warehouse this is worth pointing at?

Below roughly 5 million events a month you will get more out of a lighter tool, and we say that in demos. Where we earn our keep is where segments are long-lived, cardinality is high, and the warehouse team already has opinions about a vendor copying their data out.
