DDS, MS (Periodontics) · Oregon licence 4417-A

Who puts the implants in

Marisol founded LumiDent in 2011 after eleven years in hospital oral surgery, and still places roughly 180 fixtures a year. She runs the full-arch cases and the ones other practices have declined, and she performs every surgical consultation herself so the person quoting the fee is the person holding the drill.

Her teaching is on flapless guided surgery and socket preservation; she has published on graft volumes and implant survival in smokers, which is why she is willing to say no to a case that will not hold.

Forty minutes, one visit, a microscope

Hana does nothing but root canals and retreatments, which is the reason they can usually be finished in a single appointment. She works at magnification on every case, uses a rubber dam without exception, and keeps the tooth’s own anatomy wherever it can be kept.

Roughly a third of her work is second-opinion retreatment on teeth other clinicians had marked for extraction. She publishes on isthmus cleaning and gives the free lunchtime talk in the atrium each quarter.

DMD, Prosthodontic residency · Oregon licence 4730-P

Ceramic, colour, and the refusal to over-whiten

Theo designs the aesthetic cases and mills them in our own lab two doors from the chair, so a shade that looks wrong under daylight can be corrected the same afternoon rather than in four weeks. He works from a wax-up in every case and will not prepare a tooth until you have worn the mock-up.

He spends more time on bite and function than on appearance, which is why his veneer cases still look correct at year nine and still chew at year one.

Standing Condition of Practice

Forty hours of study, every year, in front of you.

The Oregon requirement is twenty-five continuing education hours a year. Every clinician here logs forty, and the topics are published on the noticeboard in the atrium so you can see what your dentist learned last month.

  • Monthly clinical journal club — one paper, critiqued by the whole team, open to staff questions.
  • Quarterly case rounds where our own failures are presented without the names attached.
  • Annual infection-control audit by an external assessor; the report is available to any patient.
