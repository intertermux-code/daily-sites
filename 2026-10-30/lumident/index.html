The sixty-minute clean

Our hygiene visits run a full hour with one clinician — never a two-person relay. You get a scaled and polished mouth, a fluoride varnish, an oral cancer screen, and photographs of anything we want you to look at.

If you bleed on probing, we treat the cause rather than re-booking you in six months and hoping.

Planned on a scan, not a guess

The LumiDent Method

Four entries between the waiting room and the recall card.

The sequence never changes, whether you are here for a polish or a full-arch reconstruction. You will always know which line of the process you are standing on.

  1. Entry 01

    Look properly

    A sixty-minute examination with photographs, a periodontal chart to the millimetre, and a 3D scan if bone is in question. Nothing is decided at this stage.

  2. Entry 02

    Write it down

    You leave with a printed plan in plain English: options ranked by urgency, the fee for each line, and the consequences of doing nothing for two years.

  3. Entry 03

    Treat unhurried

    We book more chair time than the procedure needs, so the appointment does not run over. Anaesthetic is delivered slowly; breaks are always offered.

  4. Entry 04

    Keep it for life

    Every restoration is recorded with its batch, its date, and its guarantee. If it fails inside that window, the correction is not charged to you.

Standards you can audit

The boring parts are the reason the rest works.

Infection control and equipment are the least glamorous commitments a practice makes, and the ones patients rarely get to inspect. So we publish ours.

  • Class B vacuum autoclave with a printed cycle log for every cassette — ask to see today's.
  • Low-dose CBCT: a limited-field scan delivers roughly one third of the radiation of a full jaw study.
  • Nitrous and oral sedation administered by a board-certified dental anaesthesiologist, never a chairside assistant.
  • Every crown, aligner and implant fixture carries a lot number recorded against your file for twenty-five years.
