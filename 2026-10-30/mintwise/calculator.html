04 Reading the ring

Three numbers, and what each one is really for

Most budgeting advice collapses into a single percentage. Here is how to interpret the three that matter, in order.

01

The savings rate is your freedom dial

It is the share of take-home pay that survives the month. Under 5% you are one car repair from credit. 20% is the planner’s benchmark. Above 30% and the number starts doing compounding work for you.

Mintwise recalculates this nightly and never rounds it in your favour.

02

Runway is the number that changes behaviour

Cash on hand divided by monthly spend. Six months means a job loss is an inconvenience rather than an emergency loan. Most households arrive at two to three months and are shocked, which is exactly why we surface it first.

Target: 3 months single, 6 months household, 9 months self-employed.

03

The benchmark gap tells you where to cut

Needs over 50% of income is a housing or transport problem, and those are structural, not discipline problems. Wants over 30% is the only place a slider helps. Compare the bars, then pick one line.

In-app, price-creep alerts find this line for you automatically.

06 Honest limits

What this calculator does not know

  • Irregular income. Enter an average across three months. The linked app re-baselines on every payslip instead.
  • Sinking funds. Car registration, holidays, and property tax do not live in these seven lines. Add their monthly equivalent to the closest category.
  • Debt payoff strategy. Minimums sit in housing, transport, and bills here; Mintwise models avalanche versus snowball separately.
  • Investment returns. The twelve-month figure assumes no market growth at all, so treat it as a floor.
