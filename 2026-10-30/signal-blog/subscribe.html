    Questions Readers Actually Send

    Ordered by how often they arrive. Open one for the answer.

    01Why is the email free if the print edition is not?

    Because the reporting has to be reachable by the people who might know something about it. Half of our best tips arrive from readers who never paid us anything, and an archive behind a wall is an archive nobody can help us correct.

    Print is a different product: paper, ink, a machine that runs at two in the morning in Porto, and postage to nineteen countries. It costs us roughly what you pay for it, which is the honest answer to almost every pricing question you could ask.

    02How many emails will I get, and can I get fewer?

    One on the Friday the desk ships, plus a confirmation when you sign up. If you tick the dataset alert you will hear perhaps ten times a year, always labelled in the subject line as such. There is no welcome sequence, no drip, and no re-engagement campaign — if you stop opening, we keep mailing, because that is the only way the promise to be non-surveillant means anything.

    03Do you accept advertising or sponsorship?

    No, and the reason is on the masthead page rather than buried. We turn down roughly thirty offers a year, most of them for the print edition's back cover. In 2019 a sponsor withdrew rather than let a piece run; we published the letter and stopped taking sponsorship altogether.

    04Can my company or library subscribe for a group?

    Yes. Institutional licences are a flat annual rate with no seat counting and no editorial conditions, and twenty-two university libraries plus four engineering institutions currently hold one. Write to post@signal.press and we will send the invoice before anything else.

    05What happens to my address if I leave?

    It is deleted within seven days, including from the print mailing file, and the deletion is not contingent on a survey. If we have already addressed a bundle of paper for you that week, we will post you the envelope rather than the list entry.

    06I think a dispatch is wrong. What is the fastest route?

    Email the dispatch number and the specific line to corrections@signal.press. Corrections are the shortest thing we read: if you are right, the note goes up that day online and prints in the next issue's masthead notes, and you are credited if you want to be. Fourteen corrections since 2016, and every one of them made the piece better.

    07Will you ever move the archive behind a paywall?

    Not while the subscriptions cover the costs, which at the time of writing they do with a small reserve. Our stated policy is that a dispatch becomes free in perpetuity on publication, and the reserve exists so that a bad quarter cannot quietly reverse it. You can hold us to that because it is written here, on a page that is part of the product.
