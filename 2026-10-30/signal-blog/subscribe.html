What Actually Lands In The Email

02 — Anatomy

The lead story

1,000–1,400 words

Never a summary of a post on this site. The Dispatch lead is drafted the day before it goes out, by the reporter who spent the week on it, and it usually contains at least one thing we found and had not published yet.

Reporting notes

Four or five bullets

Where a story is going, what we could not confirm, which door got shut in our face. This is the section readers tell us they open first, and the reason our reply rate is 3.4%.

The document

One attachment or link

A specification, an invoice, a post-mortem, a licence text. Every Thursday we point at one primary source and explain what to read in it. Nine of these have triggered a formal response from the organisation that produced them.

Patch Notes

Mondays, if you opted in

Releases and retirement dates that matter, licence deltas, and the version bumps our reproducibility lab actually tested on a clean machine. No release notes we could not verify.

Questions We Actually Get

05 — FAQ

Do you sell my email address?

No, and we do not process it anywhere that could. The list lives on a server we administer, in a rack we rent, in the Netherlands. There is no ad network, no data broker, and no third-party analytics script on this site or in our emails.

Can I read it in a browser instead?

Every issue is published on the site within an hour of sending, and the archive back to 2019 stays open permanently. The email is simply the version that reaches you without you having to remember we exist.

Why Thursday and not when it happens?

Because breaking news in this sector is mostly vendors telling you what they already decided. We publish the moment we can verify something outside the newsletter too — security alerts go immediately if you opted in.

I am on a corporate mailhost and your email bounced.

This happens more than we like. Ask your mail administrator to allow dispatch@signal.press, or use the digest-forwarding address on the confirmation page. We will never ask you to move to a personal inbox to read our work.
