About · The Publication

We Report on the Plumbing

Signal exists for one reason: the decisions behind the software everyone depends on are made in rooms nobody covers. We cover them — slowly, on paper, with named reporters.

Why This Exists

01 — Mission

Written by the masthead, reviewed annually, and binding on all of us.

Technology coverage has a structural problem. The products are reviewed by people whose access depends on the vendors, the incidents are reported by people whose employers sell remediation, and the genuinely consequential decisions — a licence change, a depreciation notice, a data-center lease in Virginia — happen in a format no publication considers newsworthy at the time.

We started Signal in 2019 with four reporters and a shared spreadsheet, on the theory that this gap could be filled by charging readers instead of advertisers. Seven years on, the theory holds. Our budget is paid by 91,400 newsletter subscribers and 6,800 contributing members; no vendor, agency, or investor has ever had a line in it, which means no one can withdraw one.

That independence has a cost we are transparent about: we publish less. A piece from us takes between six weeks and six months. We have killed four stories in the last two years because we could not verify them to the standard we set, and we say so in the issue where the next one appears.

We write for the practitioner. Not for the buyer committee, not for the analyst firm, and not for the founder of the company we are about to disappoint. If you are the person who will actually be paged at 3 a.m., this publication was built for you.

How We Work

02 — Method

  1. Two Named Sources Minimum

    Every factual claim about a company's internal state requires two people with direct knowledge, plus a document where one exists. We do not run “according to people familiar with the matter” without telling you how many and how they know.

  2. We Publish Our Data

    Benchmarks, cost models, and the spreadsheet behind any chart go out under a permissive licence alongside the piece. If we cannot share it, we say why in the text rather than publishing an unreadable number.

  3. No Embargoes We Cannot Explain

    We accept review copies only when the terms are public. A vendor cannot buy silence with access, and access we pay for with travel and time is still access we can lose without changing our budget.

  4. Corrections Are Front Page

    Errors are fixed at the top of the piece with the original wording preserved, and summarised in the following issue. We have issued 23 corrections since 2019 and we count them as output.

  5. Reporters Own Their Beat End to End

    The person who reports a story writes it, chooses the headline, and answers the reader email afterwards. No desk-layered rewriting, because the knowledge that makes a piece accurate is the same knowledge that makes it readable.

A Short History

04 — 2019 to now

  • 2019

    Four reporters, one spreadsheet

    Signal launches as a fortnightly email from a rented desk on Keizersgracht. 640 subscribers by the first Christmas, all of them personal referrals.

  • 2021

    The first investigation that changed something

    Our nine-week report on undocumented drive-firmware errata led two hosting providers to publish their own. We hired Mara Ellison with the membership surge that followed.

  • 2022

    We turned down an acquisition

    A systems-integration group offered to buy us at a multiple we could not refuse on paper and did anyway. The cooperative structure dates from the vote that followed.

  • 2024

    Open data becomes policy

    Every model, benchmark, and dataset behind our reporting now publishes under CC BY 4.0 on the day the piece goes out. Three competitors have since copied the practice.

  • 2026

    Eleven people, 91,400 readers

    Nairobi and San Francisco desks open; the print edition moves to monthly. Budget is 100% reader-funded for the seventh consecutive year.

Editorial Ethics & Money

05 — Disclosure

Every member of the masthead discloses holdings, side work, and any relationship with a company we cover, in a register published with the January issue. Reporters do not accept travel, meals, hardware, or conference passes from a vendor they have covered in the last two years; we pay our own way and we say so in the piece when it matters.

We do not sell sponsored placements, affiliate links, “partner content,” or reader lists. Our revenue is: contributing memberships at €9 per month, the print edition, and an annual reader fund that covers capital purchases like the reproducibility lab's rack. Full annual accounts — every line item, every salary band — are published each February and any reader may request the underlying ledger.

Anonymous sources are approved by two people, never one, and we describe their position rather than vouching for them in the abstract. If we get something wrong, we correct it at the top, keep the original wording, and list it in the next issue.
