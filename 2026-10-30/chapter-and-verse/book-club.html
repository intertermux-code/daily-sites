    The Four Tables

    Who sits where, and who hosts

    Tuesdays · 7:00 pm

    The Long Novel Club

    Eighteen readers, four doorstoppers a season, host: Nadia Ellery. Last winter it was Middlemarch and then One Hundred Years of Solitude, and two people did not speak to each other for a fortnight over the Buendías tree.

    Wednesdays · 7:00 pm

    Poetry & Argument

    Twelve readers, twenty poems, no notes permitted — host: Theodore Marsh. The rule exists because the best readings in this room have always come from somebody who was slightly unprepared.

    Thursdays · 8:30 pm

    Gothic Late

    Fourteen readers, lights off, one paragraph each standing up. Host: Owen Pike. Runs October through March and finishes with the All Hallows’ read, which is why the calendar sells out first.

    Sundays · 4:00 pm

    Speculative Sundays

    Sixteen readers and the rule that you must bring a book you cannot defend. Host: Priya Raman. Le Guin, Butler and Calvino are permanently on the shelf by the door for this table.

    What Happens Next

    Someone reads the sign-ups on Sunday morning. You get an email by Tuesday telling you which table you are at, which title you are starting, and whether that table needs you to bring the biscuits.

    Questions at the Till

    The four we get most, answered in order

    Do I have to have read the book?

    No, but say so out loud when you arrive. Half the table will have read it twice and the other half will be discovering it with you, and the second group is usually more interesting.

    What if I can only make one night in three?

    Join anyway. Attendance is not a membership test; it is weather and children and shifts. We keep the notes on the counter for a month so you can argue with the last table instead of at it.

    Can I bring somebody without signing them up?

    Once. After that they need a name on the sheet, mostly because we run out of chairs and it is rude to find out on the night. There is no audition and nobody votes on them.

    Who picks the books, honestly?

    The host, from the permanent shelf, then the circle vetoes by writing “no” on the programme in ink. Two titles have been killed that way in eleven seasons and both hosts complained for exactly as long as it took to pick a better one.

    Not Ready for a Table?

    The Thursday reading is free, needs no sign-up, and is where most members actually started. Come twice, then decide.
