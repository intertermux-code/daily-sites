Nights Out, Books Open

Twelve ways to be in a room with other readers.

Section One

The Season Calendar

Free unless a price is listed. Ticketed nights go to the counter or by phone; we hold four unsold seats at every paid event for walk-ins who turn up late and hungry.

Section Two

Every Week, Same Hour

Four fixtures that do not move. Show up any time; nobody will make you perform, and the chairs are already set.

Tuesday · 6:00 pm

The Long Argument. One nonfiction title, two staff, twenty minutes each, then the room. Never the book of the month — that would be cheating.

Thursday · 7:00 pm

Poetry at Half Eight. Open mic, two minutes per reader, first-timers get the first three slots. Hosted by Nadia.

Saturday · 10:00 am

Stacking & Spines. Volunteer shelving with coffee and the good biscuit tin. No experience, no commitment; wear something you can kneel in.

Sunday · 11:00 am

Story Hour in the Loft. Picture books, paper crafts, ruined carpet. Ages three to seven and their grown-ups.

Section Three

Where It Happens

Four rooms, all on one floor except the rarity room, which is down eight steps and worth the climb.

The Gold Room

Forty-two chairs, a low stage of stacked crates, and the only room with the original 1911 plaster. Hearing loop at the desk; step-free from Warren Street. Hosts most talks and every poetry night.

The Rarity Room

Down the stair, climate-held at 18 degrees, lit by lamps we replace twice a year to protect the cloth. Thirty seats maximum, and the reason we ask you to phone before you come.

The Back Stacks

No heating, no electricity during candle nights, and shelving so tall you can hear the books settle. Our late reading room, and the coldest wonderful place in Hudson.

The Children’s Loft

Up the passage: floor cushions, a ruined carpet, two thousand picture books, and a window seat that has caused more arguments than any critical edition upstairs.

Hire a room, or bring ten friends

The Gold Room is free for community groups on Monday evenings and hireable at cost for the rest of the week — forty-two chairs, a hearing loop, and a kettle that has been running since 1998. Last month it held a poetry manuscript audit, a wedding reading, and one very serious board-game night.

Book through the events desk and we will shelve a display for you: tell us the subject and Ellis will pull twenty books you can leaf through while people arrive.

Before you come

Every event is free unless a price is printed, and every price can be waived — just ask, quietly, at the counter.

We keep four seats at each ticketed night for walk-ins. The shop is step-free at the front door; the rarity room is reached by eight steps and we will bring any book up to you. Ear defenders and a printed large print schedule live in the front room, on the shelf beside the stamps.
