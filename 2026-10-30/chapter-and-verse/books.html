How the Shelf Gets Made

Four rules, pinned above the ordering desk since 2013

We do not order by what is moving. We order by what a customer would thank us for in five years, which is a slower and much more annoying metric. Every title here had to survive two of us arguing about it out loud, in front of the till, usually while somebody’s order was being wrapped.

Rule one: a book must be readable in the edition we sell, which kills about a third of the proposals. Rule two: at least one person in the shop must have finished it, standing up, holding it, and able to say why. Rule three: no title on a shelf just because a publisher sent us a nice card.

Rule four is the one customers notice. When we drop a title, it does not vanish — it goes to the condemnations box by aisle two, where five dollars buys it out and the money pays for the reading series. Our shelf is therefore a record of decisions, not of taste.
