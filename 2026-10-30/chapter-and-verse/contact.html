Section Two

Getting Here

The block was a printing works, so the doorway is wide, the floor is level, and the freight lift still takes a pushchair.

On foot. We are the third building south of Warren and Columbia, green fascia, brass ampersand. The front step has a four-inch lip; there is a ramp behind the till that we will bring out if you ask at the door.

By train. Hudson station is eleven minutes away on foot: leave the platform, turn left onto Warren, walk south past the bakery. Two Metro-North lines reach us in under two hours from Grand Central, and we stay open until ten on Fridays for exactly that reason.

By car. Free parking behind the building through the archway — take the second gap, not the first, or you will be upside a delivery pallet. Two accessible bays at the rear gate; mention it when you call and we will leave the loading door unlocked.

By bus. Route 21 stops at Columbia Street. It runs every forty minutes until seven, and the driver knows the bell.

Section Three

Practical Things People Ask

Can you find a book you do not stock?

Yes. We order from the trade daily and from two specialist wholesellers for translated and small-press titles. Ask at the counter or use the form above and pick “Find me a book” — most requests land within five working days, and we will tell you the same day if we cannot get it.

Do you buy collections?

Often, and honestly. Theo pays on the spot for anything he can sell, declines what he cannot, and will tell you which of the two it is before you carry the boxes in. Send photographs first if the collection is more than two shelves.

Can I reserve a title without paying?

Yes — give us a name and it waits behind the counter for seven days. Nothing is carded until you collect it, and if you do not turn up we shelve it again without a phone call.

Do you ship?

Anywhere in the United States, packed in the old paper we prefer rather than plastic, at cost plus four dollars. Rare items ship insured and signed for. We do not ship internationally yet; ask us and we will find a friend who does it properly.

Is the shop loud?

Thursday nights and Saturday afternoons are busy. The Back Stacks are quiet all day, Sunday mornings are the stillest hour we have, and ear defenders live on the shelf beside the stamps — take a pair, no need to ask.

Where do I park a bicycle?

Racks in the archway, three of them, and the fourth one we use for staff. It is covered, which matters more in Hudson than the space.

Our side of it

We answer everything within two working days — including the letters asking us to order something hopeless.

Mondays are shelving day and the phone goes to the answer machine, which is the only voicemail in the building anybody listens to. If you write on a Monday, count from Tuesday.
