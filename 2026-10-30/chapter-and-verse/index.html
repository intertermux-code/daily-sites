From the Counter

A letter from the shop, written every month by whoever opens up

October arrived in this shop the way it always does: slowly at first, then all at once. The Gothic shelf emptied by the second week, three customers asked us whether we “still had that Wilfred Owen section by the window,” and Verity claimed the radiator cover as her own for the fourth consecutive year. We moved the Owen to the window again, because arguing with regulars is bad for business and worse for the blood pressure.

This month we are thinking about what a shelf is actually for. Not as merchandising — as memory. A shelf in an independent shop is a record of nine arguments. Somebody insisted on the García Márquez, somebody else resisted the new translation, and the position the book ends up in is the truce. When you walk past our fiction bay and something catches your eye, that is not design. That is a settled dispute, at least until November.

We are also short on nothing but time. Two of us are training in November, which means the shelf will get stranger and better by turns. Bring your grudges to Thursday’s reading and we will find you something worth the walk.

Nadia Ellery · Head of Poetry & the Late Shift
