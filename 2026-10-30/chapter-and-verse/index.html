Why we stock the strange books

We shelve what we would read at two in the morning — and we shelve it where you can find it.

The rarity room downstairs holds first editions under glass: a 1919 Lady Ann Joyce, a mid-century Woolf with the dust wrapper the shop lost and later recovered. You may handle any of them with clean hands and a staff member at your elbow. Nothing here is behind a counter.

Section Four

Four Rooms, One Ledger

The front room is new stock and the counter where Ellis pretends to keep the hours. Behind it, the long room holds fiction in a single unbroken wall — American, then translated, then everything we could not classify. Down the stair, the rarity room. Up the passage, the children’s loft, where the carpet is ruined and nobody minds.

We bought the building in 1998 from a printing firm that left behind four crates of proofs. Two are still unsold upstairs and we refuse to price them. That is roughly the whole business philosophy.
