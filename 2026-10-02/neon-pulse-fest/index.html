§01 — The Transmission

A Signal From 2099

For seventy-two hours this December, a disused orbital relay on the Mojave fringe becomes the planet's loudest listening post. Neon Pulse is not a festival. It is a broadcast.

Built by sound engineers from Tokyo's Aoyama underground and Berlin's Tresor collective, each edition of Pulse expands the perimeter. In 2024 we hosted eleven thousand. In 2025, eighteen. In 2026, we are expecting twenty-four thousand listeners from thirty-seven countries, arriving on foot, by rail, and — in a few celebrated cases — by low-orbit shuttle.

The grounds span 420 acres of scrub, steel, and subwoofer. The main stage, The Dish, is a repurposed 280-foot satellite antenna. Two smaller stages, Orbit and Nexus, occupy the old coolant towers. Forty-two artists. Three nights. One frequency.

§03 — The Relay

A Satellite Dish. A Desert. A Frequency.

Sited on the decommissioned Atlas-7 orbital relay station in the Mojave, the grounds span 420 acres of scrub, steel, and subwoofer. The main stage — The Dish — is a repurposed satellite antenna 280 feet in diameter. Two smaller stages, Orbit and Nexus, occupy the old coolant towers.

The sound system — a custom 180,000-watt Funktion-One — is tuned for low-frequency warmth and crystalline highs. Water towers are refilled hourly. A medical tent staffed by EMTs operates 24/7. The campgrounds, a 15-minute walk from the gates, have hot showers and free coffee between 05:00 and 09:00.
