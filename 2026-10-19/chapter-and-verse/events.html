31 nights this season The Reading Room · thirty chairs · one wood stove

Events at the Shop

Readings, repair days, storytime and one very late Halloween. Everything below happens in the back room unless the entry says otherwise, and every seat is free unless the ticket says otherwise.

§ 01 Dated entries, in order of happening p. 31

October to December 2026

Seats remaining are counted at the door each morning, so if a night shows one place left, telephone us before you travel.

House rule

Latecomers are seated at the back during the first pause, not the middle of a reading. We will hold your book until the interval.

Doors open twenty minutes early. The kettle is on.

October 2026

  • Thursday 22 October 7:00 pm — 8:45 pm

    Read Me Five Minutes

    Open mic · hosted by Tomás Villareal

    Anyone may read: a paragraph of your own, a page you love, or the back of a cereal box. Five minutes, one bell, no applause required. Sign the sheet at the counter before seven.

    Children’s Hour: Picture Book Party

    Ages 3—7 · led by Sam Oyelaran

    Three picture books, one felt board, and a craft table that gets messier every month. Caregivers stay; there are chairs and good coffee. Strollers fold behind the poetry bay.

    Gothic reading for Halloween · hosted by Nadia Okonkwo

    The shop after dark, four readers, and an hour of Brontë, Shelley, Stoker and Wilde. We sell the books we have just read from a trolley by the door, and the lights stay off in the south aisle afterwards on purpose.

§ 03 Filed under “already happened” p. 40

What the room looked like in September

  • Saturday 12 September

    The Second-Hand Fair

    1,400 books through the door · 96 stalls

    The floor went up on trestles at seven and came down at five. We took in 2,180 dollars for the shop and gave the whole morning takings to the school library at Enola Street, as we do every year.

    Filed
  • Thursday 3 September

    Ninety Minutes with Du Bois

    Read-through of The Souls of Black Folk

    Eleven readers, one chapter each, no interval. The last essay was read by a fourteen-year-old from the eighth-grade group who then refused the microphone back.

    Filed
  • Sunday 6 September

    Open the Shelves

    Volunteer day · 22 helpers · 4,000 books re-shelved

    Our returns trolley has a legend attached to it. Not any more. Thank you to everybody who turned up with a screwdriver and stayed for soup.

    Filed

§ 04 The room is yours on Mondays p. 42

Host something in the Reading Room

Thirty chairs, a lamp per seat, a kettle, and a shelf of books we will pull for your theme. We take no hire fee from writing groups, school clubs or neighbourhood associations — we ask for the door collection and that you leave before the shelves need opening.
