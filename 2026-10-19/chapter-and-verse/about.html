Fifteen years 27 Winsley Street · a former cobbler’s shop

The Shop on Winsley Street

Two people, one sprung floor, and a stubborn belief that a bookshop is a public room before it is a business. This is the whole account, including the part with the fire.

§ 01 1998 to now, told out of order p. 58

It started as a lending problem

Margin note

The original lease is in a frame behind the counter. Six hundred and forty square feet, no kitchen, and a clause about hammering we have never tested.

Miriam Ainsley was a high-school English teacher with a shelf that would not fit in her flat. Joel Ferreira had spent nine years running a second-hand stall and could tell you the print run of anything published between 1945 and 1975. They met in 2009 at the county library, arguing quietly about a translation, and by the spring of 2011 they had signed for a former cobbler’s shop with a sprung floor and a north window.

The idea was never clever. Buy well, shelve honestly, and leave the lamp on. The first year sold four hundred and twelve fiction titles and lost money on all of them. The second year sold eight hundred and nine and stopped losing money, mostly on the strength of a display of travel writing that nobody had thought to try.

In February 2018 a fire in the unit next door put eleven thousand dollars of water through our south wall. Four thousand books were ruined; the ceiling came down in the Reading Room. Two hundred and six people turned up on the first Saturday of the rebuild to sort, dry and shelve what would survive. We have a list of their names by the door, and we read it out every February.

Fifteen years on the shop employs five people, runs nine book clubs, and still does not sell coffee. The cobbler’s bench is at the back of the Reading Room with a lamp on it, and on Wednesdays somebody sits at it and mends a spine instead of selling one.

§ 02 Dated entries p. 60

Fifteen years, abridged

  • 2011

    Six hundred and forty square feet

    The cobbler’s unit at 27 Winsley Street opens on 2 April with 2,100 titles, two of whom were the owners, and a bell on the door that Joel still refuses to replace.

  • 2013

    Seven people round a table

    The Tuesday Night Book Club starts as a way of shifting stock. It has met every first Tuesday since, missing only the fortnight after the fire.

  • 2016

    The Reading Room

    We take the unit behind and knock through, gaining thirty chairs, a wood stove, and one load-bearing problem that took eight months to certify.

  • 2018

    The February fire

    Four thousand books lost, the ceiling down, the shop shut for eleven weeks. Two hundred and six neighbours turned up to help. We reopened with the south wall shelved in the order people handed books back.

  • 2021

    The First Editions Fair

    Ninety-six stalls on the floor in September, run by volunteers, with the morning takings to a school library. It became the fixture that bookers now plan around.

  • 2023

    A shelf for local writers

    Consignment terms, no fee, and a decision made by the staff meeting rather than a distributor. Sixty-one Western North Carolina titles at last count, and four of them are now stocked by libraries.

  • 2026

    Fourteen thousand two hundred titles

    Five staff, nine clubs, thirty-one event nights booked this season, and the same bell on the door. The bench is still at the back.

§ 03 Who to ask, and about what p. 63

Behind the counter

Five of us, each responsible for the shelf we would defend in a bar fight. Ask for anybody by name; we do not make you explain it twice.

MA

Miriam Ainsley

Co-owner · Fiction

Taught English for eleven years before this. Reads two novels a week and the reviews for pleasure. Will talk you out of the book you came in for, usually correctly.

JF

Joel Ferreira

Co-owner · Buying & accounts

Ran a market stall for nine years and still arrives at half past six. Decides what we stock, argues with the distributors, and keeps the six-hundred-page spreadsheet that lets us stay open.

TV

Tomás Villareal

Rare & used · Wednesdays by appointment

Trained at a bindery in Oporto. Catalogues the cabinet, mends on the cobbler’s bench, and can date a reprint by the endpapers. Runs the open mic because nobody else would.

BL

Bea Lindqvist

Events · Book clubs

Keeps the chair list, the waitlists and the kettle. Has run the Tuesday Night club since the fortnight after the fire, and knows which twelve people will argue about Dorothea.

SO

Sam Oyelaran

Children’s · Schools & libraries

Illustrates the shop’s own event posters, does Children’s Hour every Saturday, and supplies the county’s teachers with book boxes on the last Wednesday of the month.

On the other hand

We have turned down three franchise offers, one investment round, and a very generous proposal involving a wine bar. The shop is 1,900 square feet on purpose.

§ 05 Five rules we can be held to p. 68

What we promise, specifically

i

We sell the book you want

If we do not have it we order it, and the order costs you nothing extra. We do not push a title because a distributor offered us a better margin.

ii

Condition is graded out loud

Used copies say what is wrong with them. A creased spine is not “charming”, and a name in the front board is worth telling you about before you pay.

iii

The room is free

Thirty chairs, a lamp each, no purchase required. Sit all day on a Sunday. We have never asked anybody to leave and we are not starting.

iv

Banned books stay on the shelf

We keep a standing display of the titles challenged in this county, and we write the reason somebody gave on the card beside them.

v

Local writers get a shelf, not a fee

Consignment is free and the decision is made by the staff meeting. Sixty-one titles are on the wall now; four have gone on to library stock.

§ 06 The rest of it you have to see p. 69

Come and judge the shelves yourself

Nine-thirty in the morning is the quiet hour. Bring a list, or bring nobody’s list, and ask for the south wall.
