We close for ten minutes at one o’clock on Wednesdays while the Reading Room is turned over between the poetry class and the repair table. The bell on the door has not worked since 2014, so come in and look for somebody; five of us work in shifts and at least two are always on the floor.

§ 02 Four ways to arrive p. 71

Getting Here

On foot

27 Winsley Street
Asheville, North Carolina 28801
One block off the plaza, green awning.

By car

the Rankin Garage is ninety seconds away on Hill Street and charges three dollars after five. On-street bays on Winsley are free for twenty minutes — long enough for a hold, not for the whole shelf.

By bus

Routes d10 and GR stop at Winsley and College Street, two hundred feet from the door. The GR runs every twelve minutes until eight on weekdays.

By bike

Six racks under the awning and a shaded row along the south wall. The Greenway path reaches us in eight minutes from the wall, and the pavement on Winsley is flat the whole way.

§ 03 What to expect at the door p. 72

Access, Noise and Quiet

Margin note

If something here is not obvious from the pavement, telephone the shop and ask. We would rather talk you through a step than have you turn around.

(828) 555-0142 — a person answers, not a menu.

i.

Step-free from the curb

The ramp on the left of the door takes a wheelchair, a stroller and a trolley of paperbacks. The Reading Room is on the same level as the shop floor; the cellar stack is reached only by a spiral stair, so ask us to fetch anything down there.

ii.

Quiet hour, every Tuesday

From 9:30 to 10:30 there is no music, no espresso machine, no events briefing, and the aisle lamp is dimmed to its lowest setting. Two booksellers are on the floor and the shop holds about twelve people comfortably.

iii.

Sight, sound and reading

Large-print and braille-labelled titles are shelved together at eye level near the front window, not cordoned off at the back. An induction loop runs the length of the Reading Room lectern, and we book a sign-language interpreter for every launch at fourteen days’ notice.

iv.

Dogs, seating and the toilet

Assistance dogs always, other well-behaved dogs on a Wednesday. There are nine chairs and two benches, none of them hidden. The accessible toilet is past the poetry shelf, and the key is on the hook by its door rather than behind a counter.

§ 06 Asked at the desk p. 77

Questions We Answer Standing

Can you order a book you do not have?

Yes, and it costs you nothing extra. We order from three distributors on Monday and Thursday; most in-print titles reach the counter in four working days, and imported editions in about two weeks. Ask at the desk or send the title through the form above and we will hold a copy the moment it is stamped.

Do you buy books, and what will you not take?

We buy collections outright twice a month and take single volumes at the counter on Wednesdays. Bring photographs first if the book has a plate, a signature or any date before 1900. We do not take Reader’s Digest abridged editions, water-damaged volumes, encyclopaedias, or anything with a library stamp we cannot legally clear.

Is there anywhere to sit without buying?

Nine chairs, two benches and the long table in the Reading Room. You may read for as long as you like and you do not have to buy the book. This is the whole reason the room exists. The only rule is that the table is cleared at eighteen on event nights.

Can I bring my dog?

Assistance dogs always, in every part of the shop. Other dogs come on Wednesdays, on a short lead, and they are not allowed on the chairs. There is a hook for a lead beside the door and a water bowl under the counter.

Do you host private events?

The Reading Room seats thirty-four and we close the floor for book launches, wakes, wedding readings and one very determined birthday quiz. It is free for a launch of a book we stock; otherwise the room hire is a hundred and twenty dollars an hour, and half of it goes to the repair table.

What happens to a hold if I do not come?

A held book waits seven days on the shelf behind the counter, then goes back to the floor and we email you once, unkindly, to say it has gone. If it was a second-hand copy there may not be another; if it was a new one, we can order it again in four days.

Or skip the form and come in

The kettle is off but the lamp is on, and there are fourteen thousand books with nobody to sell them. Start with the shelf, or see who is reading this month.
