Nine Clubs, One Long Table

Every club meets in the Reading Room with a lamp each and no president. Pick a table below, sign the list, and come once. If it is the wrong group, we will move you without a single awkward conversation.

§ 01 Four things, in order p. 44

What joining actually means

Margin note

Nobody keeps a register of who read what. The only rule we enforce is that you say something, even if what you say is “I stopped at page sixty.”

  1. Choose a table

    Nine clubs, roughly one a fortnight between them. Two meet online in winter, seven in the shop. The season’s list is further down this page.

  2. Get the book here

    Members pay ten percent off the club title and we hold copies behind the counter from the week the pick is announced. Six copies are kept on the loan shelf for anyone who cannot buy one.

  3. Turn up

    Doors at half past six, reading aloud for ten minutes, then talking until eight. Bring the book, bring notes, or bring nothing — the first ten minutes are always the same question.

  4. Stay or move

    Two nights and it is not right? Tell Bea and we will place you somewhere else. A third of our members have changed clubs at least once, and nobody remembers it as a failure.

We only use it for club dates. Never sold, never shared.

Before you ask

Do I have to have read the book?

No. Roughly a quarter of any given night has not finished it, and the conversation is better for them. We do ask that you have started.

Can I bring a guest?

Bring them. Add a line in the box above and we will set out two chairs. Guests are always free; only members pay anything.

What if I miss a night?

Nothing happens, ever. The club carries on and the pages are written on the whiteboard so you know where the room got to.

Prefer to sign the list in person? Bring a friend to the last Wednesday staff meeting and put it in Bea’s handwriting yourself.
