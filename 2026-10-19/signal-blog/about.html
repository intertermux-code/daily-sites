Ten People, One Ledger, No Advertisers

Signal exists because the technology press kept writing about a physical industry as though it were abstract, and because the people who actually build it kept sending us the documents. We are a cooperative. Nine reporters, one fact-checker, and a funding model we can show you line by line.

What We Are For

Page D1

The first issue of Signal was four pages long, set in a single column because none of us knew how to set two, and mailed to sixty-one people who had asked to be mailed something. That was March 2017. The reason it existed was narrow and, we still think, correct: the stories that mattered most about computing infrastructure were happening in buildings, and almost nobody covering technology was allowed to go inside the buildings.

Nine years later the beat has become physical in every direction at once. Power procurement decides where models get trained. Water permits decide where chips get made. A cross-connect in a carrier hotel eleven miles from a trading floor decides latency, and latency decides who wins an auction. Meanwhile the trade press consolidated, the investigative desks shrank, and the vocabulary stayed abstract on purpose, because abstraction is cheaper than reporting.

We are not a newsletter with a website and we are not a consultancy with a blog. We are a publication with a masthead, a corrections policy, and an editor who has vetoed her own favourite story twice because the sourcing was not there yet. If that sounds like a low bar, it is — and it is the bar most of the industry has stopped clearing.

“Abstraction is cheaper than reporting. That is the whole reason we are still here.”

Mara Ellison, Editor-in-Chief

Our remitThree Sentences, Held To

  1. 01We report the physical layer. Data centers, fibre, silicon, power, cooling, and the labour that keeps them running are technology news, not background.
  2. 02We name our sources or we do not run the claim. Anonymous sourcing is permitted, documented, and approved by the editor — never by the reporter who wants the deadline.
  3. 03We take no money that buys influence. No advertising, no sponsored copy, no affiliate links, no conference sponsorship from a company we cover.

Corrections & Standards

Page D5

We correct in public and we never delete. An amended sentence stays amended, with the original in the log beneath it, because the record of getting it wrong is the only part of the record that is genuinely useful to you.

Four rules govern what we publish, and they are not negotiable by the subject of a story, by a funder, or by the reporter who filed it.

  1. 01Documents before assertions. If a claim rests on a contract, an invoice, a filing, or a log, we ask to see it and we say in the piece whether we did.
  2. 02Right of reply, in writing, with a deadline. Every subject gets the specific claims and at least five working days. Refusals to respond are reported as refusals to respond.
  3. 03Anonymity is an editorial decision. Reporters may request it; the editor grants it; the reason is stated in the copy in plain language.
  4. 04No gifts, no travel, no preview access. Review units, conference passes, and sponsored trips are declined in writing and the declinations are logged.

Corrections are emailed to every member who received the original piece, and the corrections log for the current issue appears at the foot of every feature.
